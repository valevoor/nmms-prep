### `sci-8-01-investigative-world` — Chapter 1: Exploring the Investigative World of Science
- **Book Metadata:** Class 8 Science Part 1 | Chapter 1 | Book pp. 1–7 (PDF pp. 19–25) | **Sub-Discipline:** Scientific Inquiry
- **Canonical Topic Aliases:** Scientific Method, Experimental Variables, Controlled Investigation, Observation and Hypothesis
- **Core Concepts & Subtopics:**
  - **1.1 Scientific Wonder and Investigation:** Moving from simple wonder ("Why does one side of a *puri* puff up thinner than the other?") to systematic scientific inquiry — asking focused questions, designing simple experiments, observing, and explaining.
  - **1.2 Designing Controlled Experiments (The *Puri* Puffing Investigation):**
    - **Parameters / Independent Variables (Things We Can Change):** Thickness and size of rolled dough, type of flour (*atta* vs. *maida*), temperature of hot oil, way of dropping the dough into oil (vertical vs. slanted/sliding), moisture content in dough, resting time of dough.
    - **Observed & Measured Outcomes (Dependent Variables):** Whether the *puri* puffs up (yes/no), time taken to puff (seconds), whether the top layer is thin, oil splatter/smoke.
    - **Fair Testing Principle:** Changing only **one variable at a time** while keeping all other conditions identical, and recording observations systematically.
  - **1.3 Overview of Class 8 Science Themes:** Progression from microscopic life (cells, microbes, health) to everyday forces/energy (electricity, force, pressure, cyclones), to the submicroscopic constitution of matter (particles, elements, compounds, solutions, density), and finally to light, sky cycles, ecosystems, and Earth's habitability.
- **Key Terms & Clues:**
  - *Controlled experiment*, *variables*, *hypothesis*, *systematic observation*, *puri puffing experiment*, *fair test*, *root and kite motifs* (grounded observation vs. soaring curiosity).
- **Mapping & Disambiguation Rules:**
  - **Map here** ONLY when a question explicitly tests the **scientific method**, **identifying independent vs. dependent vs. controlled variables** in an everyday experiment, or the textbook's signature *puri* puffing investigation.
  - **Do NOT map here** if the question asks about a specific domain concept (e.g., yeast fermentation in dough $\to$ map to `sci-8-02-invisible-living-world`; heat transfer $\to$ Class 7 Science).

### `sci-8-02-invisible-living-world` — Chapter 2: The Invisible Living World: Beyond Our Naked Eye
- **Book Metadata:** Class 8 Science Part 1 | Chapter 2 | Book pp. 8–27 (PDF pp. 26–45) | **Sub-Discipline:** Biology (Cell Biology & Microbiology)
- **Canonical Topic Aliases:** Cell Structure and Functions, Microorganisms: Friend and Foe, Plant Cell vs. Animal Cell, Useful Microbes, Nitrogen Fixation, Fermentation
- **Core Concepts & Subtopics:**
  - **2.1 What Is a Cell? (Discovery & Structure):**
    - **Discovery:** **Robert Hooke** (1665) observed thin slices of cork under a microscope, saw honeycomb-like compartments, and coined the term *cell* (published in *Micrographia*). **Antonie van Leeuwenhoek** (1660s) built improved single-lens microscopes to observe living pond-water microbes and blood cells; known as the **Father of Microbiology**.
    - **Microscopic Slides:** Preparing temporary mounts of **onion peel** (stained with **safranin** + **glycerin** to prevent drying and improve visibility) and **human inner cheek cells** (stained with **methylene blue** + glycerin).
    - **Parts of a Cell:**
      - **Cell Membrane (Plasma Membrane):** Porous outer boundary in animal cells (and inner to cell wall in plants); encloses cytoplasm and nucleus; regulates entry and exit of materials for life processes.
      - **Cytoplasm:** Jelly-like matrix filling the cell between the cell membrane and nucleus; contains dissolved nutrients, water, salts, and other organelles where most life processes occur.
      - **Nucleus:** Spherical central structure bounded by a **nuclear membrane**; regulates cell activities and growth; contains genetic material.
      - **Cell Wall:** Rigid outer layer present in **plant cells, fungi, and bacteria** (absent in animal cells); provides structural strength, compactness, and rigidity.
      - **Plastids / Chloroplasts:** Tiny rod-shaped structures in plant cells; **chloroplasts** contain green **chlorophyll** for photosynthesis; other plastids store substances or impart colour to fruits/flowers.
      - **Vacuoles:** Large central space in plant cells (stores dissolved substances, maintains turgidity/shape); absent or very small in animal cells.
    - **2.1.1 Variation in Shape and Structure of Cells:**
      - **Muscle cells:** Spindle-shaped (tapered at ends) to contract and relax for movement (including peristalsis in the food pipe).
      - **Nerve cells (Neurons):** Very long and branched to carry messages rapidly across the body.
      - **Cheek/Epithelial cells:** Thin and flat to form protective linings.
      - **Plant cells:** Rectangular, elongated, or tube-like to conduct water.
      - **Ostrich egg yolk:** Largest single cell ($130\text{ mm}$ to $170\text{ mm}$ diameter).
  - **2.2 Levels of Organisation in the Living World:**
    - Hierarchy: $\text{Cell} \to \text{Tissue} \to \text{Organ} \to \text{Organ System} \to \text{Organism}$.
  - **2.3 What Are Microorganisms?**
    - **Unicellular vs. Multicellular Microbes:**
      - **Unicellular:** Bacteria, Protozoa (*Amoeba*, *Paramecium*), some Fungi (**Yeast**), some Algae (*Chlorella*, *Diatoms*).
      - **Multicellular:** Moulds (*Rhizopus* / bread mould, *Aspergillus*), some Algae (*Spirogyra*).
    - **Bacterial Cell Structure (Prokaryote):** Has a cell membrane and cell wall, **lacks a well-defined nucleus and nuclear membrane**, and instead has a **nucleoid** containing genetic material.
    - **Viruses:** Microscopic, acellular infectious agents; reproduce **only inside host cells** of plants, animals, or bacteria.
  - **2.4 How Are We Connected to Microbes? (Beneficial Roles):**
    - **2.4.1 Cleaning the Environment & Biogas:** Bacteria and fungi decompose dead plant waste, fruit/vegetable peels, and animal dung into nutrient-rich **manure/compost**. Anaerobic bacteria (especially methane-producing bacteria) break down plant/animal waste in oxygen-free environments to produce **biogas** (rich in **methane** $\text{CH}_4$ and carbon dioxide $\text{CO}_2$), used as fuel for cooking and generating electricity. **Dr. Ananda Mohan Chakrabarty** (1971) engineered a bacterium capable of breaking down crude oil spills (first patent on a living organism, 1980).
    - **2.4.2 Microorganisms in Food Preparation & Agriculture:**
      - **Yeast (Fungus):** Respires and ferments sugar in warm conditions, releasing **carbon dioxide ($\text{CO}_2$)** gas (which turns **lime water milky** and forms bubbles that make bread, cake, *bhatura*, *jalebi*, *idli*, and *dosa* dough soft and fluffy) along with small amounts of alcohol and acids.
      - ***Lactobacillus* (Bacterium):** Multiplies in lukewarm milk, converts sugar (**lactose**) into **lactic acid**, souring the milk and coagulating it into **curd** (*dahi*), buttermilk, and fermented batters.
      - ***Rhizobium* (Bacterium):** Lives symbiotically in the **root nodules of leguminous plants** (pea, bean, cowpea, pulses, gram); fixes atmospheric nitrogen ($\text{N}_2$) into plant-usable nitrogen compounds, naturally replenishing soil fertility without chemical fertilisers (basis for crop rotation with legumes).
    - **2.4.3 Amazing Microalgae (Tiny Helpers in Water):** Microscopic plant-like organisms in water/soil/bark (*Chlorella*, *Spirulina*, *Diatoms*) that perform photosynthesis, produce **more than half of the Earth's oxygen supply**, serve as food for aquatic animals, act as health supplements/superfoods, and help produce biofuels and clean wastewater.
  - **2.5 Why Is the Cell the Basic Unit of Life?** Every living organism (unicellular or multicellular) begins life as a single cell and carries out essential life functions at the cellular level.
- **Key Terms, Scientists & Heritage Boxes:**
  - *Robert Hooke* (*Micrographia*, 1665, cork cells), *Antonie van Leeuwenhoek* (Father of Microbiology), *Dr. Ananda Mohan Chakrabarty* (oil-eating bacteria), *Vedic texts / Atharvaveda* (*Krimi* classified into *Drishya* [visible] and *Adrishya* [invisible]), *safranin*, *methylene blue*, *glycerin*, *cell wall*, *cell membrane*, *cytoplasm*, *nucleus*, *nucleoid*, *plastids*, *chloroplast*, *vacuole*, *Amoeba*, *Paramecium*, *Spirogyra*, *Rhizopus*, *Aspergillus*, *Penicillium*, *Yeast*, *Lactobacillus*, *Rhizobium*, *Spirulina*, *Chlorella*, *Diatoms*, *biogas* ($\text{CH}_4 + \text{CO}_2$).
- **Mapping & Disambiguation Rules:**
  - **Map here** for cell structure, plant vs. animal vs. bacterial cells, staining slides, levels of cellular organisation ($\text{Cell} \to \text{Organism}$), types of microorganisms, fermentation, curd formation, *Rhizobium* nitrogen fixation, biogas production, and microalgae.
  - **Do NOT map here** if the question focuses on **human diseases caused by pathogens, modes of transmission, vaccines, or antibiotics** $\to$ map to `sci-8-03-health-diseases`.
  - **Do NOT map here** if the question asks about **mushrooms/fungi as decomposers in a forest food chain** $\to$ map to `sci-8-12-nature-in-harmony`.

### `sci-8-03-health-diseases` — Chapter 3: Health: The Ultimate Treasure
- **Book Metadata:** Class 8 Science Part 1 | Chapter 3 | Book pp. 28–45 (PDF pp. 46–63) | **Sub-Discipline:** Biology (Human Health, Diseases, & Immunity)
- **Canonical Topic Aliases:** Human Health and Diseases, Communicable and Non-Communicable Diseases, Pathogens and Vectors, Vaccination and Immunity, Antibiotics and Antibiotic Resistance
- **Core Concepts & Subtopics:**
  - **3.1 What Is Health?**
    - **WHO Definition:** Health is a state of complete **physical, mental, and social well-being**, and not merely the absence of disease or infirmity.
    - **Ayurvedic Perspective:** Health as balance of body, mind, and surroundings practiced through ***dinacharya*** (daily routine), ***ritucharya*** (seasonal routine), eating according to one's ***prakriti*** (body constitution), wholesome food, sleep, yoga (*asanas*), and mindfulness (*pranayama*).
  - **3.2 How Can We Stay Healthy?**
    - **3.2.1 Healthy Lifestyle:** Balanced diet (fruits, vegetables, whole grains, pulses; avoiding ultra-processed/fast foods), personal hygiene, regular exercise, adequate sleep, limiting screen time, avoiding tobacco, alcohol, and addictive substances.
    - **3.2.2 Clean Environment:** Safe drinking water, sanitation, preventing mosquito breeding in stagnant water, monitoring **Air Quality Index (AQI)**.
  - **3.3 How Do We Know That We Are Unwell? (Symptoms vs. Signs):**
    - **Symptoms:** What the patient *feels* internally (e.g., pain, dizziness, tiredness, nausea, headache).
    - **Signs:** What can be *seen or measured* objectively by another person or doctor (e.g., high body temperature/fever, rash, swelling, high blood pressure).
  - **3.4 Diseases: Causes and Types:**
    - **Classification:**
      1. **Communicable (Infectious) Diseases:** Caused by **pathogens** (disease-causing bacteria, viruses, fungi, protozoa, or parasitic worms/helminths); spread from an infected person or environment to a healthy person.
      2. **Non-Communicable Diseases (NCDs):** Not caused by pathogens and do **not** spread from person to person; linked to lifestyle, nutritional deficiencies, genetics, or environmental toxins. Some are **chronic diseases** (persisting $> 3$ months).
    - **3.4.1 Communicable Diseases — Pathogens, Transmission, & Prevention (Table 3.1 & 3.2):**
      - **Airborne / Droplet Transmission (Coughing, Sneezing):**
        - **Common Cold & Influenza (Flu):** Caused by **Viruses** (nasal congestion, cough, sore throat, fever).
        - **Chickenpox:** Caused by **Virus** (fever, itchy skin rash/blisters; prevented by Varicella vaccine).
        - **Measles:** Caused by **Virus** (fever, sore throat, red skin rash; prevented by MMR vaccine).
        - **Tuberculosis (TB):** Caused by **Bacteria** (*Mycobacterium*; affects **lungs** — persistent cough, fever, fatigue, weight loss, night sweats; prevented by BCG vaccine & hygiene).
        - **COVID-19:** Caused by **Virus** (respiratory tract).
      - **Contaminated Water and Food:**
        - **Hepatitis A:** Caused by **Virus** (affects **liver** — jaundice/yellow skin & eyes, fatigue, loss of appetite; prevented by boiled water & vaccination).
        - **Cholera:** Caused by **Bacteria** (*Vibrio cholerae*; affects **intestine** — severe watery diarrhoea and dehydration; treated with **ORS**).
        - **Typhoid:** Caused by **Bacteria** (*Salmonella typhi*; affects **intestine** — sustained high fever, abdominal pain, headache, diarrhoea/constipation).
        - **Ascariasis (Roundworms):** Caused by **Worms / Helminths** (live as parasites in the **small intestine**, feeding on nutrients; spread via soil/food/water contaminated with faecal eggs; causes anaemia, stunted growth, diarrhoea).
        - **Polio:** Caused by **Virus** (spread via contaminated water/food).
      - **Insect Vectors (Mosquitoes, Flies):**
        - **Malaria:** Caused by **Protozoa** (*Plasmodium*), transmitted by the bite of an infected **female *Anopheles* mosquito** ( affects skin/blood — high fever, profuse sweating, periodic chills).
        - **Dengue Fever (Breakbone Fever):** Caused by **Virus**, transmitted by *Aedes* mosquitoes (fever, headache, severe muscle and joint pain, nausea).
        - **Chikungunya:** Caused by **Virus**, transmitted by mosquitoes.
      - **Direct / Indirect Contact:** Ringworm (Fungus), Conjunctivitis.
    - **3.4.2 Non-Communicable Diseases (NCDs):**
      - **Nutritional Deficiency Diseases:** **Scurvy** (Vitamin C), **Anaemia** (Iron / Vitamin B12), **Goitre** (Iodine), **Rickets** (Vitamin D / Calcium).
      - **Lifestyle & Chronic Diseases:** **Diabetes** (hormonal imbalance, obesity, unhealthy diet, lack of exercise — frequent urination, excessive thirst, slow healing, weight loss; India has the highest number of diabetes patients globally), **Hypertension (High Blood Pressure)**, **Cardiovascular / Heart Diseases**, **Asthma**, **Cancer**.
  - **3.5 Prevention, Immunity, Vaccines, and Treatment:**
    - **Immunity:** The natural ability of the body's **immune system** to fight diseases.
    - **Vaccines & Acquired Immunity:** Vaccines train the immune system using dead, weakened, or harmless parts (antigens) of a pathogen to develop **acquired immunity** without causing the disease.
      - **Edward Jenner (late 1700s):** Discovered the world's first vaccine against **smallpox** after observing that milkmaids infected with milder **cowpox** did not contract smallpox.
      - **Indian *Variolation* (*Teekedaars*):** Centuries before Jenner, Indian healers practiced *variolation* by scratching skin with material from a smallpox blister to induce mild infection and lifelong immunity.
      - **Dr. Maharaj Kishan Bhan:** Chief architect of India's indigenous **Rotavirus vaccine** (preventing fatal rotavirus diarrhoea in children).
    - **3.5.1 Treatment — Antibiotics & Antibiotic Resistance:**
      - **Antibiotics:** Medicines that kill or inhibit the growth of **bacteria only**; they are completely **ineffective against viral or protozoan infections** (like common cold, flu, dengue, or malaria).
      - **Alexander Fleming (1928):** Discovered **Penicillin** (the first antibiotic) by chance when he observed that the mould ***Penicillium*** inhibited the growth of harmful bacteria on a discarded Petri dish.
      - **Antibiotic Resistance:** Indiscriminate, unnecessary, or incomplete use of antibiotics (in humans, livestock, and poultry) allows surviving resistant bacteria to multiply and pass resistance traits to other bacteria, making common infections untreatable.
      - **ORS (Oral Rehydration Solution):** Replaces lost water and electrolytes during diarrhoea/cholera.
- **Key Terms & Scientists:**
  - *Pathogens*, *vectors*, *communicable vs. non-communicable*, *chronic disease*, *symptoms vs. signs*, *immunity*, *acquired immunity*, *vaccine*, *variolation*, *Edward Jenner* (smallpox/cowpox), *Alexander Fleming* (penicillin from *Penicillium*), *Dr. Maharaj Kishan Bhan* (Rotavirus vaccine), *Plasmodium*, *female Anopheles mosquito*, *antibiotics*, *antibiotic resistance*, *ORS*, *dinacharya*, *ritucharya*, *prakriti*.
- **Mapping & Disambiguation Rules:**
  - **Map here** for all questions on human diseases (cholera, typhoid, TB, malaria, dengue, hepatitis A, measles, chickenpox, ascariasis, diabetes), modes of disease transmission, vectors, vaccines, antibiotics, and lifestyle/deficiency diseases.
  - **Exception:** If a question specifically tests *which deficiency causes night blindness / beriberi / scurvy / rickets* alongside food nutrient tests (iodine test for starch, copper sulphate + caustic soda test for protein), check if it aligns more closely with Class 7 Nutrition or `sci-8-03-health-diseases` (both cover deficiency diseases; `sci-8-03` explicitly lists scurvy, anaemia, goitre, rickets under NCDs).

### `sci-8-04-electricity-effects` — Chapter 4: Electricity: Magnetic and Heating Effects
- **Book Metadata:** Class 8 Science Part 1 | Chapter 4 | Book pp. 46–61 (PDF pp. 64–79) | **Sub-Discipline:** Physics (Electricity & Magnetism)
- **Canonical Topic Aliases:** Chemical Effects and Magnetic/Heating Effects of Electric Current, Electromagnets, Heating Element, Voltaic Cell, Dry Cell, Rechargeable Batteries
- **Core Concepts & Subtopics:**
  - **4.1 Magnetic Effect of Electric Current:**
    - **Oersted's Discovery (1820):** **Hans Christian Oersted** observed that a magnetic compass needle placed near a wire deflects whenever an electric current flows through the wire, proving that an electric current produces a **magnetic field** around the conductor. When the current stops, the magnetic field disappears and the compass needle returns to its original north–south direction.
    - **Electromagnets:**
      - A current-carrying cylindrical coil of insulated wire acts like a magnet (with North and South poles).
      - Inserting a **soft iron core (iron nail)** inside the coil makes the electromagnet **much stronger** (attracts many more paper clips).
      - **Factors Affecting Strength of an Electromagnet:**
        1. **Magnitude of electric current** (more cells/higher current $\to$ stronger magnetic field).
        2. **Number of turns of the coil** (more turns $\to$ stronger magnetic field).
        3. **Presence of an iron core.**
      - **Polarity of an Electromagnet:** Reversing the direction of electric current (swapping battery terminals) reverses the **North and South poles** of the electromagnet (tested via compass repulsion/attraction).
      - **Applications:** Lifting and sorting heavy iron/steel scrap in factories and scrap yards (switched ON to lift, OFF to release), electric bells, motors, loudspeakers, magnetic separation.
      - **Earth's Magnetic Field:** Generated by the movement of **liquid iron in the Earth's outer core** creating electric currents; forms a protective shield in space against harmful solar/cosmic particles and guides migratory birds, fish, and sea turtles.
  - **4.2 Heating Effect of Electric Current:**
    - **Principle:** When an electric current passes through a conductor, it encounters **resistance (opposition to current flow)**, converting **electrical energy into heat energy**.
    - **Heating Element (Nichrome Wire):** An alloy wire (**nichrome**) heats up significantly when current flows through it.
    - **Factors Affecting Heat Produced in a Wire:**
      1. **Material of the wire** (resistance of nichrome $>$ copper).
      2. **Thickness and length of the wire.**
      3. **Magnitude of electric current** (2 cells warm up the wire much more than 1 cell).
      4. **Duration (time) for which current flows.**
    - **Applications:** Electric room heater, electric stove, electric iron, immersion water rod, geyser, electric kettle, hair dryer, incandescent filament bulb (filament glows white-hot), and **industrial electric arc furnaces** (melting scrap steel for recycling).
    - **Hazards & Safety:** Overheating in household wiring can melt plastic insulation on plugs/sockets, cause short circuits, energy loss, and electrical fires; prevented using properly rated wires, plugs, and **safety devices (fuses/MCBs)** placed in household circuits.
  - **4.3 How Does a Battery Generate Electricity? (Electrochemical Cells):**
    - **4.3.1 Voltaic (Galvanic) Cell:**
      - Named after **Alessandro Volta** and **Luigi Galvani** (late 1700s — Galvani observed a dead frog's leg kick when touched by two different metals, copper and iron; Volta proved the electricity came from the two dissimilar metals and liquid, not the frog).
      - **Structure:** Two plates/rods of **different metals** called **electrodes** (e.g., copper/iron and zinc) partially immersed in a weakly acidic or salt liquid called an **electrolyte** (e.g., dilute acid, salt solution, or lemon juice in a **Lemon Cell**). Chemical reactions between the electrodes and electrolyte generate electric current; once chemicals are used up, the cell becomes "dead".
      - **Metal Activity Pairs for Voltaic Cells:** Zinc, aluminium, iron, lead, copper, silver (e.g., Zinc–Copper or Zinc–Iron pair with LED indicator).
    - **4.3.2 Dry Cell:**
      - Widely used single-use cell because its electrolyte is a **thick moist paste** instead of a spillable liquid.
      - **Structure:** Outer **zinc container** acts as the **negative terminal (anode)**; a central **carbon rod** (surrounded by moist electrolyte paste and capped with a metal cap) acts as the **positive terminal (cathode)**.
    - **4.3.3 Rechargeable Batteries:**
      - Can be recharged and reused multiple times by passing an external electric current to reverse the internal chemical reactions.
      - **Types & Uses:** Small batteries in watches/phones, medium in laptops/tablets, large **lead-acid** batteries in inverters and automobiles, and **lithium-ion (Li-ion)** batteries (depend on special metals like **lithium and cobalt**) in smartphones, laptops, and Electric Vehicles (EVs).
      - **Future & Environment:** Scientists are developing **solid-state batteries** (replacing liquid/paste electrolytes with solid materials for higher safety, faster charging, and longer life). Improper disposal of batteries harms the environment; they must be recycled via **e-waste** facilities.
- **Key Terms & Scientists:**
  - *Magnetic effect of electric current*, *Hans Christian Oersted*, *electromagnet*, *lifting electromagnet*, *heating effect of electric current*, *electrical resistance*, *heating element*, *nichrome wire*, *Voltaic cell*, *Galvanic cell*, *Alessandro Volta*, *Luigi Galvani*, *electrodes*, *electrolyte*, *lemon cell*, *dry cell* (zinc container $[-]$, carbon rod $[+]$), *rechargeable batteries*, *lithium-ion battery*, *solid-state battery*, *e-waste*.
- **Mapping & Disambiguation Rules:**
  - **Map here** for Oersted's compass experiment, electromagnets, heating effect of current (nichrome wire, heaters, fuses), and chemical generation of electricity (Voltaic cell, lemon cell, dry cell structure, rechargeable Li-ion batteries).
  - **Note on Class 7 Electricity:** Basic electric circuit symbols, open/closed switches, conductors vs. insulators, and LED terminal polarity are introduced in Class 7 Science; however, if the question involves **magnetic deflection, electromagnets, heating elements, or internal structure of dry/voltaic cells**, map to `sci-8-04-electricity-effects`.

### `sci-8-05-exploring-forces` — Chapter 5: Exploring Forces
- **Book Metadata:** Class 8 Science Part 1 | Chapter 5 | Book pp. 62–79 (PDF pp. 80–97) | **Sub-Discipline:** Physics (Mechanics)
- **Canonical Topic Aliases:** Force and Laws of Motion Basics, Friction, Contact and Non-Contact Forces, Mass vs. Weight, Buoyancy and Archimedes' Principle
- **Core Concepts & Subtopics:**
  - **5.1 & 5.2 What Is a Force and What Can It Do?**
    - **Definition:** A push or a pull applied on an object; SI unit is **newton ($\text{N}$)**.
    - **Effects of Force on an Object:**
      1. Make an object move from rest.
      2. Change the speed of a moving object (increase speed or slow it down / bring it to a stop).
      3. Change the direction of motion of a moving object.
      4. Change the shape or size of an object.
      *(Note: None, some, or all of these effects may occur simultaneously.)*
    - **Balanced Forces:** When two or more forces acting on an object balance each other out (net force $= 0$), the object remains at rest or shows no change in its state of motion.
  - **5.3 Force as an Interaction:**
    - At least **two objects must interact** for a force to come into play. Whenever two objects interact, **each object experiences a force from the other**, and the forces cease as soon as the interaction stops.
  - **5.4 Types of Forces:**
    - **5.4.1 Contact Forces (Require Physical Contact):**
      1. **Muscular Force:** Force resulting from the contraction and elongation of muscles (walking, lifting, chewing, animals pulling loads, and internal involuntary actions like heart muscle pumping blood and food movement/peristalsis in the alimentary canal).
      2. **Friction (Frictional Force):** Force that comes into play when an object moves or tends to move over another surface; always acts in a direction **opposite to the direction of motion (or attempted motion)**.
         - **Cause:** Microscopic **irregularities** on the two surfaces in contact lock into each other.
         - **Factors:** Depends on the **nature (roughness/smoothness) of the two surfaces** in contact (greater on rough sand/carpet than on smooth glass/tile/ice).
         - **Fluid Friction (Drag):** Liquids and gases (air and water) also exert frictional force on objects moving through them; hence airplanes, ships, high-speed trains, birds, and fish have **streamlined shapes** to minimise fluid friction.
    - **5.4.2 Non-Contact Forces (Act at a Distance Without Physical Contact):**
      1. **Magnetic Force:** Force exerted by a magnet on magnetic materials (iron, nickel, cobalt) or another magnet. **Like poles repel; unlike poles attract.**
      2. **Electrostatic Force:** Force exerted by a **charged body** on another charged or uncharged body. When two materials are rubbed together (e.g., polythene/balloon rubbed with wool or dry hair, plastic scale rubbed with polythene), static electrical charges build up on their surfaces. There are **two kinds of static charges**: **like charges repel** each other, and **unlike charges attract** each other (as well as uncharged tiny bits of paper).
      3. **Gravitational Force (Gravity):** Attractive force exerted by the **Earth** (or any celestial body) on objects towards itself. Gravitational force is **always attractive** (never repulsive).
         - **Vertical Motion:** When a ball is thrown vertically upwards, its speed decreases continuously due to downward gravity, becomes zero at the highest point, reverses direction, and increases speed while falling down (**free fall**).
  - **5.5 Weight and Its Measurement (Weight vs. Mass):**
    - **Weight:** The gravitational force with which the Earth (or a planet/moon) pulls an object towards itself. Measured in **newton ($\text{N}$)** using a **spring balance**.
    - **Mass:** The amount of matter in an object. Measured in **grams ($\text{g}$) or kilograms ($\text{kg}$)** using a **beam balance**.
    - **Key Distinction:** **Mass remains constant everywhere**, whereas **weight varies from place to place** depending on the planet's gravitational pull:
      $$\text{Weight of a } 1\text{ kg mass: Earth} = 10\text{ N} \mid \text{Moon} = 1.6\text{ N} \mid \text{Mars} = 3.8\text{ N} \mid \text{Venus} = 9\text{ N} \mid \text{Jupiter} = 25.4\text{ N}$$
  - **5.6 Floating and Sinking (Buoyancy & Archimedes' Principle):**
    - **Upthrust / Buoyant Force:** When an object is immersed in a liquid, the liquid exerts an **upward force** on it, making the object feel lighter inside water.
    - **Archimedes' Principle:** When an object is fully or partially immersed in a liquid, it experiences an upward force (**upthrust**) equal to the **weight of the liquid displaced by it**.
      - If $\text{Weight of displaced liquid} = \text{Weight of object}$ $\to$ the object **floats**.
      - If $\text{Weight of displaced liquid} < \text{Weight of object}$ $\to$ the object **sinks**.
    - **Floating Rocks (*Pumice*):** Volcanic **pumice** rock contains numerous trapped gas/air pockets formed during rapid cooling of frothy lava, making its overall density less than water so it floats.
- **Key Terms & Scientists:**
  - *Force*, *newton ($\text{N}$)*, *balanced forces*, *contact forces*, *muscular force*, *friction*, *surface irregularities*, *fluid friction / drag*, *streamlined shape*, *non-contact forces*, *magnetic force*, *electrostatic force*, *static charges*, *gravitational force*, *mass vs. weight*, *spring balance*, *upthrust / buoyant force*, *Archimedes' Principle*, *pumice*.

  - **Map here** for types of forces (muscular, friction, magnetic, electrostatic, gravitational), spring balance readings, mass vs. weight across planets, and upthrust / Archimedes' Principle.

### `sci-8-06-pressure-winds-cyclones` — Chapter 6: Pressure, Winds, Storms, and Cyclones
- **Book Metadata:** Class 8 Science Part 1 | Chapter 6 | Book pp. 80–97 (PDF pp. 98–115) | **Sub-Discipline:** Physics & Earth Science (Fluid Mechanics & Meteorology)
- **Canonical Topic Aliases:** Force and Pressure, Atmospheric Pressure, Liquid Pressure, Winds Storms and Cyclones, Thunderstorms and Lightning
- **Core Concepts & Subtopics:**
  - **6.1 Pressure (Solids and Liquids):**
    - **Definition & Formula:** Pressure is the force acting perpendicularly per unit area of a surface:
      $$\text{Pressure} = \frac{\text{Force}}{\text{Area}}$$
    - **Units:** **newton per square metre ($\text{N/m}^2$)**, also called **pascal ($\text{Pa}$)**.
    - **Everyday Applications of Area vs. Pressure:**
      - **Larger Area $\to$ Lower Pressure:** Broad straps of school bags, a porter placing a folded round cloth (*pagri*) on the head before lifting heavy loads, broad foundations of buildings, flat-bottomed vessels, broad tyres of heavy trucks.
      - **Smaller Area $\to$ Higher Pressure:** Sharp edge of a knife for cutting, pointed tip of a nail or sewing needle, pointed high heels sinking into soft ground.
    - **Pressure Exerted by Liquids:**
      - Liquids exert pressure not only on the **bottom** of the container but also on its **side walls** (in all directions).
      - Liquid pressure at a point depends on the **height (depth) of the liquid column above it** (and liquid density), **NOT on the width, shape, or total volume of the container** (communicating vessels of different widths reach the exact same water level; holes at the same depth in narrow and wide vessels spurt water to the same horizontal distance).
      - Because liquid pressure increases with depth, **overhead water tanks** are placed at a height (for high tap water pressure on lower floors) and the **bases of dams are built much broader than the top** to withstand immense horizontal water pressure near the bottom.
  - **6.2 Pressure Exerted by Air (Atmospheric Pressure):**
    - **Atmosphere:** Envelope of air surrounding the Earth; exerts **atmospheric pressure** in all directions.
    - **Practical Units of Air Pressure:** **millibar ($\text{mb}$)** and **hectopascal ($\text{hPa}$)**, where $1\text{ mb} = 1\text{ hPa} = 100\text{ Pa}$.
    - **Demonstrations:**
      - **Rubber sucker** pressed against a smooth flat surface expels inside air; outside atmospheric pressure holds it firmly against the surface.
      - **Otto von Guericke's Magdeburg Hemispheres (17th century):** Two hollow copper hemispheres of $50\text{ cm}$ diameter evacuated of air could not be pulled apart even by 16 horses (8 on each side).
      - **Magnitude of Atmospheric Pressure:** Over a $15\text{ cm} \times 15\text{ cm}$ area ($225\text{ cm}^2$, roughly the size of our head), atmospheric pressure exerts a force equivalent to a **$225\text{ kg}$ ($2250\text{ N}$)** mass!
      - **Why We Are Not Crushed:** The **internal pressure inside our bodies** (exerted by blood and fluids in cells/tissues) is equal to the external atmospheric pressure and balances it out.
  - **6.3 Formation of Wind:**
    - **Principle:** Air always moves from a region of **high air pressure** to a region of **low air pressure**. Greater pressure difference $\to$ higher wind speed.
    - **Warm Air vs. Cool Air:** When air is heated, it expands, becomes lighter (less dense), rises up, and creates a **low-pressure region**; cooler, denser air from surrounding high-pressure areas rushes in to take its place.
    - **Sea Breeze & Land Breeze:**
      - **Daytime (Sea Breeze):** Land heats up faster than sea water $\to$ warm air over land rises (low pressure over land) $\to$ cooler air from the sea (high pressure) blows towards the land.
      - **Night-time (Land Breeze):** Water cools slower than land $\to$ warmer air over the sea rises (low pressure over sea) $\to$ cooler air from the land blows towards the sea.
  - **6.4 High-Speed Winds Result in Lowering of Air Pressure:**
    - **Demonstrations:**
      1. Blowing hard **between two suspended water-filled balloons** increases wind speed between them, **reducing air pressure** in the gap; higher surrounding air pressure pushes the two balloons **towards each other**.
      2. Blowing **over a strip of paper** reduces air pressure above it, causing the strip to rise upwards.
    - **Storm Damage to Roofs:** During high-speed storms, wind blowing rapidly over weak tin/thatch roofs creates a **low-pressure zone above the roof**, while higher air pressure inside a closed house pushes the roof upwards and blows it off. **Safety tip:** Keep doors and windows slightly open during high-speed winds (unless it is a cyclone/hailstorm) to equalise indoor and outdoor pressure.
  - **6.5 Storms, Thunderstorms, and Lightning:**
    - **Thunderstorm Formation:** Strong upward winds carry warm, moist air to high altitudes where water vapour condenses into water droplets and freezes into ice particles. Strong **updrafts and downdrafts** cause water droplets and ice particles to **rub against each other**, generating **static electric charges** by friction.
    - **Charge Separation in Clouds:** Positively charged lighter ice particles move to the **upper part of the cloud**, while negatively charged heavier water droplets accumulate at the **lower part of the cloud** (inducing a positive charge on the ground/trees/tall buildings below).
    - **Lightning & Thunder:** Although air is normally an electrical insulator, when the charge build-up becomes huge, the insulating property of air breaks down and a sudden flow of charges occurs (within a cloud, between clouds, or between cloud and ground) as a bright flash of **lightning**, rapidly heating the surrounding air so it expands explosively to produce the sound of **thunder**.
    - **Local Pre-Monsoon Thunderstorms in India:**
      - ***Kalbaisakhi*** (West Bengal, Bihar, Jharkhand)
      - ***Bordoisila*** (Assam)
      - ***Mango Showers* / Coffee Showers** (Kerala, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu — help ripen mangoes and aid coffee blossoms).
    - **Lightning Safety & Lightning Conductor:** Crouch low with hands on ears and heels together (minimising ground contact; never lie flat or stand under isolated tall trees or hold metallic umbrellas); inside a car/bus is safe; tall buildings use a metallic **lightning conductor** (pointed metal rod at the top connected by a thick wire to deep underground earth) to safely channel charges into the ground.
  - **6.6 Cyclones:**
    - **Mechanism:** Over warm ocean waters, warm moist air rises and condenses (releasing heat that warms the air further), creating a **very low-pressure centre**; surrounding air rushes in and, due to the **Earth's rotation**, spins rapidly around the calm, cloudless low-pressure centre called the **eye of the cyclone**.
    - **Impacts & Mitigation:** Storm surge (wall of seawater pushed inland flooding coastal areas and salinising soil/freshwater), tracked by **IMD (India Meteorological Department)** weather satellites, cyclone shelters, and disaster management forces.
- **Key Terms & Formulas:**
  - $\text{Pressure} = \frac{\text{Force}}{\text{Area}}$, *pascal ($\text{Pa}$)*, *millibar ($\text{mb}$)*, *hectopascal ($\text{hPa}$)*, *Otto von Guericke*, *Magdeburg hemispheres*, *sea breeze*, *land breeze*, *Bernoulli-type high-speed wind pressure drop*, *updrafts & downdrafts*, *Kalbaisakhi*, *Bordoisila*, *Mango showers*, *lightning conductor*, *eye of the cyclone*, *storm surge*, *IMD*.
- **Mapping & Disambiguation Rules:**
  - **Map here** for pressure calculations ($\text{Force}/\text{Area}$), liquid pressure in containers/dams, atmospheric pressure demonstrations (sucker, Magdeburg hemispheres), high-speed winds lowering pressure (balloon experiment, blown roofs), lightning charge mechanism, lightning conductors, and cyclone physics.
  - **Cross-Subject Disambiguation with Class 8 Social Science (Geography — Atmosphere):** If a question asks about **layers of the atmosphere** (Troposphere, Stratosphere, Mesosphere, Thermosphere, Exosphere), **planetary winds** (Trade winds, Westerlies), **isobars/isotherms**, or **types of rainfall** (convectional, orographic, cyclonic), map to **Class 8 Social Science (Atmosphere)**. If it asks about the **physics of pressure, blowing between balloons, lightning charge separation, or *Kalbaisakhi*/*Bordoisila* thunderstorms**, map here (`sci-8-06-pressure-winds-cyclones`).

### `sci-8-07-particulate-matter` — Chapter 7: Particulate Nature of Matter
- **Book Metadata:** Class 8 Science Part 1 | Chapter 7 | Book pp. 98–115 (PDF pp. 116–133) | **Sub-Discipline:** Chemistry (States of Matter & Particle Theory)
- **Canonical Topic Aliases:** Matter in Our Surroundings, States of Matter (Solid, Liquid, Gas), Interparticle Forces and Spaces, Diffusion
- **Core Concepts & Subtopics:**
  - **7.1 What Is Matter Composed Of?**
    - **Constituent Particles:** Matter is not continuous; it is composed of extremely small particles (**atoms and molecules**) held together by **interparticle attractions**.
    - **Physical vs. Chemical Change in Grinding:** Grinding chalk or breaking rock only reduces grain size (**physical change**); the smallest chalk speck still contains millions of constituent particles of chalk.
    - **Evidence of Interparticle Spaces:** When sugar or salt dissolves in water, the **water level initially rises slightly and then drops back** because sugar breaks into individual constituent particles that occupy the tiny **interparticle spaces** between water particles.
    - **Acharya Kanad's *Parmanu*:** Ancient Indian philosopher **Acharya Kanad** proposed in his ***Vaisheshika Sutras*** that matter is composed of eternal, indivisible ultimate particles called ***Parmanu*** (atoms).
    - **Constituent Particles vs. Suspended Particulate Matter (SPM):** Constituent particles (atoms/molecules) cannot be seen even with an ordinary microscope, whereas **SPM** consists of much larger dust/soot specks suspended in air (each containing billions of atoms/molecules).
  - **7.2 What Decides Different States of Matter?**
    - The physical state of matter is governed by the balance between **interparticle forces of attraction** (which depend on the nature of the particles and the **interparticle distance** — decreasing rapidly as distance increases) and **thermal energy (particle motion)**.
    - **7.2.1 Solid State:**
      - Particles are **very closely packed**; **interparticle spaces are minimum**; **interparticle forces of attraction are very strong (maximum)**.
      - Particles cannot move freely from place to place; they only **oscillate/vibrate about their fixed positions** (thermal energy is low).
      - Solids have a **definite shape**, **fixed volume**, and **negligible compressibility**.
      - **Melting Point:** The minimum temperature at atmospheric pressure at which a solid melts to become a liquid (thermal vibrations overcome interparticle attractions):
        $$\text{Ice} = 0^\circ\text{C} \mid \text{Urea} = 133^\circ\text{C} \mid \text{Iron} = 1538^\circ\text{C}$$
    - **7.2.2 Liquid State:**
      - Particles are **slightly loosely packed** compared to solids; interparticle spaces are slightly larger; interparticle attractions are **weaker than in solids** but still strong enough to keep particles close together.
      - Particles can **move/slide around within a limited space**, allowing liquids to **flow (fluidity)**.
      - Liquids have a **definite volume**, **no fixed shape** (take the shape of the container), and **negligible compressibility** (water in a blocked syringe cannot be pushed in).
      - **Boiling Point vs. Evaporation:** At the **boiling point**, particle movement becomes so vigorous that particles overcome interparticle attractions and escape into the vapour/gas state throughout the bulk; **evaporation** occurs slowly at **all temperatures** from the surface only.
    - **7.2.3 Gaseous State:**
      - Particles have **maximum interparticle spaces** and **negligible interparticle attractions**.
      - Particles move **freely and rapidly in all directions**, filling the entire available volume of any container (demonstrated using **incense smoke** or **iodine vapour** spreading from one gas jar into an inverted jar on top).
      - Gases have **neither fixed shape nor fixed volume** and are **highly compressible** (air in a blocked syringe can be easily compressed by pushing the plunger, pushing gas particles closer together).
      - **Fluids:** Both **liquids and gases** flow and are collectively classified as **fluids**.
  - **7.3 & 7.4 Interparticle Spacing & Particle Motion (Diffusion):**
    - **Diffusion in Liquids and Gases:**
      - **Potassium Permanganate ($\text{KMnO}_4$) in Water:** A grain of potassium permanganate spreads pink streaks and eventually colours the entire glass uniformly due to the **constant random motion** of water and solute particles.
      - **Effect of Temperature on Particle Motion:** Diffusion is **much faster in hot water than in cold water** because heating supplies thermal energy, increasing the kinetic speed of particles.
      - **Diffusion in Air:** Lighting an incense stick (*agarbatti*) or spraying perfume in one corner of a room quickly spreads fragrance throughout the room because moving air particles collide with and disperse fragrance particles in all directions.
    - **How Soap Particles Clean Oil:** Soap molecules have two ends — an **oil-loving (hydrophobic) end** that attaches to oil/grease droplets on clothes and a **water-loving (hydrophilic) end** that interacts with water, surrounding and lifting oil droplets off the fabric.
- **Key Terms & Scientists:**
  - *Constituent particles*, *interparticle spaces*, *interparticle forces of attraction*, *Acharya Kanad*, *Parmanu*, *Vaisheshika Sutras*, *Suspended Particulate Matter (SPM)*, *melting point*, *boiling point*, *evaporation*, *compressibility*, *fluids*, *potassium permanganate ($\text{KMnO}_4$) diffusion*, *iodine vapour*, *soap micelles (oil-loving vs. water-loving ends)*.
- **Mapping & Disambiguation Rules:**
  - **Map here** for questions comparing solids, liquids, and gases in terms of **interparticle spacing, interparticle attraction, compressibility (syringe experiment), diffusion ($\text{KMnO}_4$ in hot/cold water, incense stick), melting/boiling points, and Acharya Kanad's *Parmanu***.
  - **Do NOT map here** if the question asks to classify substances as **elements, compounds, or homogeneous/heterogeneous mixtures** $\to$ map to `sci-8-08-elements-compounds-mixtures`.

### `sci-8-08-elements-compounds-mixtures` — Chapter 8: Nature of Matter: Elements, Compounds, and Mixtures
- **Book Metadata:** Class 8 Science Part 2 | Chapter 8 | Book pp. 1–18 (PDF pp. 7–24) | **Sub-Discipline:** Chemistry (Classification of Matter)
- **Canonical Topic Aliases:** Elements, Compounds and Mixtures, Pure Substances vs. Mixtures, Homogeneous and Heterogeneous Mixtures, Alloys, Electrolysis of Water, Iron vs. Iron Sulfide, Minerals
- **Core Concepts & Subtopics:**
  - **8.1 What Are Mixtures?**
    - **Definition:** Formed when two or more substances (**components**) are mixed together in any proportion **without undergoing any chemical reaction**, so each component **retains its own individual properties** and can generally be separated by physical methods.
    - **Types of Mixtures:**
      1. **Non-Uniform (Heterogeneous) Mixtures:** Components are distinguishable by the naked eye or microscope and are not uniformly distributed (e.g., *poha*, sprout salad, sand + water, oil + water, granite rock).
      2. **Uniform (Homogeneous) Mixtures:** Components are evenly distributed throughout and cannot be seen separately even under a microscope (e.g., sugar + water, salt + water, air, alloys).
    - **Alloys (*Mishraloha*):** Uniform mixtures of two or more metals (or a metal and a non-metal) blended at the particle level to enhance strength and corrosion resistance:
      - **Stainless Steel:** Iron ($\text{Fe}$) + Nickel ($\text{Ni}$) + Chromium ($\text{Cr}$) + Carbon ($\text{C}$).
      - **Brass:** Copper ($\text{Cu}$) + Zinc ($\text{Zn}$).
      - **Bronze (*Kamsya*):** Copper ($\text{Cu}$, 4 parts) + Tin ($\text{Sn}$, 1 part) — described in ancient Indian texts (*Charaka Samhita*, *Susruta Samhita*, *Rasaratnasamuccaya*) for medicinal/digestion benefits; used in traditional **Dhokra** lost-wax metal craft.
    - **8.1.1 Is Air a Mixture?**
      - Air is a **uniform (homogeneous) mixture** of gases: **Nitrogen ($\approx 78\%$)**, **Oxygen ($\approx 21\%$)**, **Argon**, **Carbon dioxide ($\text{CO}_2$)**, and **water vapour** (plus suspended dust/soot pollutants like $\text{PM}_{2.5}$, $\text{PM}_{10}$, $\text{SO}_2$, $\text{NO}_x$, $\text{CO}$ measured by **AQI**).
      - **Lime Water Test for $\text{CO}_2$ in Air:** Adding water to **quicklime (calcium oxide, $\text{CaO}$)** produces **slaked lime / lime water (calcium hydroxide, $\text{Ca(OH)}_2$)**. When exposed to air or exhaled breath, $\text{CO}_2$ reacts with calcium hydroxide to form insoluble white particles of **calcium carbonate ($\text{CaCO}_3$)**, turning the solution **milky**.
  - **8.2 & 8.3 Pure Substances: Elements and Compounds:**
    - **Scientific Definition of a Pure Substance:** Consists of **only one kind of constituent particle** (atoms or molecules) throughout and cannot be separated into other substances by any physical process. (Many everyday items labelled "pure" — like milk, ghee, fruit juice, honey — are scientifically **mixtures**!)
    - **8.3.1 Elements:**
      - Simplest pure substances that **cannot be broken down into simpler substances** by chemical means.
      - Composed of identical building blocks called **atoms**. In many non-metallic gases (like hydrogen $\text{H}_2$, oxygen $\text{O}_2$, nitrogen $\text{N}_2$, chlorine $\text{Cl}_2$), two atoms combine to form stable **molecules of an element**.
      - **118 Known Elements (Classification & Exceptions):**
        - Classified into **Metals** (gold, silver, iron, aluminium, copper) and **Non-metals** (carbon, sulfur, phosphorus, oxygen, hydrogen).
        - **Metalloids:** Elements showing intermediate properties between metals and non-metals — **Silicon ($\text{Si}$)** and **Boron ($\text{B}$)**.
        - **Physical States at Room Temperature:** Most elements are solids; **11 elements are gases** (oxygen, helium, nitrogen, hydrogen, chlorine, etc.); only **2 elements are liquids at room temperature** — **Mercury ($\text{Hg}$, metal)** and **Bromine ($\text{Br}$, non-metal)** (while **Gallium** and **Caesium** melt just above room temperature around $30^\circ\text{C}$).
    - **8.3.2 Compounds:**
      - Pure substances formed when **two or more elements combine chemically in a fixed ratio**.
      - Constituent elements **cannot be separated by physical methods**, and the resulting compound has **entirely different properties** from its constituent elements.
      - **Key Experimental Proofs of Compounds:**
        1. **Electrolysis of Water ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$):** Passing electric current from a $9\text{ V}$ battery through water containing a few drops of dilute sulfuric acid (using stainless steel screws inside inverted test tubes) decomposes liquid water into two gases: **Hydrogen ($\text{H}_2$)** (burns with a **'pop' sound** near a burning candle) and **Oxygen ($\text{O}_2$)** (makes a candle flame **glow brighter**), combined in a fixed **$2:1$ atomic ratio**.
        2. **Heating Sugar:** Heating dry sugar in a test tube decomposes it chemically into black **carbon (charcoal)** and **water droplets** (tested with blue $\to$ pink anhydrous copper sulfate); hence sugar is a **compound** of carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen, not an element.
        3. **Iron + Sulfur Mixture vs. Iron Sulfide ($\text{FeS}$) Compound (Sample A vs. Sample B):**

           | Property / Test | Sample A: Mixture of Iron ($\text{Fe}$) + Sulfur ($\text{S}$) | Sample B: Compound Iron Sulfide ($\text{FeS}$, heated) |
           | :--- | :--- | :--- |
           | **Appearance** | Yellow sulfur powder and black iron filings visible separately | Uniform black mass |
           | **Magnet Test** | Iron filings are **attracted** to a magnet and separate out | **No effect**; iron cannot be separated by a magnet |
           | **Dilute $\text{HCl}$ Test** | Iron reacts with dilute hydrochloric acid to release **colourless, odourless Hydrogen ($\text{H}_2$) gas** (burns with a **'pop' sound**); yellow sulfur remains undissolved | Reacts with dilute $\text{HCl}$ to release **Hydrogen sulfide ($\text{H}_2\text{S}$) gas** having a characteristic **rotten egg smell** |

  - **8.4 Uses of Elements, Compounds, and Mixtures:**
    - Engineering materials like alloys, plastics, medicines, fertilizers, and **Graphene Aerogel** (made from carbon; the **lightest solid material on Earth** with ultra-high porosity, used for oil-spill cleanup and energy storage).
  - **8.5 What Are Minerals?**
    - Naturally occurring solid substances found in rocks:
      - **Native Minerals:** Pure elements occurring naturally (e.g., gold, silver, copper, sulfur, carbon/diamond/graphite).
      - **Compound Minerals:** Most minerals are chemical compounds of multiple elements — **Quartz**, **Calcite**, **Mica**, **Pyroxene**, **Olivine**, and **Talc** (ground into talcum powder). Cement is made from calcite, quartz, alumina, and iron oxide.Rocks are mixtures of minerals.

  - *Homogeneous (uniform) vs. heterogeneous (non-uniform) mixtures*, *alloys* (*Mishraloha*, brass, bronze/*Kamsya*, stainless steel, *Dhokra* art), *lime water test* ($\text{CaO} + \text{H}_2\text{O} \to \text{Ca(OH)}_2$; $\text{Ca(OH)}_2 + \text{CO}_2 \to \text{CaCO}_3$), *pure substance*, *elements*, *atoms*, *molecules*, *metals*, *non-metals*, *metalloids* ($\text{Si}, \text{B}$), *liquid elements* ($\text{Hg}, \text{Br}$), *compounds*, *electrolysis of water* ($\text{H}_2$ pop sound, $\text{O}_2$ bright flame), *Iron + Sulfur vs. Iron Sulfide ($\text{FeS}$)*, *rotten egg gas ($\text{H}_2\text{S}$)*, *graphene aerogel*, *minerals* (quartz, calcite, mica, pyroxene, olivine, talc).

### `sci-8-09-solutes-solvents-density` — Chapter 9: The Amazing World of Solutes, Solvents, and Solutions
- **Book Metadata:** Class 8 Science Part 2 | Chapter 9 | Book pp. 19–36 (PDF pp. 25–42) | **Sub-Discipline:** Chemistry & Physics (Solutions, Solubility, Measurement, & Density)
- **Canonical Topic Aliases:** Solutions and Solubility, Saturated and Unsaturated Solutions, Effect of Temperature on Solubility, Mass Volume and Density, Anomalous Expansion of Water
- **Core Concepts & Subtopics:**
  - **9.1 What Are Solute, Solvent, and Solution?**
    - **Definitions:** A **solution** is a **uniform (homogeneous) mixture**.
      - When a solid dissolves in a liquid, the **solid is the solute** and the **liquid is the solvent** (e.g., salt + water in *ORS* or *chashni* / sugar syrup for *gulab jamun*, where water is still the solvent and sugar is the solute even when sugar is present in a large amount).
      - When two liquids or two gases are mixed, the substance present in a **larger amount is the solvent**, and substances in **smaller amounts are solutes** (e.g., in **air**, **Nitrogen ($\text{N}_2$) is the solvent** and oxygen, argon, $\text{CO}_2$ are solutes).
    - **9.2 How Much Solute Can a Fixed Amount of Solvent Dissolve?**
      - **Unsaturated Solution:** A solution in which **more solute can still be dissolved** at a given temperature.
      - **Saturated Solution:** A solution in which **no more solute can be dissolved** at a given temperature (undissolved solute settles at the bottom).
      - **Concentration:** The amount of solute dissolved in a fixed quantity of solution or solvent (dilute vs. concentrated solutions).
      - **Solubility:** The **maximum amount of solute** that can dissolve in a fixed quantity (typically **$100\text{ mL}$ or $100\text{ g}$**) of a solvent at a particular temperature.
      - **9.2.1 Effect of Temperature on Solubility of Solids:** For most solids (like baking soda, salt, sugar), **solubility increases with an increase in temperature** — a saturated solution at $20^\circ\text{C}$ becomes **unsaturated** when heated to $50^\circ\text{C}$ and can dissolve more solute.
    - **9.3 Solubility of Gases in Liquids:**
      - Gases like **oxygen ($\text{O}_2$)** dissolve in water in small amounts, creating a uniform mixture essential for sustaining **aquatic plants and animals (fish)**.
      - **Effect of Temperature on Gas Solubility (Inverse Relationship):** Unlike solids, the **solubility of gases in liquids DECREASES as temperature increases**. Warm/heated water holds **less dissolved oxygen**, which can stress or suffocate aquatic life.
  - **9.4 & 9.5 Why Do Objects Float or Sink in Water? — Density:**
    - **Definition & Formula:** Density is the **mass present in a unit volume** of a substance:
      $$\text{Density} = \frac{\text{Mass}}{\text{Volume}}$$
    - **Units:** SI unit is **kilogram per cubic metre ($\text{kg/m}^3$)**; laboratory/CGS units are **gram per cubic centimetre ($\text{g/cm}^3$)** or **$\text{g/mL}$** (since $1\text{ mL} = 1\text{ cm}^3$ and $1\text{ L} = 1000\text{ mL} = 1000\text{ cm}^3$). Density of water at room temperature $\approx 1\text{ g/cm}^3 = 1000\text{ kg/m}^3$; Aluminium $= 2.7\text{ g/cm}^3$.
    - **Relative Density:** The ratio of the density of a substance to the density of water at the same temperature; it is a **unitless number**:
      $$\text{Relative Density} = \frac{\text{Density of substance}}{\text{Density of water}}$$
    - **Floating and Sinking of Solids & Immiscible Liquids:** Substances with density $< 1\text{ g/cm}^3$ (e.g., cooking oil, kerosene, wood, ice, wax) **float** on water; substances with density $> 1\text{ g/cm}^3$ (e.g., iron, aluminium, stone, honey) **sink** in water. Earth's internal layers are also arranged by density (**crust** lightest at top $\to$ **upper mantle** $\to$ **lower mantle** $\to$ **outer core** $\to$ **inner core** densest at centre).
    - **9.5.1 Experimental Determination of Mass, Volume, and Density:**
      - **Measuring Mass:** Digital weighing balance (using the **Tare / Reset** button with a watch glass) or traditional **two-pan beam balance**.
      - **Measuring Volume with a Measuring Cylinder:**
        - **Least Count:** Smallest volume difference between adjacent markings (e.g., $1\text{ mL}$, $2\text{ mL}$, or $0.5\text{ mL}$).
        - **Meniscus Reading Rule:** For **water and colourless transparent liquids**, keep eye level with the **bottom of the curved surface (lower meniscus)**; for **coloured or opaque liquids**, read the **top of the meniscus (upper meniscus)**.
      - **Volume of Solids:**
        - **Regular Cuboid:** $\text{Volume} = \text{length} \times \text{width} \times \text{height}$ ($\text{cm}^3$).
        - **Irregular Insoluble Solid (Stone):** By **water displacement method** in a measuring cylinder ($\text{Volume of stone} = \text{Final volume } V_2 - \text{Initial volume } V_1$).
    - **9.5.2 Effect of Temperature on Density & Anomalous Expansion of Water:**
      - Generally, heating causes a substance to expand (volume increases while mass stays constant), so **density decreases on heating** (why hot air rises, including in hot air balloons).
      - **Anomalous Behaviour of Water at $4^\circ\text{C}$ and $0^\circ\text{C}$:** Water reaches its **maximum density at $4^\circ\text{C}$**. When cooled further from $4^\circ\text{C}$ to $0^\circ\text{C}$ and frozen into **ice**, its particles arrange into a crystal structure with open spaces between them, so **volume increases and density decreases**. Thus, **ice is lighter (less dense) than liquid water and floats on top**, forming an **insulating layer** on frozen lakes/oceans that keeps water underneath liquid at $\approx 4^\circ\text{C}$ so fish and aquatic life survive winter!
    - **9.5.3 Effect of Pressure on Density:** Increasing pressure compresses **gases** strongly (reducing volume and sharply increasing density), has a very small effect on liquids, and virtually no effect on solids.

### `sci-8-10-light-mirrors-lenses` — Chapter 10: Light: Mirrors and Lenses
- **Book Metadata:** Class 8 Science Part 2 | Chapter 10 | Book pp. 37–54 (PDF pp. 43–60) | **Sub-Discipline:** Physics (Optics)
- **Canonical Topic Aliases:** Light — Reflection and Refraction Basics, Spherical Mirrors (Concave and Convex), Lenses (Convex and Concave), Laws of Reflection
- **Core Concepts & Subtopics:**
  - **10.1 What Are Spherical Mirrors?**
    - Curved mirrors that are part of a hollow glass sphere (demonstrated by the inner and outer surfaces of a shiny stainless-steel spoon):
      1. **Concave Mirror:** Reflecting surface is **curved inwards** (like the inner hollow of a spoon).
      2. **Convex Mirror:** Reflecting surface is **curved outwards** (like the outer back of a spoon).
    - **Plane Mirror Review:** Flat reflecting surface; always forms an **erect, laterally inverted image of the exact same size** as the object, and the image distance behind the mirror changes as the object moves.
  - **10.2 Characteristics of Images Formed by Spherical Mirrors (Table 10.2) & Applications:**
    - **Concave Mirror:**
      - **Close to the mirror:** Forms an **erect and enlarged (magnified)** image.
      - **Moved farther away from the mirror:** Image becomes **inverted (upside down)** — initially enlarged, and at larger distances **diminished (smaller)**.
      - **Applications:** **Reflectors of torches, car/scooter headlights**, **dentist's inspection mirror** (magnified view of teeth), **shaving/makeup mirrors**, **reflecting telescopes**, and **solar concentrators/furnaces** (converges sunlight to generate intense heat).
    - **Convex Mirror:**
      - **At ANY object distance:** Always forms an **erect and diminished (smaller)** image (gets slightly smaller as the object moves away).
      - **Applications:** **Side-view / rear-view mirrors in vehicles** (provides a **much wider field of view** of traffic behind because images are always erect and diminished), **road-safety mirrors at sharp blind turns/intersections**, and **surveillance/security mirrors in large shops/malls**.
  - **10.3 What Are the Laws of Reflection?**
    - **Terminology:** **Incident ray**, **point of incidence**, **reflected ray**, **normal** (line drawn at $90^\circ$ to the mirror surface at the point of incidence), **angle of incidence ($\angle i$)** (between incident ray and normal), **angle of reflection ($\angle r$)** (between reflected ray and normal).
    - **First Law of Reflection:** The angle of incidence is always equal to the angle of reflection:
      $$\angle i = \angle r$$
      *(Note: When a ray strikes along the normal, $\angle i = 0^\circ$ and $\angle r = 0^\circ$, so the ray retraces its own path.)*
    - **Second Law of Reflection:** The **incident ray**, the **normal to the mirror at the point of incidence**, and the **reflected ray**, **all lie in the same plane**.
    - **Validity & Converging/Diverging Action:** The laws of reflection hold true for **all mirrors** — plane, concave, and convex!
      - A **concave mirror converges** (brings together) a parallel beam of light rays (can focus sunlight onto a bright point to burn paper/cardboard) $\to$ **Converging Mirror**.
      - A **convex mirror diverges** (spreads out) a parallel beam of light rays $\to$ **Diverging Mirror**.
  - **10.4 What Is a Lens?**
    - A transparent material (glass or plastic) bounded by two surfaces, of which at least one is curved (unlike a flat glass strip where light passes straight through without size change):
      1. **Convex Lens (Converging Lens):** **Thicker in the middle** than at the edges (just like a spherical water drop on an oiled surface that magnifies print underneath).
         - **Image Characteristics:** When held **close to an object**, forms an **erect and enlarged** image (acts as a **magnifying glass** to read small print); when the object is placed **farther away**, forms an **inverted** image (initially enlarged, then diminished at greater distances).
         - **Converging Action:** Converges parallel sunlight rays to a sharp bright spot (can burn paper).
         - **Applications:** **Magnifying glass**, **corrective eyeglasses**, **cameras**, **microscopes**, **telescopes**, and the natural **convex lens inside the human eye** (which changes shape to focus objects at different distances).
      2. **Concave Lens (Diverging Lens):** **Thinner in the middle** than at the edges.
         - **Image Characteristics:** Always forms an **erect and diminished** image for all object positions.
         - **Diverging Action:** Spreads out (diverges) parallel rays of light passing through it.
         - **Applications:** Corrective eyeglasses, spyholes/peepholes.
- **Key Terms, Formulas & Heritage Boxes:**
  - *Concave mirror (converging)*, *convex mirror (diverging)*, *incident ray*, *reflected ray*, *normal*, *angle of incidence ($\angle i$)*, *angle of reflection ($\angle r$)*, $\angle i = \angle r$, *lateral inversion*, *convex lens (converging, thicker in middle)*, *concave lens (diverging, thinner in middle)*, *magnifying glass*, *human eye lens*, *solar concentrator*, *Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj's mirror communication*, *Bhaskara II (Bhaskaracharya)* (*Siddhanta Shiromani* — measuring celestial positions using reflections in shallow water bowls).

### `sci-8-11-keeping-time-skies` — Chapter 11: Keeping Time with the Skies
- **Book Metadata:** Class 8 Science Part 2 | Chapter 11 | Book pp. 55–74 (PDF pp. 61–80) | **Sub-Discipline:** Astronomy & Space Science
- **Canonical Topic Aliases:** Stars and the Solar System, Phases of the Moon, Lunar Solar and Luni-Solar Calendars, Indian National Calendar (Saka), Artificial Satellites and ISRO Missions
- **Core Concepts & Subtopics:**
  - **11.1 How Does the Moon's Appearance Change and Why?**
    - **11.1.1 Phases of the Moon:**
      - The Moon is a **non-luminous body** (does not emit its own light; shines by reflecting sunlight).
      - **Cycle of Phases ($\approx 29.5\text{ days}$):**
        - **Full Moon (*Purnima*):** Entire illuminated half faces Earth.
        - **Waning Period (*Krishna Paksha*):** Illuminated portion decreases over ~2 weeks from **gibbous phase** $\to$ **half Moon** $\to$ **crescent phase** $\to$ **New Moon (*Amavasya*)** (invisible from Earth).
        - **Waxing Period (*Shukla Paksha*):** Illuminated portion grows over ~2 weeks from thin crescent $\to$ half Moon $\to$ gibbous $\to$ Full Moon.
    - **11.1.2 Locating the Moon in the Sky:**
      - On a **Full Moon day**, the Moon is nearly opposite the Sun (rises in the east at sunset).
      - On a **Waxing Half-Moon**, it is high overhead at sunset; on a **Waning Half-Moon**, it is overhead at sunrise.
      - Because the Moon revolves forward in its orbit while the Earth rotates once every 24 hours, the Moon rises (and appears in the same sky position) **about 50 minutes later each subsequent day** (which is why **ocean tides** also occur ~50 minutes later each day).
    - **11.1.3 Making Sense of Our Observations (Why Phases Occur):**
      - The Sun always illuminates **exactly half of the Moon**, and the half of the Moon facing Earth is always the same side, **but** as the Moon revolves around the Earth, the **fraction of the Moon's illuminated half that faces the Earth changes continuously**.
      - **Crucial Misconception Alert:** Phases of the Moon are **NOT caused by the Earth's shadow** falling on the Moon! Earth's shadow falling on the Moon causes a **Lunar Eclipse** (which occurs only occasionally on a **Full Moon day**), while the Moon's shadow falling on Earth causes a **Solar Eclipse** (only occasionally on a **New Moon day**). Eclipses do not happen every month because the **Moon's orbit is slightly tilted** with respect to the Earth's orbit around the Sun.
  - **11.2 How Did Calendars Come into Existence?**
    - Natural astronomical cycles: **Solar Day** ($24\text{ hours}$ — mean time between consecutive shortest shadows at solar noon due to Earth's rotation), **Lunar Month** ($\approx 29.5\text{ days}$ — Moon's revolution around Earth), and **Solar Year** ($\approx 365\frac{1}{4}\text{ days}$ — Earth's revolution around the Sun and cycle of seasons).
    - **11.2.1 Lunar Calendars:**
      - Consist of 12 lunar months $\approx \mathbf{354\text{ days}}$ ($11\text{ days}$ shorter than the solar year), so **seasons do not stay synchronised with the same lunar months** from year to year (e.g., Islamic Hijri calendar).
    - **11.2.2 Solar Calendars:**
      - Synchronised with the seasons ($365\text{ days}$). Because Earth takes $\approx 365\frac{1}{4}\text{ days}$, the **Gregorian calendar** adds 1 extra day to February ($29\text{ days}$) every **4 years (Leap Year)**.
      - **Century Leap Year Rule:** Since the tropical year is slightly less than $365.25\text{ days}$, century years ($1700, 1800, 1900$) are **NOT leap years** unless they are **divisible by $400$** (e.g., **$1600$ and $2000$ ARE leap years**).
      - **Tropical Year vs. Sidereal Year:**
        - **Tropical Year:** Time between successive spring equinoxes (governs Gregorian calendar & **Summer/Winter Solstices** on ~21 June / ~21 December and **Equinoxes** on ~21 March / ~23 September; ***Uttarayan*** [northward apparent movement of sunrise from Dec to June] and ***Dakshinayan*** [southward from June to Dec]).
        - **Sidereal Year:** Time taken for the Earth to return to the same position relative to distant background stars/constellations (**~20 minutes longer** than the tropical year).
    - **11.2.3 Luni-Solar Calendars (*Panchang*):**
      - Use the Moon's phases to count months ($12 \times 29.5 \approx 354\text{ days}$) and add an **intercalary month (*Adhika Maasa*) every 2–3 years** to step back in sync with the $365$-day solar cycle and seasons.
      - **12 Indian Months:** *Chaitra, Vaisakha, Jyeshtha, Ashadha, Shravana, Bhadrapada, Ashwin, Kartika, Margashirsha (Agrahayana), Pausha, Magha, Phalguna*.
      - ***Amant*** (month starts the day after New Moon) vs. ***Purnimant*** (month starts the day after Full Moon) traditions.
    - **11.2.4 The Indian National Calendar (Saka Calendar):**
      - Adopted by the Government of India in **1956** (based on the **Calendar Reform Committee** chaired by astrophysicist **Meghnad Saha**, published in the ***Rashtriya Panchang***).
      - A **solar calendar** of $365\text{ days}$ where the year begins on **22 March** (or **21 March** in a Gregorian leap year). First month is ***Chaitra*** ($30\text{ days}$ normally, $31\text{ days}$ in a leap year), followed by 5 months of $31\text{ days}$ (*Vaisakha* to *Bhadrapada*) and 6 months of $30\text{ days}$.
  - **11.3 Are Festivals Related to Astronomical Phenomena?**
    - **Luni-Solar / Lunar Festivals (Shift dates in Gregorian calendar):** **Diwali** (*Amavasya* of *Kartika*), **Holi** (*Purnima* of *Phalguna*), **Buddha Purnima** (*Purnima* of *Vaisakha*), **Dussehra** (10th day of *Ashwin*), **Ganesh Chaturthi** (*Bhadrapada*), **Eid-ul-Fitr** (sighting of crescent Moon after *Ramadan*).
    - **Solar / Sidereal Festivals (Fixed around mid-January / mid-April):** **Makar Sankranti, Pongal, Magh Bihu, Lohri** (around 14 January), and **Vaisakhi, Puthandu, Vishu, Bohag Bihu, Poila Baisakh** (around 14 April). Because they follow the **sidereal year**, their Gregorian dates slowly shift forward by ~1 day every 70 years.
  - **11.4 Why Do We Launch Artificial Satellites in Space?**
    - Man-made spacecraft orbiting Earth (commonly at $\approx 800\text{ km}$ altitude, completing 1 orbit in $\approx 100\text{ minutes}$; appear as fast-moving star-like points of light at dawn/dusk).
    - **Applications & ISRO Missions (Founded under Vikram Sarabhai):**
      - Communication, navigation, weather monitoring, disaster management, and Earth observation (**Cartosat** series / **Bhuvan** platform).
      - Scientific & Planetary Exploration: **AstroSat** (astronomy observation of stars/galaxies), **Chandrayaan-1, 2, 3** (Moon exploration), **Aditya-L1** (studying the Sun), **Mangalyaan (MOM)** (Mars exploration).
      - Student satellites: **AzaadiSat**, **InspireSat-1**, **Jugnu**.
      - **Space Debris:** Old defunct satellites and rocket fragments orbiting at high speeds that pose collision hazards and require safe de-orbiting/disposal.

    - **Components of a Habitat:**
      1. **Biotic Components:** All **living** organisms (plants, animals, microorganisms).
      2. **Abiotic Components:** All **non-living** physical/chemical factors (air, water, sunlight, soil, temperature, wind, moisture).

      $$\text{Individual (Organism)} \to \text{Population} \to \text{Community} \to \text{Ecosystem}$$
      - **Population:** Group of individuals of the **same species** living in a specific habitat at a specific time (determined in plants by counting individuals within a $1\text{ m} \times 1\text{ m}$ plot).

    - **Pond With Fish vs. Pond Without Fish (Trophic Cascade Study):**
      - In a **pond with fish**, fish eat **dragonfly larvae** $\to$ fewer adult dragonflies emerge $\to$ more **bees, butterflies, and wasps** survive around the pond $\to$ higher **pollination** and **more seeds/flowers** produced by surrounding shore plants! (Demonstrates indirect cascading effects across aquatic and terrestrial boundaries).
    - **Biotic–Abiotic & Biotic–Biotic Interactions:**
      - Plants depend on abiotic factors (sunlight, $\text{CO}_2$, water, soil nutrients) and regulate soil moisture/temperature; earthworms aerate soil while needing moist soil.
    - **Five Major Ecological Interactions Between Organisms:**
      1. **Mutualism ($+/+$):** Both organisms **benefit** from the interaction (e.g., **honeybees/butterflies and flowers** — bees get nectar, flowers get pollinated; **fruit-eating birds and trees** — seed dispersal).
      2. **Commensalism ($+/0$):** One organism **benefits** while the other is **neither benefited nor harmed** (e.g., **orchids growing on tree branches** for support and sunlight without taking nutrients from the tree; cattle egrets and grazing cattle).
      3. **Parasitism ($+/-$):** One organism (**parasite**) **benefits** while living on or inside the **host** and causing it **harm** (e.g., **ticks on dogs**, head lice, tapeworms/roundworms in intestines, *Cuscuta*).
      4. **Predation ($+/-$):** One organism (**predator**) hunts, kills, and eats another (**prey**) (e.g., lion and deer, snake and frog, frog and insect).
      5. **Competition ($-/-$):** Two organisms compete for the **same limited resources** (sunlight, water, space, prey, nesting sites), which negatively affects both if resources become scarce — crucial for **controlling population size** and maintaining ecological balance.
  - **12.5 & 12.6 Who Eats Whom? (Food Chains, Trophic Levels, Food Webs, & Decomposers):**
    - **Classification by Feeding Mode:**

    - **Types of Ecosystems:** **Aquatic** (ponds, rivers, lakes, oceans), **Terrestrial** (forests, grasslands, deserts), **Human-made** (farmlands, crop fields, fish ponds, parks), and overlapping zones (riverbanks, wetlands, estuaries).

    - Life exists only in a thin crust/surface layer of the Earth (comparing Earth to an apple, the biosphere is thinner than the apple's skin).
    - **13.2 Solar System Planets (Table 13.2):**
      - **Inner Rocky Planets (1–4):** **Mercury** (smallest, no atmosphere, $-180^\circ\text{C}$ to $430^\circ\text{C}$, average $167^\circ\text{C}$), **Venus** (**hottest planet** at $464^\circ\text{C}$ due to thick **$\text{CO}_2$ atmosphere** trapping heat via runaway greenhouse effect), **Earth** ($15^\circ\text{C}$, liquid water), **Mars** (*Red Planet* due to reddish iron-oxide soil, thin atmosphere $100\times$ thinner than Earth's, $-65^\circ\text{C}$).

    - **13.3.3 Magnetic Field of the Earth:**
      - Deflects high-energy **cosmic rays** and **solar wind particles** that would otherwise strip away the atmosphere, damage the ozone layer, and harm living organisms.
  - **13.4 What Allows Life to Be Sustained on Earth? (Four Interconnected Spheres):**
    - **Atmosphere:** Supplies oxygen ($\text{O}_2$) for respiration and carbon dioxide ($\text{CO}_2$) for photosynthesis; regulates temperature.
    - **Hydrosphere:** Covers $\approx 70\%$ of Earth's surface (oceans, rivers, lakes, groundwater, ice caps); acts as a universal solvent, medium for cellular reactions, nutrient transport, body temperature regulation, and aquatic habitat.
    - **Geosphere (Solid Earth):** Crust, mantle, and core; **soil, rocks, and minerals** supply nitrogen, iron, copper, potassium, salt, coal, oil, and diverse landforms (**geodiversity**) that create varied habitats.
    - **Biosphere:** All living organisms (plants, animals, microbes) interacting continuously with the atmosphere, hydrosphere, and geosphere.
  - **13.5 What Keeps Life from Disappearing? — Reproduction:**
    - **Definition & Role of Genetic Material (Genes):** Reproduction ensures the **continuity of every species** while passing genetic instructions (**genes** inside cells) from parents to offspring (along with small variations that enable **adaptation** to changing environments, such as camel humps or microbial extremophiles).
    - **13.5.1 Asexual Reproduction:**
      - Involves only a **single parent**; offspring are **exact genetic copies (clones)** of the parent with no mixing of genetic material.
      - **Vegetative Propagation in Plants:** Growing new plants from vegetative parts (roots, stems, leaves) — **Money plant** (stem cutting), **Potato** (eyes/buds on tuber), **Ginger** (rhizome buds), **Bamboo**, ***Bryophyllum*** (buds along leaf margins).
      - **Asexual Reproduction in Microbes & Simple Animals:**
        - **Binary Fission / Cell Division:** Single-celled **bacteria, algae, and *Amoeba*** divide into two identical cells.
        - **Budding:** ***Hydra*** develops a small outgrowth (bud) that detaches as a new individual.
        - **Regeneration:** ***Planaria*** (flatworm) regrows a complete body from a cut fragment (also partial regeneration like a lizard regrowing its tail).
    - **13.5.2 Sexual Reproduction:**
      - Involves **two parents** (male and female), each producing specialized reproductive cells called **gametes** that carry **half of the parent's genetic material**.
      - **Why Gametes Carry Only Half the Genetic Material:** So that when the male and female gametes fuse during **fertilisation**, the resulting **zygote** has a complete (not doubled!) set of genes — half from each parent, producing **genetic variation** (why siblings look slightly different from each other and from parents).
      - **Sexual Reproduction in Flowering Plants (Figure 13.7):**
        - **Male Part (Stamen):** **Anther** produces **pollen grains** (contain male gametes), supported by the **filament**.
        - **Female Part (Pistil / Carpel):** **Ovary** contains **ovules** (female gametes), connected to the sticky **stigma** via the **style** (surrounded by **petals** and **sepals**).
        - **Pollination:** Transfer of pollen from anther to stigma by wind, water, insects, or animals.
        - **Fertilisation & Post-Fertilisation Changes:** Male and female gametes fuse inside the ovule to form a **zygote** $\to$ develops into an **embryo**; the **fertilised ovule develops into a seed**, and the surrounding **ovary swells into a fruit**!
      - **Sexual Reproduction in Animals:**
        - Male gametes are **sperm**; female gametes are **eggs (ova)**.
        - **External Fertilisation & Development (in Water):** In **fish and frogs**, sperm and eggs are released into water where fertilisation and development of the zygote into tadpoles/young occur **outside the body** (eggs lack hard shells).
        - **Internal Fertilisation + Egg-Laying (Oviparous):** In **birds and reptiles**, fertilisation occurs **inside the female's body**, after which the female lays a fertilized egg with stored food (yolk) and a protective shell; parents incubate the egg until it hatches.
        - **Internal Fertilisation + Internal Development (Viviparous):** In **mammals** (humans, cows, dogs), both fertilisation and embryonic development occur **inside the mother's body**, and the mother gives birth to live young.
  - **13.6 What Are the Threats to Life on Earth? (Triple Planetary Crisis):**
    1. **Climate Change:** Burning **fossil fuels (coal, oil, natural gas)** releases excess greenhouse gases ($\text{CO}_2$, methane) that were locked underground for millions of years, while cutting down forests/wetlands/plankton reduces carbon absorption $\to$ **Global Warming** (even a $1^\circ\text{C}$ to $3^\circ\text{C}$ global rise melts ice caps, raises sea levels, submerges coastal cities, and triggers extreme weather).
    2. **Biodiversity Loss:** Habitat destruction, deforestation, and extinction disrupt food webs.
    3. **Pollution:** Air pollution (smog, respiratory distress, acid rain damaging crops/soil), water and soil pollution (factory effluents, agricultural run-off, plastic waste), and improper waste disposal.
    - **Global Environmental Agreements & Solutions:**
      - **Montreal Protocol (1987):** Successfully phased out **chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs)** to allow the **ozone layer** to recover.
      - **Earth Summit (1992):** International cooperation on climate change and biodiversity.
      - **Kyoto Protocol (2005) & Paris Agreement (2015):** Committed countries to reduce greenhouse gas emissions; the Paris Agreement set a goal to limit global warming to **below $1.5^\circ\text{C}$**.
      - Renewable energy (solar, wind), public transport, energy efficiency, recycling, and limiting plastics.
