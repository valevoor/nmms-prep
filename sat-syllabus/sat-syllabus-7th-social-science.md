### `ss-7-01-major-developments-world` — Chapter 1: Major Developments in the World
- **Book Metadata:** Class 7 Social Science Part 1 | Chapter 1 | Book pp. 1–10 (PDF pp. 15–24) | **Sub-Discipline:** History (World History & Religions)
- **Canonical Topic Aliases:** Rise of Christianity and Islam, Life and Teachings of Jesus Christ and Prophet Muhammad, Arab Civilization Contributions, The Crusades, Mongols (Genghis Khan, Qublai Khan, Timur), Ottoman Turks
- **Core Concepts & Subtopics:**
  - **1.1 What Is History?** Systematic study of the past narrating the progress of mankind through three core questions: *When* did the event happen (dates), *Where* did it happen (places), and *Who* were associated with it (persons). Religion is translated as *Dharma* in Kannada.
  - **1.2 Christianity:**
    - **Origin & Founder:** Founded over 2000 years ago by **Jesus Christ** (*Yesu* in Kannada; *Christ* means **"Saviour"**), a Jewish (Hebrew) descendant born in **Bethlehem** (in the province of Judea, near Jerusalem in Israel) to **Mother Mary**.
    - **Holy Book:** **The Bible** (contains the life and preachings of Christ).
    - **Life & Crucifixion:** At about 30 years of age, Jesus left home to serve the weak and poor, preaching compassion through **parables** (moral stories). Opposed by orthodox religious leaders who accused him of treason, he was **crucified**.
    - **Teachings:** All humans are children of God and brothers; lead a pure life without sin; *"Love your neighbour as yourself"*; *"Service to mankind is service to God"*.
    - **Spread & Roman Catholic Church:** Spread by his **12 disciples**; eventually recognized by the Roman Empire. **Rome** (Italy) became the main centre of the Christian Church with the **Pope** as its supreme head, sending missionaries worldwide.
  - **1.3 Islam & The Arab Empire:**
    - **Origin & Founder:** Founded by **Prophet Muhammad** (the last Prophet; *Prophet* = one who proclaims God's message), born at **Mecca** in Arabia to father **Abdullah** (died before Muhammad's birth) and mother **Aminah**.
    - **Early Life & Prophethood:** Helped his uncle in business; hired by and later married a rich widow named **Khadijah**. At **age 40**, while engaged in prayer and meditation in the **Cave of Hira**, he received prophethood.
    - **Holy Book & Meaning:** **Quran** (in Arabic). *Islam* means **"Peace / Submission"**; *Muslim* means **"One who surrenders to God"**.
    - **Hijrah Era:** Faced opposition in Mecca for criticizing idol worship at the **Kaaba**; his flight from **Mecca to Madina** marks the commencement of the **Hijrah era**. Later welcomed back to Mecca, where the Kaaba was freed from idols.
    - **Teachings:** Strict **monotheism** (One impersonal God, Allah; Muhammad is His messenger); honesty in trade; prohibition of collecting interest on money lent, infanticide, drinking alcohol, and gambling.
    - **Five Pillars / Religious Practices of Islam:**
      1. **Kalima:** Belief only in Allah and Muhammad as His Prophet.
      2. **Namaz:** Prayer to Allah **five times a day**.
      3. **Roza:** Fasting in the month of **Ramzan**.
      4. **Zakath:** Donating a definite sum of income to the poor.
      5. **Haj:** Pilgrimage to **Mecca** once in a lifetime.
    - **Caliphs & Expansion:** Successors of Muhammad were called **Caliphs**, who built a vast Islamic empire, conquered **Sindh** in India, and were later succeeded by the **Turks** (who entered India via Afghanistan and established the **Delhi Sultanate**).
    - **Contributions of the Arabs:**
      - Absorbed Greek, Roman, and Indian knowledge; **Baghdad** was their famous centre of learning.
      - **Literature (Persian/Arabic):** *The Arabian Nights*, **Omar Khayyam's** *Rubaiyat*, and **Firdausi's** *Shahnamah*.
      - **Science & Mathematics:** Algebra, chemistry, astronomy; learnt the use of **numbers including zero from India** and passed them to the Western world; **Unani** system of medicine.
      - **Architecture:** Palaces, libraries, hospitals, and magnificent mosques at Mecca, Madina, Baghdad, and Jerusalem.
  - **1.4 The Crusades:**
    - **Nine religious wars** fought between Christians and Muslims to gain control over **Palestine** and its capital **Jerusalem** (holy land for Jews, Christians, and Muslims).
    - **Cause:** Ottoman Turks stopped the entry of Christian pilgrims into Palestine.
    - **Course & Results:** ~40,000 Christians marched on foot in the **First Crusade** and captured Jerusalem; subsequent Crusades failed (in the tragic **Fourth Crusade**, ~50,000 Europeans participated, many died or were enslaved). Although Christians failed to retain the holy land, the Crusades expanded **trade and cultural contacts between Europe and Asia**.
  - **1.5 The Mongols:**
    - Nomadic cattle-rearing tribe of the vast grasslands of **Mongolia** (Central Asia).
    - **Genghis Khan:** United the Mongols and built a vast Asian empire (noted for fierce military campaigns).
    - **Qublai Khan:** Grandson of Genghis Khan; became **Emperor of China**; benevolent administrator who undertook public works, fed the hungry during famines, patronized scholars/art, and **converted to Buddhism**.
    - **Timur:** Successor after the decline of Qublai Khan's empire; conquered Persia, Iraq, Syria, Afghanistan, parts of Russia, and invaded India. **Babur** (founder of the Mughal Empire in India) belonged to Timur's lineage.
  - **1.6 The Ottoman Turks:**
    - Nomadic people originating in Central Asia's **Turkistan** (modern Turkmenistan) who adopted Islam, occupied vast territories after Qublai Khan's decline, and built the military state called **Ottoman**.
- **Key Terms, Personalities, & Places:**
  - *Jesus Christ*, *Bethlehem*, *Judea*, *Mary*, *Bible*, *Parables*, *Crucifixion*, *12 Disciples*, *Pope*, *Rome*, *Prophet Muhammad*, *Mecca*, *Abdullah*, *Aminah*, *Khadijah*, *Cave of Hira*, *Quran*, *Hijrah* (Mecca to Madina), *Kaaba*, *Kalima, Namaz, Roza, Zakath, Haj*, *Caliphs*, *Baghdad*, *Arabian Nights*, *Omar Khayyam* (*Rubaiyat*), *Firdausi* (*Shahnamah*), *Unani medicine*, *Crusades* (9 wars, Jerusalem/Palestine), *Genghis Khan*, *Qublai Khan*, *Timur*, *Turkistan / Ottoman Turks*.
- **Mapping & Disambiguation Rules:**
  - **Map here** for questions on the life/teachings of Jesus Christ and Prophet Muhammad, the Five Pillars of Islam, Arab contributions (Unani, *Rubaiyat*, *Shahnamah*, Baghdad, zero transmission), the Crusades, Genghis Khan, Qublai Khan, Timur, and Ottoman Turks.
  - **Do NOT map here** if the question asks about **Gautama Buddha, Mahavira, Shankaracharya, Ramanujacharya, Madhvacharya, or Basavanna** $\to$ map to **Class 8 Social Science**.

### `ss-7-02-medieval-europe` — Chapter 2: Medieval Europe
- **Book Metadata:** Class 7 Social Science Part 1 | Chapter 2 | Book pp. 11–34 (PDF pp. 25–48) | **Sub-Discipline:** History (European History)
- **Canonical Topic Aliases:** Beginning of the Modern Age, Renaissance in Europe, Religious Reformation and Counter-Reformation, Geographical Explorations and Sea Voyages
- **Core Concepts & Subtopics:**
  - **2.1 Beginning of the Modern Age — The Renaissance (1400–1600 C.E.):**
    - **Periodization of European History:** Ancient Age (till ~500 C.E.), Middle Age (~500–1500 C.E.), Modern Age (from ~1500 C.E. onwards). Three milestones marking the transition to the Modern Age: **Renaissance**, **Reformation**, and **Geographical Explorations**.
    - **Meaning & Birthplace:** *Renaissance* means **"Re-birth"** or **"Revival"**; began in **Italy** around 1400–1600 C.E.
    - **Core Features:**
      1. **Humanism:** Belief that man plays the central role in the world, is not a sinner, and is God's greatest creation (scholars of Ancient Greek literature were called *Humanists*).
      2. **Rationalism:** Examining every idea logically without prejudice; rejecting medieval obsessions with Heaven/Hell in favour of present worldly life.
      3. **Growth of Vernacular / Local Languages:** Replacing classical Greek and Latin with spoken national/local languages.
      4. **Human-Centric Art & Architecture:** Rejecting the ornate medieval **Gothic style** (pointed arches) in favour of **circular arches**.
    - **Causes of the Renaissance:**
      - Ideological freedom, decline of feudalism, spread of education, geographical discoveries.
      - **Fall of Constantinople (1453 C.E.):** Turkish Sultan **Mohammed II** attacked and seized **Constantinople** (earlier *Byzantium*, modern *Istanbul*, capital of the Eastern Roman Empire and centre of knowledge for 11 centuries) from **Emperor Constantine**. Fleeing scholars carried valuable Greek and Latin texts to **Italy**.
      - **Invention of the Printing Press:** Around **1450 C.E.**, German inventor **John Gutenberg** used movable metal type in his press at **Mainz** (printing capacity jumped from 2 books/year to **24,000 copies/year**). In **1477 C.E.**, **William Caxton** established an improved printing press in **England**.
    - **Renaissance Literature:**
      - **Petrarch:** First prominent Renaissance author; famous for lyrics and sonnets admiring Greek/Roman antiquity.
      - **Dante (Italian poet):** Wrote the epic ***The Divine Comedy***.
      - **Boccaccio (Italian story-teller):** Wrote ***Decameron***.
      - **John Calvin:** Religious reformer who gave a new style to French prose literature.
      - **Cervantes (Spanish writer):** Satirized medieval knights in ***Don Quixote***.
      - **William Shakespeare (English dramatist):**Compared to Kalidasa; wrote ***Julius Caesar*, *Romeo and Juliet*, *King Lear*, *Macbeth***.
    - **Renaissance Architecture, Sculpture, & Painting:**
      - **Architecture:** **St. Peter's Church (Rome)** and **St. Paul's Church (London)**.
      - **Sculpture:** **Donatello** (***Image of David***); **Michael Angelo** (***Image of Moses***).
      - **Painting & Polymaths:** **Leonardo da Vinci** — painter, sculptor, scientist, engineer, architect, mathematician (prepared models of flying machine, submarine, parachute, tanker; could write with both hands simultaneously); famous paintings: ***Virgin on the Rocks*, *The Last Supper*, *Mona Lisa***. **Michael Angelo** was another great 16th-century Italian artist.
    - **Renaissance Science:**
      - **Copernicus (Poland):** Discovered **heliocentrism** (Earth and planets revolve around the Sun), contradicting the Church-backed **Ptolemaic** geocentric view.
      - **Johannes Kepler (German mathematician):** Mathematically proved Copernicus's findings.
      - **Galileo Galilei (Italian astronomer):** Invented the **telescope**, supported Copernicus, summoned before the **Court of the Church (Inquisition)**, and demonstrated at the **Tower of Pisa** that cannon balls of different weights fall from the same height to the ground at the same time.
      - **Sir Isaac Newton (England):** Proved **gravitational force**.
      - **William Harvey (1478–1557):** Discovered the **circulation of blood** in the human body.
      - **Vesalius (Belgian scientist):** Published a book on human anatomy/blood circulation and was sentenced to death for questioning Church beliefs.
  - **2.2 Religious Reformation (16th Century):**
    - **Causes:** Luxury and corruption of the Pope and Catholic clergy, sale of church offices, political interference by Popes clashing with kings, and the **sale of "Letters of Forgiveness" (Indulgences)** by **Pope Leo X** to raise money to reconstruct **St. Peter's Church in Rome**.
    - **Early Precursors:**
      - **John Wycliffe (1324–1384):** Oxford University Professor; called the **"Morning Star of Religious Reformation"**.
      - **John Hus / Jan Hus (1367–1415):** Condemned Church immorality; declared *"I cannot accept any ideas against my conscience"* before the Church Court and was **burnt alive**.
    - **Martin Luther & Protestantism:**
      - Priest at St. Augustine Church who led the Religious Reformation in **Germany**.
      - Nailed **95 Theses (statements)** against the sale of indulgences on the main door of **Wittenberg Church**.
      - Translated the **Bible into German** and introduced German in place of Latin in church services. His followers came to be called **Protestants**, dividing Western Christianity into **Catholics** and **Protestants** and fostering political **Liberalism**.
    - **Counter-Reformation:**
      - Internal reform movement within the Roman Catholic Church to regain public trust.
      - Led by **St. Ignatius Loyola** (a Spanish Military General), who founded the **Society of Jesus** in **1541 C.E.** Its members, called **Jesuits**, spread Catholicism while extending educational and health facilities.
  - **2.3 Geographical Explorations (15th–16th Centuries):**
    - **Causes:** Fall of Constantinople to the Turks in **1453 C.E.** blocking overland trade routes; **Marco Polo's** accounts of the wealth of India and China; European demand for Asian spices and goods (saffron, teak, cotton cloth, tamarind, black pepper, cloves, cinnamon, sandal, musk, cardamom, diamond, nutmeg); scientific inventions (**compass, astrolabe, caravel ships**); and Christian missionary zeal.
    - **Major Explorers & Voyages:**
      - **Prince Henry the Navigator (Portugal):** Founded the **School for Navigation** to explore the west coast of Africa and find a sea route to India/China.
      - **Bartholomeo Diaz (1488 C.E.):** Portuguese sailor who reached the southern tip of Africa; named it **Cape of Storm**, which **King John II** renamed **Cape of Good Hope**.
      - **Christopher Columbus (1492 C.E.):** Backed by Spanish monarchs **King Ferdinand and Queen Isabella**, sailed west from **Palos Harbour** (August 1492) with 88 sailors on 3 ships across the Atlantic Ocean; sighted land on **12 October 1492** at the **Bahamas / West Indies**, mistaking it for India and calling the natives **Red Indians**.
      - **Vasco da Gama (1498 C.E.):** Rounded the Cape of Good Hope, touched **Melindi Island** (east coast of Africa), crossed the Arabian Sea, and landed at **Kappad** (10 km from **Calicut**, ruled by the **Zamorin** kings) in **1498 C.E.**
      - **Captain Pedro Cabral:** Portuguese fleet bound for India was blown west by heavy winds and accidentally discovered **Brazil** (South America).
      - **Amerigo Vespucci (Italy):** Reached South America and declared it was not India but the **"New World"**; German geographer **Martin Waldseemuller** named the continent **America** after him.
      - **Ferdinand Magellan (1519–1522 C.E.):** Portuguese navigator sailing from Spain (1519) with 5 ships and 267 sailors; crossed the **Magellan Strait** at the southern tip of South America into the calm **Pacific Ocean**, reached the **Philippines** (where he was killed in a skirmish with natives), and his surviving crew returned to Spain in **1522 C.E.** aboard the ship ***Victoria*** — the **first ship to circumnavigate the world**, proving the Earth is round.
    - **Effects:** Expansion of global trade, rise of European commercial colonialism, brutal **Trans-Atlantic Slave Trade** (enslaving natives of West Indies, Mexico, Peru, Brazil), spread of Christianity, and colonial rivalry.
- **Key Dates & Personalities:**
  - *1450 C.E.* (Gutenberg printing press at Mainz), *1453 C.E.* (Fall of Constantinople to Sultan Mohammed II), *1477 C.E.* (William Caxton press in England), *1488 C.E.* (Bartholomeo Diaz reaches Cape of Good Hope), *12 Oct 1492 C.E.* (Columbus reaches Bahamas/West Indies), *1498 C.E.* (Vasco da Gama reaches Kappad/Calicut), *1519–1522 C.E.* (Magellan's circumnavigation on ship *Victoria*), *1541 C.E.* (Ignatius Loyola founds Society of Jesus / Jesuits).
- **Mapping & Disambiguation Rules:**
  - **Map here** for European Renaissance (literature, art, science), Reformation (Wycliffe, Hus, Martin Luther, 95 Theses, Ignatius Loyola, Jesuits), and global Geographical Explorations (Henry the Navigator, Diaz, Columbus, Cabral, Vespucci, Magellan).
  - **Do NOT map here** if the question specifically asks about **European trading companies in India (Portuguese Viceroys Almeida/Albuquerque, Dutch at Pulicat, English Dastaks, French Dupleix)** $\to$ map to `ss-7-03-advent-of-europeans`.

### `ss-7-03-advent-of-europeans` — Chapter 3: The Advent of Europeans to India
- **Book Metadata:** Class 7 Social Science Part 1 | Chapter 3 | Book pp. 35–41 (PDF pp. 49–55) | **Sub-Discipline:** History (Modern Indian History)
- **Canonical Topic Aliases:** Arrival of Portuguese, Dutch, English, and French in India, European Trading Companies and Settlements, Dastaks
- **Core Concepts & Subtopics:**
  - **3.1 The Portuguese (1498–1961 C.E.):**
    - **Arrival:** **Vasco da Gama** reached **Calicut** on **17 May 1498 C.E.** and obtained trade permission from the **Zamorin**, King of Calicut. **First Europeans to enter India and last to leave India (1961 C.E.)**.
    - **Viceroys & Settlements:**
      - **Francisco de Almeida:** **First Portuguese Viceroy (Governor)** in India.
      - **Albuquerque (appointed 1509 C.E.):** Captured **Goa** from the **Sultan of Vijayapur (Bijapur)** in **1510 C.E.** Goa remained the **Portuguese capital** until 1961.
      - **Colonies:** Goa, Diu, Daman, Salsette, Bassein, Chaul, Mumbai, Santhome, and Hugli (Bengal).
    - **Causes for Decline:** Strong navies of the Dutch and English, corrupt officials, religious fanaticism (forced conversions), and the decline of the **Vijayanagara Empire** (which hurt their trade).
    - **Liberation of Goa (1961 C.E.):** After non-violent protesters were treated inhumanly, the **Indian Navy** liberated Goa, Diu, and Daman in **1961 C.E.**
  - **3.2 The Dutch (1596–1792 C.E.):**
    - From the **Netherlands (Holland)**; established the **Dutch East India Company in 1602 C.E.**
    - **Capital:** **Pulicat**.
    - **Trading Centres:** Agra, Machilipatnam, Surat, Karaikal, Nagapattinam, Cochin.
    - **Decline:** Defeated by the English; shifted focus to the **Spice Islands of South-East Asia** (Indonesia, Malaya, Thailand, Philippines, Cambodia).
  - **3.3 The English (1600–1947 C.E.):**
    - **British East India Company:** Established in **1600 C.E.** with a charter from **Queen Elizabeth I**.
    - **Early Settlements:** First ships anchored at **Surat**; **Sir Thomas Roe** visited Mughal Emperor **Jahangir's** court in **1615 C.E.** and obtained permission to establish factories at **Surat**, followed by Agra, Ahmedabad, and Broach, and later **Calcutta, Madras, and Bombay**.
    - **First Capital:** **Calcutta (Kolkata)**.
    - **Dastaks:** Mughal Emperor **Farrukhsiyar** granted the British **Dastaks** (special passes/licences for **duty-free / tax-free trade**) in Bengal (Bengal, Bihar, Odisha) and Surat.
  - **3.4 The French (1664–1954 C.E.):**
    - Last Europeans to arrive for trade; established the **French East India Company in 1664 C.E.** (government-owned company).
    - Opened their **first warehouse at Surat in 1668 C.E.**
    - **Capital:** **Pondicherry**.
    - **Trading Centres:** Pondicherry, Machilipatnam, Calicut, Mahe, Karaikal, Chandranagar.
    - **Governor Dupleix & Decline:** Dupleix expanded French political influence in Hyderabad and the Carnatic, leading to the **Carnatic Wars** with the English. Declined due to lack of support from the French government, political revolution in France, and a weaker navy compared to the English.
- **Key Chronology & Matching Pairs:**
  - **Companies:** British East India Company (**1600 C.E.**) | Dutch East India Company (**1602 C.E.**) | French East India Company (**1664 C.E.**).
  - **Capitals:** Portuguese $\to$ **Goa** | Dutch $\to$ **Pulicat** | English $\to$ **Calcutta (Kolkata)** | French $\to$ **Pondicherry**.
- **Mapping & Disambiguation Rules:**
  - **Map here** for European trading companies, founding years (1600, 1602, 1664), capitals (Goa, Pulicat, Calcutta, Pondicherry), Almeida, Albuquerque (1510 Goa capture), Sir Thomas Roe (1615), Farrukhsiyar's *Dastaks*, and Goa liberation (1961).
  - **Do NOT map here** if the question focuses on the **Carnatic Wars, Battle of Plassey (1757), or Maratha Peshwas** $\to$ map to `ss-7-04-india-18th-century`.

### `ss-7-04-india-18th-century` — Chapter 4: India in the 18th Century (1707–1757 C.E.)
- **Book Metadata:** Class 7 Social Science Part 1 | Chapter 4 | Book pp. 42–49 (PDF pp. 56–63) | **Sub-Discipline:** History (Modern Indian History)
- **Canonical Topic Aliases:** Decline of Mughal Empire, Maratha Supremacy and Peshwas, Third Battle of Panipat, Carnatic Wars, Battle of Plassey
- **Core Concepts & Subtopics:**
  - **4.1 Decline of the Mughal Empire (Post-1707 C.E.):**
    - Following **Aurangzeb's death in 1707 C.E.**, weak pleasure-loving successors, internal conflicts, **Persian king Nadir Shah's invasion in 1739 C.E.** (who looted the **Peacock Throne** and **Kohinoor Diamond**), and **Ahmad Shah Abdali's** invasions crippled the Mughal Empire.
  - **4.2 The Supremacy of the Marathas (18th Century = "Era of Maratha Supremacy"):**
    - **Shivaji** founded the Maratha Empire assisted by eight ministers (**Ashta Pradhans**), among whom the **Peshwa** (Prime Minister) was pre-eminent. Seven Peshwas ruled the Deccan and parts of North India for ~100 years (**1713–1818 C.E.**).
    - **Three Important Maratha Peshwas:**
      1. **Balaji Vishwanath (1713–1720 C.E.):** Signed the **Treaty of Delhi** with the Mughal Emperor, earning the right to collect taxes from the **six subhas of the Deccan**.
      2. **Baji Rao I (1720–1740 C.E.):** Came to power at age 20; captured **Hyderabad, Malwa, Gujarat, and Bundelkhand**, marched to North India and attacked Delhi; hailed as **"Shivaji II" (Second Shivaji)**.
      3. **Balaji Baji Rao (1740–1761 C.E.):** Expanded Maratha rule into **Punjab** (north-west), **Orissa** (east), and parts of **Tamil Nadu** (south).
    - **Third Battle of Panipat (14 January 1761 C.E.):** Fought between the **Marathas** (under Balaji Baji Rao's reign) and Afghan invader **Ahmad Shah Abdali**. The Marathas were defeated, weakening their military strength and enabling British political rise.
  - **4.3 The Carnatic Wars (1746–1763 C.E.):**
    - **Region & Capital:** Europeans called the **Coromandel coast** (coastal Tamil Nadu and Andhra Pradesh) and its hinterland **Carnatic**; its capital was **Arcot** (Tamil Nadu).
    - **Cause & Leaders:** Succession dispute for the Nawabship of Arcot between **Chanda Sahib** (backed by the **French** under **Dupleix**) and **Mohammad Ali** (backed by the **British** under **Robert Clive**).
    - **Three Wars:**
      - **First Carnatic War:** French victorious; ended with the **Treaty of Aix-la-Chapelle**.
      - **Second Carnatic War:** British retained stronghold in **Arcot**; French retained stronghold in **Hyderabad**.
      - **Third Carnatic War:** **British won decisively**, sidelining the French in India.
    - **Reasons for French Defeat:** Neglected trade for politics; weaker navy; quarrels among French officials; French company was under government control whereas the British EIC was privately owned.
  - **4.4 Beginning of British Administration in Bengal & Battle of Plassey (1757 C.E.):**
    - **Background:** Bengal provincial officer **Alivardi Khan** became independent as the Mughals declined; succeeded by his young grandson **Siraj-ud-Daulah** as Nawab of Bengal.
    - **Causes of Plassey:** The British strengthened **Fort William at Calcutta** without the Nawab's permission, abused *Dastak* tax concessions, and plotted with his enemies. Siraj-ud-Daulah seized British warehouses.
    - **Conspiracy & Battle (1757 C.E.):** **Robert Clive** conspired with Siraj's general **Mir Jafar** (offering him Nawabship in exchange for **Rs. 17 crore 70 lakh**, mediated by merchant **Aminchand**). At the **Battle of Plassey (1757 C.E.)**, Mir Jafar betrayed Siraj-ud-Daulah, who was defeated and killed.
    - **Consequences:** Mir Jafar became a puppet Nawab; the British EIC received huge wealth and **zamindari rights over the 24 Parganas** (first territory grabbed by the Company in India), laying the foundation of the British Empire in India.
- **Key Dates & Clues:**
  - *1707 C.E.* (Death of Aurangzeb), *1713–1818 C.E.* (Peshwa rule; *1711–1761* Maratha supremacy), *1739 C.E.* (Nadir Shah's invasion; Peacock Throne & Kohinoor diamond), *1746–1763 C.E.* (Carnatic Wars; Treaty of Aix-la-Chapelle), *1757 C.E.* (Battle of Plassey), *14 Jan 1761 C.E.* (Third Battle of Panipat).

### `ss-7-05-growth-british-supremacy` — Chapter 5: The Growth of British Supremacy (1758–1856 C.E.)
- **Book Metadata:** Class 7 Social Science Part 1 | Chapter 5 | Book pp. 50–59 (PDF pp. 64–73) | **Sub-Discipline:** History (Modern Indian History)
- **Canonical Topic Aliases:** Battle of Buxar, Grant of Diwani Rights, Regulating Act 1773, Anglo-Mysore / Anglo-Maratha / Anglo-Sikh Wars, Ranjit Singh, Subsidiary Alliance, Doctrine of Lapse
- **Core Concepts & Subtopics:**
  - **5.1 British Misrule in Bengal & Battle of Buxar (1764 C.E.):**
    - **Robert Clive** was appointed the **first Governor of Bengal (1758 C.E.)**. Company officials engaged in *"open and shameless looting"*.
    - The British dethroned **Mir Jafar** and installed his son-in-law **Mir Qasim** (who gave zamindari rights of 3 districts). When Mir Qasim abolished all trade taxes in Bengal to stop British misuse of *Dastaks*, the British deposed him.
    - **Battle of Buxar (1764 C.E.):** **Mir Qasim** formed a triple military alliance with the **Nawab of Oudh (Awadh)** and the **Mughal Emperor (Shah Alam II)**, but the British defeated the combined alliance at **Buxar (1764 C.E.)**.
  - **5.2 Grant of Diwani Rights (1765 C.E.):**
    - **Robert Clive** returned as Governor and signed a treaty in **1765 C.E.** with Mughal Emperor **Shah Alam II**: in return for an annual payment of **Rs. 26 lakh** to the Mughal Emperor, the Company obtained **Diwani rights** (*right to collect land revenue*) over **Bengal (including Assam), Bihar, and Odisha**.
    - **Bengal Famine:** After Clive returned to England in 1767, severe maladministration and famine killed **one-third of Bengal's population**, while the Company hoarded and sold rice at maximum profit.
  - **5.3 Regulating Act (1773 C.E.) & Pitt's India Act (1784 C.E.):**
    - Passed by the British Parliament in **1773 C.E.** to regulate Company corruption; **Warren Hastings** was appointed the **First Governor-General** (with Calcutta as centre of power). **Pitt's India Act (1784 C.E.)** was enacted to rectify defects in the Regulating Act.
  - **5.4 Wars of Expansion:**
    - **Anglo-Mysore Wars (1767–1799 C.E.):** Four wars against **Hyder Ali** and **Tippu Sultan**; after 1799, Mysore was divided into four parts (British, Nizam, Marathas, and Mysore Wodeyars).
    - **Anglo-Maratha Wars (1775–1818 C.E.):** Three wars; the Peshwa surrendered and the **British abolished the post of Peshwa**.
    - **Anglo-Sikh Wars & Ranjit Singh:** Sikhs were organized into sects called **Misls**. **Ranjit Singh** became king of **Lahore (Punjab)** at age 18, united all Sikh Misls, ruled for over 4 decades, and received the **Kohinoor diamond** from the Afghan king. After Ranjit Singh's death (**1839 C.E.**), the British fought four battles against the Sikhs and annexed **Punjab in 1849 C.E.**
  - **5.5 Cunning British Policies:**
    - **Doctrine of Subsidiary Alliance (Lord Wellesley, 1799–1805 C.E.):** Indian kings were forced to retain a British army in their kingdom and pay for its maintenance in cash or cede territory, losing their sovereignty and financial stability.
    - **Doctrine of Lapse (Lord Dalhousie, 1848–1856 C.E.):** Denied adopted children the right to inherit the throne if an Indian king died without natural descendants. **Oudh, Satara, Nagpur, Jhansi**, and 11 other states were annexed; by 1856, two-thirds of India was under British rule.
- **Key Dates & Clues:**
  - *1758 C.E.* (Clive first Governor of Bengal), *1764 C.E.* (Battle of Buxar), *1765 C.E.* (Diwani Rights from Shah Alam II for Rs. 26 lakh/year), *1773 C.E.* (Regulating Act — Warren Hastings), *1775–1818 C.E.* (Anglo-Maratha Wars), *1784 C.E.* (Pitt's India Act), *1799–1805 C.E.* (Lord Wellesley — Subsidiary Alliance), *1839 C.E.* (Death of Ranjit Singh), *1848–1856 C.E.* (Lord Dalhousie — Doctrine of Lapse), *1849 C.E.* (Annexation of Punjab).

### `ss-7-06-impact-british-rule` — Chapter 6: Impact of British Rule
- **Book Metadata:** Class 7 Social Science Part 1 | Chapter 6 | Book pp. 60–70 (PDF pp. 74–84) | **Sub-Discipline:** History (Modern Indian History & Constitutional History)
- **Canonical Topic Aliases:** British Land Revenue Systems (Permanent Zamindari, Ryotwari, Mahalwari), Introduction of English Education (Macaulay, Wood's Despatch), Drain of Wealth Theory, Constitutional Acts (1773 to 1935)
- **Core Concepts & Subtopics:**
  - **6.1 Land Revenue Policies:**
    - **Auction / Bidding System:** Introduced initially by Governor-General **Warren Hastings**.
    - **Permanent Zamindari System (1793 C.E.):** Introduced by **Lord Cornwallis** in **Bengal, Bihar, and Odisha**. Fixed land tax permanently with **Zamindars** acting as government agents; led to severe peasant exploitation, forced commercial cropping, collapse of agricultural output, and bonded labour.
    - **Ryotwari System (1801 C.E.):** Introduced by **Sir Thomas Munro** in **Madras province** (and implemented in South and West India). Direct relationship between the government and the farmer (*ryot*) without intermediaries; tax fixed at **half (50%) of the produce** based on land measurement, fertility, and irrigation, mandatory even during floods or droughts.
    - **Mahalwari System (1833 C.E.):** *Mahal* means village or estate. Tax fixed for the entire *Mahal* with the local Zamindar responsible for collection; implemented in **Uttar Pradesh, parts of Madhya Pradesh, and Punjab**.
    - **Overall Impact:** Land became a saleable commodity; cash tax increased money lending and land auctions; shift from food crops to commercial crops (cotton, jute, peanut, tobacco, sugarcane) caused food shortages.
  - **6.2 English Education & Orientalist Studies:**
    - **Charter Act of 1813:** Set aside **Rs. 1 lakh per year** for Indian education (unspent till 1823).
    - **Orientalist Scholars:** **Sir William Jones** (Supreme Court Judge) established the **Asiatic Society of Bengal in 1784 C.E.**; **Sir Charles Wilkins** translated the **Bhagavadgita into English in 1785 C.E.**; **Max Muller** translated the *Rig Veda* and other works.
    - **Macaulay's Minute & Bentinck's Policy (1835 C.E.):** Resolving the Orientalist–Anglicist dispute over medium of instruction, **Lord Macaulay** (an ardent Anglicist who claimed *"the entire storehouse of knowledge of the East is no match for one row of books on one shelf of a good European library"*) drafted the note on which Governor-General **William Bentinck (1835 C.E.)** declared **English** as the medium of instruction for Western science and literature. **English became the official administrative language in 1844 C.E.**
    - **Charles Wood's Report / Despatch (1854 C.E.):** Basis of the New Education Policy; led to the establishment of **three Universities at Bombay, Calcutta, and Madras in 1857 C.E.** and provincial Education Departments.
  - **6.3 Economic Impact & Drain of Wealth:**
    - Following the Industrial Revolution in England, British trade policy reduced India to an **exporter of raw materials** (e.g., raw cotton) and **importer of British machine-made goods**, destroying Indian cottage/handloom industries.
    - **Drain of Wealth Theory:** Formulated by **Dadabhai Naoroji** to explain how the continuous flow of wealth and profits from India to England impoverished India and caused capital shortage.
    - **Adam Smith** (the *"Father of Economics"*) described the British rulers in India as **"Plunderers"**.
  - **6.4 Constitutional Developments:**
    - **Regulating Act (1773 C.E.):** Implemented under **Warren Hastings**; first milestone giving a constitution to the East India Company.
    - **Pitt's India Act (1784 C.E.):** Brought by British PM **William Pitt**; subordinated the Company as a unit of the British government.
    - **Morley–Minto Reforms (1909 C.E.):** Viceroy **Lord Minto** and Secretary of State **Lord Morley** expanded Central and Provincial Legislative Assemblies (without giving real responsibility to representatives).
    - **Montagu–Chelmsford Reforms (1919 C.E.):** Based on Secretary of State **Montagu's 1917 declaration**; expanded the Indian Council (5-year term) and appointed a **High Commissioner for India in London**.
    - **Government of India Act (1935 C.E.):** Proposed an **All-India Federation** of states and provinces, introduced **Diarchy at the Central Government**, and established **Responsible Governments at the Provincial level**.
- **Key Terms, Personalities, & Dates:**
  - *Warren Hastings* (Tax auction; Regulating Act 1773), *Lord Cornwallis* (Permanent Zamindari System, 1793), *Sir Thomas Munro* (Ryotwari System, 1801, Madras), *Mahalwari System* (1833), *Charter Act of 1813* (Rs. 1 lakh), *Sir William Jones* (Asiatic Society of Bengal, 1784), *Sir Charles Wilkins* (Bhagavadgita translation, 1785), *Max Muller*, *Lord Macaulay & William Bentinck* (1835 English Education Policy), *Charles Wood's Report* (1854; 1857 Universities at Bombay, Calcutta, Madras), *Dadabhai Naoroji* (Drain of Wealth Theory), *Adam Smith* ("Plunderers"), *Pitt's India Act* (1784), *Morley-Minto Reforms* (1909), *Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms* (1919), *Government of India Act* (1935).
- **Mapping & Disambiguation Rules:**
  - **Map here** for British land revenue systems (Permanent Settlement, Ryotwari, Mahalwari), Charter Act 1813, Asiatic Society of Bengal (1784), Macaulay/Bentinck (1835), Wood's Despatch (1854), Drain of Wealth theory, and British Constitutional Acts (1773, 1784, 1909, 1919, 1935).

  - **7.1 Wodeyars of Mysore, Hyder Ali, Tippu Sultan, & Modern Mysore:**
    - **Wodeyar Symbols & Traditions:** Capital: **Mysore**; Family Deity: **Chamundeshwari Devi** (Chamundi Hill); Royal Emblem: **Ganda-Bherunda** (two-headed mythical bird); **Navaratri (Dasara)** festival. Present Mysore Palace was completed in **1910 / 1912** (after the wooden palace burnt down), with Darbar Hall paintings by **Raja Ravi Varma**.
    - **Chikkadevaraja Wodeyar (1673–1704 C.E.):**
      - Established the **Athara Kacheri** (Central Administrative Office with **18 departments**).
      - Regulated weights and measures; established a **postal system (*Anche*)**.
      - Took **Bengaluru on lease from the Mughals in 1689 C.E.** for **3 lakh varahas**.
      - Built a dam across the **Kaveri** near Srirangapattana; accumulated vast treasury and earned the title **"Navakoti Narayana"**.
      - **Court Poets:** **Singararya** wrote ***Mitravinda Govinda*** (the **first Kannada drama**); **Sanchi Honnamma** wrote ***Hadibadeya Dharma***.
    - **Hyder Ali (1761–1782 C.E.):**
      - Rose as a military commander under **Dalavayi Nanjarajayya**, sidelined King **Krishnaraja Wodeyar II**, called himself *Karyakarta*, conquered **Keladi (1763)** and **Chitradurga (1779)**, and started **Lalbagh Garden** in Bengaluru.
      - **First Anglo-Mysore War (1767–1769 C.E.):** Hyder Ali defeated the British, ending with the **Treaty of Madras (1769)** (mutual defensive alliance).
      - **Second Anglo-Mysore War (1780–1784 C.E.):** Triggered when the British failed to aid Hyder against the Marathas in violation of the Madras Treaty. Hyder Ali died of illness in **1782 C.E.** during the war; his son **Tippu Sultan** continued it and signed the **Treaty of Mangaluru (1784 C.E.)**.
    - **Tippu Sultan (1782–1799 C.E.) — "Tiger of Mysore":**
      - **Third Anglo-Mysore War (1790–1792 C.E.):** British besieged **Srirangapattana**; Tippu signed the humiliating **Treaty of Srirangapattana (1792)** — surrendering **half of his kingdom**, paying a huge war indemnity, and handing over **two of his sons as hostages**.
      - **Fourth Anglo-Mysore War (1799 C.E.):** Tippu died fighting bravely at **Srirangapattana**.
      - **Achievements:** Popularized **sericulture (silk)** in Mysore; modernized the army with **French** help and pioneered **military rockets**; built **Bengaluru Palace**, **Dariya Daulat** (Summer Palace at Srirangapattana), **Jumma Masjid** (Srirangapattana), and **Hyder Ali's Tomb**; established mints at Srirangapattana and Bengaluru; gave grants to Hindu temples and **Sringeri Math**.
    - **Krishnaraja Wodeyar III & Commissioners' Rule (1831–1881 C.E.):**
      - In 1799, the British placed 5-year-old **Krishnaraja Wodeyar III** on the throne with **Diwan Poornaiah** administering the state. In **1831 C.E.**, alleging failure to suppress the **Bidanuru (Nagara) revolt** in Shivamogga, the British dethroned him and imposed **Commissioners' Rule (1831–1881 C.E.)**.
      - **Sir Mark Cubbon (1834–1861 C.E.):** Shifted the capital from **Mysuru to Bengaluru**; organized districts/taluks, police, judiciary; made **Kannada the official language**; laid telegraph lines and started the **first railway line in the state between Bengaluru and Jolarpet in 1859 C.E.**
      - **Louis Bentham Bowring (1862–1870 C.E.):** Divided the state into **Nandidurga and Ashtagrama** units; reformed revenue, judiciary, police, and established the **Department of Public Instruction**.
    - **Chamaraja Wodeyar X (1881–1894 C.E.):**
      - Restored to the throne by the British in **1881 C.E.** — known as **Rendition (*Punaradana*)**.
      - Served by Diwans **C. Rangacharlu** and **K. Sheshadri Iyer**; established the **People's Representative Assembly (*Praja Pratinidhi Sabha*) in 1881 C.E.**
    - **Naalvadi Krishnaraja Wodeyar (1902–1940 C.E.):**
      - Ascended at age 11 in 1895 under the regency of his mother **Kempa Nanjammanni**; direct rule **1902–1940 C.E.** Called **"Rajarshi" (*Rajyashri*)** by **Mahatma Gandhi**, who praised his Mysore as **"Ramarajya"**.
      - **Key Achievements:** Granted **371 acres of land and >Rs. 5 lakh** to **J.N. Tata** to establish the **Indian Institute of Science (IISc)** in Bengaluru; established **Nyaya Vidhayaka Sabha** (Legislative Assembly) in **1907 C.E.**; completed present **Mysore Palace (1910)**; started **Mysore Economic Conference (1911)**; appointed the **Miller Commission (1918)** for Backward Classes reservation in government service; built **Krishnaraja Sagara (KRS) Dam** across the Kaveri (selling royal family gold ornaments in Bombay to complete it); and **granted voting rights to women for the first time**.
    - **Sir M. Visvesvaraya (Diwan: 1912–1918 C.E.):**

    - **Accession to India (1947 C.E.):** Last ruler **Jayachamaraja Wodeyar** acceded to the Indian Union after the **Palace Satyagraha (*Aramane Satyagraha* / *Mysuru Chalo*)** led by **K.C. Reddy**. Responsible government formed on **24 October 1947** with **Jayachamaraja Wodeyar as first Governor** and **K.C. Reddy as first Chief Minister** of Mysore State.

### `ss-7-08-bhakthi-cult-sufi-heritage` — Chapter 8: Bhakthi Cult and Sufi Heritage
- **Book Metadata:** Class 7 Social Science Part 1 | Chapter 8 | Book pp. 115–127 (PDF pp. 129–141) | **Sub-Discipline:** History (Medieval Cultural & Religious History)
- **Canonical Topic Aliases:** Bhakti Movement in South and North India, Alwars and Dasas, Vachana and Keerthana Literature, Sufi Saints and Chishti Order
- **Core Concepts & Subtopics:**
  - **8.1 Meaning & Gist of Bhakthi Cult:**
    - *Bhakthi* is derived from the Sanskrit root ***Bhuj*** (meaning *to receive*). Condemned caste discrimination and superstitions, preached equality, universal brotherhood, and devotion (*Bhakthi*) as the path to *Moksha*, and enriched vernacular languages.
  - **8.2 Bhakthi Heritage in South India:**
    - **Aandal (Godadevi):** Only female saint among the **12 Alwars** (Vishnu devotees of Tamil Nadu); adopted daughter of **Vishnuchitha (Periyalwar)** of **Srivilliputhur**; devoted to Lord Krishna as her husband; composed ***Thiruppavai*** (basis of *Thiruppavai Vratha*).
    - **Akkamahadevi (12th Century):** Prominent **Vachana** poetess born at **Uduthadi** (Shivamogga district) to **Nirmalashetty and Sumathi**; renounced her forced marriage to **King Koushika**, joined Basaveshwara's **Anubhava Mantapa at Kalyana**, composed Vachanas under the *ankita* (pen name) ***"Channamallikarjuna"***, and attained union at the **Kadali forest of Srishaila**.
    - **Purandaradasa (1480–1564 C.E.):** Born at **Purandaragada** as **Srinivasa Nayaka** (parents: Varadappa Nayaka & Leelavathi; wife: Lakshmibai); initiated by **Guru Vyasaraya** at Krishnadevaraya's court (who praised him as *"Purandaradasa is the Ultimate as a Dasa"*). Known as **"Karnataka Sangeetha Pithamaha"** (*Father of Carnatic Music*); pen name: ***"Purandara Vittala"***.
    - **Kanakadasa (1508–1606 C.E.):** Born at **Bada** village (Bankapura taluk, Haveri district) as **Thimmappa / Kanakanayaka** (parents: Beerappa & Bachamma); disciple of **Vyasaraya**; pen name: ***"Kagineleya Adikeshava"***. Associated with **Kanakana Kindi** at Udupi Krishna Temple. **Literary Works:** ***Haribhakthisara*, *Nalacharithre*, *Ramadhanya Charithe*** (Shatpadi epic), and ***Mohana Tarangini*** (Sangatya Kavya).
    - **Shishunala Sharifa (born 1819 C.E.):** First Kannada Muslim mystic poet, born at **Shishunala** (parents: Imam Saheb & Hajjamma); disciple of **Guru Govindabhatta**; composed *Tatvapadas* (*Kalagapada, Dharmika Neethipada, Alaavipada, Savaal Jawab padas*); known as **"Kabir of Karnataka"**.
  - **8.3 Bhakthi Heritage in North India:**
    - **Kabirdas (1398/1440–1518 C.E.):** Disciple of **Ramananda**; raised by Muslim weaver couple **Neeru and Neema** at **Varanasi**; preached Hindu–Muslim unity (*"pots of the same clay"*, *"son of Rama and Allah"*); composed two-line couplets called ***Dohas***; followers are called **Kabir Panthis**.
    - **Guru Nanak (1469–1539 C.E.):** Founder of **Sikhism**; born at **Talwandi** (Punjab, now in Pakistan) to **Mehta Kalu and Tripta**; songs called ***Japji*** are compiled in the Sikh holy book ***Guru Granth Sahib***. Visited **Bidar** in Karnataka (**Nanak Jhara** sweet water spring).
    - **Tulasidas (1532–1623 C.E.):** Foremost Rama bhakta in North India; wrote ***Ramacharithamanasa*** (*Tulasiramayana*) in Hindi.
    - **Sri Chaithanya (1485–1533 C.E.):** Born at **Navadweep (Nadia)** in Bengal as **Vishwambhara** (parents: Jagannatha Mishra & Sachidevi; Guru: **Ishvarapuri**); called **Chaithanya Mahaprabhu**; inspired the **Hare Krishna** movement.
    - **Meerabai (1498–1569 C.E.):** Rajput princess of Rajasthan (daughter of **Raja Ratan Singh**, married to the Prince of Mewar); left the palace for **Brindavan**; composed hundreds of *Bhajans* devoted to **Giridhara Gopala**; known as **"Radha of Kaliyuga"**.
  - **8.4 Sufi Heritage:**
    - *Sufi* derives from ***Saaf*** (clean/clear); Muslim mystics wearing plain woollen dress who preached love, service to mankind, equality, and Hindu–Muslim harmony.
    - **Moyinuddin Chishti (Ajmer):** Founder of the **Chishti sect** in India.
    - **Nizamuddin Auliya (1238–1325 C.E., Delhi):** Lived in a thatched-roof masjid in Delhi; refused village grants from the Sultan of Delhi.
    - **Khwaja Bande Nawaz (Kalaburagi):** Chishti saint, disciple of Nasiruddin of Delhi; original name **Syed Muhammad Hussaini**; also called ***Gesudaraj*** ("long-haired"); respected by Bahmani Sultan **Firoz Shah**; wrote in **Dakhani (Urdu)**.
    - **Other Sufi Centres:** **Salim Chishti** (Fatehpur Sikri), **Sheikh Ismail** (Lahore), **Tippu Mastan Auliya** (Arcot).

  - **Map here** for Aandal, Akkamahadevi, Purandaradasa, Kanakadasa, Shishunala Sharifa, Kabirdas, Guru Nanak, Tulasidas, Chaithanya, Meerabai, and Sufi saints (Moyinuddin Chishti, Nizamuddin Auliya, Khwaja Bande Nawaz, Salim Chishti).

### `ss-7-09-legislative-executive-judicial-terms` — Chapter 9: Introduction to Legislative, Executive and Judicial Terminologies
- **Book Metadata:** Class 7 Social Science Part 1 | Chapter 9 | Book pp. 128–143 (PDF pp. 142–157) | **Sub-Discipline:** Civics (Political Science Concepts)
- **Canonical Topic Aliases:** Three Organs of Government, Unicameral vs. Bicameral Legislature, Types of Executive, Unified vs. Federal Judiciary
- **Core Concepts & Subtopics:**
  - **9.1 The Legislature:**
    - **Etymology & Epithets:** Derived from French ***Parler*** and Latin ***Parliamentum*** (meaning *to talk or discuss*). Called the **"barometer of public opinion"**, **"mirror of public opinion"**, **"mirror of national life"**, and **"law-making factory"**.
    - **Names of National Legislatures Worldwide:**
      - **India:** Parliament | **Maldives:** Majlis | **Germany:** Bundestag | **Israel:** Knesset | **France:** National Assembly | **Denmark:** Folketing.
    - **Types of Legislature:**
      1. **Unicameral Legislature (One House):** Economical, avoids repetition (e.g., **Greece, Finland, Sweden, Denmark, Yugoslavia, Portugal, New Zealand**).
      2. **Bicameral Legislature (Two Houses — Upper & Lower House):** Originated in **England** (Lower: **House of Commons**, Upper: **House of Lords**); adopted in **India** (Lower: **Lok Sabha**, Upper: **Rajya Sabha**).
  - **9.2 The Executive (*Karyanga*):**
    - **Etymology & Definitions:** Derived from medieval Latin ***Exsequi*** (*"carry out the work"*); called the **administrative organ**. Defined by **Garner**, **Willoughby**, **Liftman**, and **D.V. Varner**.
    - **Eight Types of Executive:**
      1. **Nominal Executive:** Head of state in whose name power is exercised (e.g., **President of India**, **King/Queen of Great Britain**).
      2. **Real Executive:** Exercises actual executive power (e.g., **Prime Minister and Cabinet of India**).
      3. **Parliamentary Executive:** Elected by and accountable to the Lower House of the Legislature (e.g., India, UK).
      4. **Presidential Executive:** Directly elected by citizens, not accountable to the Legislature; Head of State and Head of Government are the same (e.g., **United States of America**).
      5. **Single Executive:** Executive power concentrated in a single central authority (e.g., **England / USA**).
      6. **Plural Executive:** Executive power distributed among multiple persons/institutions (e.g., **Switzerland**).
      7. **Political Executive:** Elected by the people for a fixed term (Cabinet).
      8. **Permanent Executive:** Professional **bureaucracy / civil service** serving until retirement.
  - **9.3 The Judiciary:**
    - **Etymology & Thinkers:** Derived from Latin ***Justitia***; called the **"security-guard of the nation"** and **"protector of rights and freedom"**. Quotes by **John Rawls**, **Lord Bryce**, **Justice P.N. Bhagwati**, and **Justice Charles Evans Hughes** (*"We are under the Constitution, but the Constitution is what the judges say it is"*).
    - **Types of Judicial Structure:**
      1. **Unified Judiciary:** Single hierarchical pyramid of courts from Magistrate/Sessions Courts to High Courts to the Supreme Court as the final court of appeal (e.g., **India, Canada**).
      2. **Federal Judiciary:** Separate judicial systems for the Centre and the States (e.g., **USA, Switzerland** — in Switzerland, judges are elected by the Federal Assembly).
    - **Functions:** Interpreting laws, judicial decisions, **issuing writs**, giving judicial advice to the government, and **Judicial Review** (examining the constitutionality of legislative acts and executive orders, practiced in **India and USA**).
- **Mapping & Disambiguation Rules:**
  - **Map here** for conceptual questions on the root words (*Parler/Parliamentum*, *Exsequi*, *Justitia*), names of foreign parliaments (*Majlis, Bundestag, Knesset, Folketing*), unicameral vs. bicameral countries, nominal vs. real / parliamentary vs. presidential / single vs. plural (Switzerland) executives, and unified vs. federal judiciary.
  - **Do NOT map here** if the question asks for specific **Indian Constitutional articles, age qualifications, seat numbers, or powers of the Indian President/PM/Lok Sabha/Rajya Sabha** $\to$ map to `ss-7-10-union-government`.

### `ss-7-10-union-government` — Chapter 10: Union Government
- **Book Metadata:** Class 7 Social Science Part 1 | Chapter 10 | Book pp. 144–153 (PDF pp. 158–167) | **Sub-Discipline:** Civics (Indian Polity)
- **Canonical Topic Aliases:** Parliament of India, Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha, President and Vice-President of India, Prime Minister and Union Cabinet, 28 States and 8 Union Territories
- **Core Concepts & Subtopics:**
  - **10.1 The Union of India & Union Legislature (Parliament):**
    - India is a Union of **28 States and 8 Union Territories** (full table of capitals on Book p. 153 / PDF p. 167, including **Amaravati** for Andhra Pradesh, **Hyderabad** for Telangana, **Srinagar/Jammu** for J&K, **Leh** for Ladakh, **Silvassa** for Dadra & Nagar Haveli and Daman & Diu).
    - **Parliament of India:** Consists of the **President of India**, the **Lok Sabha (Lower House)**, and the **Rajya Sabha (Upper House)**.
    - **New Parliament House:** Inaugurated on **28 May 2023** (Central Vista expansion project), with an **888-seat Lok Sabha hall** and a **384-seat Rajya Sabha hall**.
    - **Lok Sabha (Lower House):**
      - **Maximum strength:** **552 members**; directly elected by citizens aged **18 years and above** for a **5-year term**.
      - **Qualifications:** Citizen of India; minimum age **25 years**; no office of profit under the government; sound mind; not an undischarged insolvent or sentenced criminal.
      - **Presiding Officer:** **Speaker** (elected by Lok Sabha members from among themselves).
      - **Exclusive Financial Power:** **Financial / Money Bills must be presented first in the Lok Sabha.**
    - **Rajya Sabha (Upper House):**
      - **Maximum strength:** **250 members** — **238 elected** by Members of State Legislative Assemblies + **12 nominated by the President**.
      - **Qualifications & Tenure:** Minimum age **30 years**; members serve a **6-year term**.
      - **Ex-Officio Chairman:** **Vice-President of India**.
  - **10.2 The Union Executive:**
    - Composed of the **President, Prime Minister, and Union Council of Ministers (Cabinet)**.
    - **President of India:**
      - Head of the Indian Republic and **First Citizen of India**; official residence is **Rashtrapati Bhavan** (New Delhi, completed in **1929** with **340 rooms**).
      - **Electoral College:** Elected by the **elected members of both Houses of Parliament (Lok Sabha & Rajya Sabha)** and the **elected members of State Legislative Assemblies**.
      - **Qualifications & Term:** Minimum age **35 years**, qualified to be a Lok Sabha member; **5-year term**.
      - **Powers:** Appoints the Leader of the majority party in Lok Sabha as **Prime Minister** and other Ministers on the PM's advice; gives assent to Bills to become Acts; **Supreme Commander of the Defence Forces** (power to declare war); appoints Judges of the Supreme Court and High Courts; **power to grant pardon** and reduce punishment.
    - **Vice-President of India:**
      - Elected by the **members of both Houses of Parliament**; minimum age **35 years**; **5-year term**; acts as **Chairman of the Rajya Sabha** and discharges the President's functions during the President's absence.
    - **Prime Minister & Union Cabinet:**
      - **Prime Minister:** **Leader of the Lok Sabha** and **Head of the Government** (Real Executive); allocates and reshuffles portfolios; recommends appointment/dismissal of Ministers; **resignation of the PM dissolves the entire Cabinet**.
      - **Collective Responsibility:** The Union Cabinet is collectively accountable to the **Lok Sabha** and must resign if the Lok Sabha passes a **No-Confidence Motion**.

      - **Powers:** Appoints the **Chief Minister** and other Ministers (on CM's advice); gives assent to State Bills; can report constitutional breakdown to the President recommending dissolution of the State Government and imposition of **President's Rule** (during which the Governor administers the state).

      - **Real Head of the State Government**; leader of the majority party in the Legislative Assembly; allocates/changes portfolios, can dismiss ministers, and maintains Centre–State relations.

### `ss-7-12-physical-divisions-india` — Chapter 12: Physical Divisions of India
- **Book Metadata:** Class 7 Social Science Part 1 | Chapter 12 | Book pp. 161–170 (PDF pp. 175–184) | **Sub-Discipline:** Geography (Geography of India & Karnataka)
- **Canonical Topic Aliases:** Geographical Location of India and Karnataka, Four Physical Divisions of India, Islands and Thar Desert, Three Physical Divisions of Karnataka (Coastal Plain, Malnad, Maidan)
- **Core Concepts & Subtopics:**
  - **12.1 India — Geographical Context:**
    - Located entirely in the **Northern Hemisphere**; **7th largest country by area**; **28 States and 8 Union Territories**.
    - **Latitudinal & Longitudinal Extent:** **$8^\circ\text{N}$ to $37^\circ\text{N}$ latitudes** and **$68^\circ\text{E}$ to $97^\circ\text{E}$ longitudes**.
    - **Tropic of Cancer ($23\frac{1}{2}^\circ\text{N}$)** passes through the centre of India; **Indian Standard Time (IST)** is based on **$82\frac{1}{2}^\circ\text{E}$ longitude**.
    - **Extremes:** Kashmir (North) to Kanyakumari (South); Rann of Kutch (West) to Arunachal Pradesh (East). Sri Lanka is separated from India by the **Palk Strait** and **Gulf of Mannar**.
  - **12.2 Four Major Physical Divisions of India:**
    1. **The Northern Mountains (Himalayas):** Highest mountain range in the world; **Mt. Everest ($8,848\text{ m}$)** is the highest peak in the world, while **Mt. Godwin Austen / Mt. $\text{K}_2$ ($8,611\text{ m}$)** is the **highest peak in India**. Checks cold Central Asian winds and intercepts monsoon winds.
    2. **The Northern Plains:** Alluvial plains formed by the **Sutlej, Ganga, and Brahmaputra** rivers and their tributaries between the Himalayas and Peninsular Plateau.
    3. **The Peninsular Plateau:** Largest and oldest physical division (hard rocks). Divided by the **Narmada–Son rift valley** into:
       - **Malwa Plateau (North):** Lies between the **Aravalli Range** (highest peak: **Mt. Guru Shikhar, $1,722\text{ m}$**) and the **Vindhya Range**.
       - **Deccan Plateau (South):** Bounded by **Satpura, Maikala, Amarkantak, and Rajmahal hills** in the north, **Western Ghats** in the west, and **Eastern Ghats** in the east. Western Ghats continue south as **Anamalai, Cardamom, and Palani hills**; **Anaimudi ($2,695\text{ m}$)** on the Anamalai hills is the **highest peak in South India**. Western and Eastern Ghats meet at the **Nilgiri Hills** (location of **Ooty**).
    4. **The Coastal Plains:**
       - **Western Coastal Plain:** Narrow and rocky, between Western Ghats and Arabian Sea (Gulf of Kutch to Kanyakumari).
       - **Eastern Coastal Plain:** Broader, between Eastern Ghats and Bay of Bengal (Gangetic delta to Kanyakumari).
  - **12.3 Island Groups & Thar Desert of India:**
    - **Islands (247 total):** **204 islands in the Bay of Bengal** (**Andaman & Nicobar Islands**, formed of hard volcanic rocks) and **43 islands in the Arabian Sea** (**Lakshadweep Islands**, formed of **coral sand**).
    - **Thar Desert:** Located in North-Western India ($\frac{2}{3}$ in Rajasthan, rest in Punjab, Haryana, Gujarat); contains salt lakes called ***Playas*** (e.g., **Sambhar, Didwana, Sargol**). **Sambhar Lake** is the **largest salt lake in India**.
  - **12.4 Karnataka — Location & Three Physical Divisions:**
    - **Location & Size:** South-western India; **8th largest state** in India (note: Ch. 21 mentions 7th after Telangana split, area $1,91,791\text{ sq. km}$); **$11^\circ\text{N}$ to $18^\circ\text{N}$ latitudes** and **$74^\circ\text{E}$ to $78^\circ\text{E}$ longitudes**; **31 Districts and 227 Taluks**.
    - **Three Physical Divisions of Karnataka:**
      1. **The Coastal Plain (*Karavali*):** Stretches **$324\text{ km}$** from Goa to Kerala across **3 coastal districts: Uttara Kannada, Udupi, and Dakshina Kannada**; includes **St. Mary's Island (Coconut Island)** and **New Mangalore Port** (called the **"Gateway of Karnataka"**).
      2. **The Malnad Region (*Sahyadri Ranges*):** Hilly, densely forested region receiving heavy rainfall; **Agumbe** receives the **highest rainfall in Karnataka**; **Mullayanagiri ($1,913\text{ m}$)** is the **highest peak in Karnataka**; famous for **Jog Falls**.
      3. **The Maidan Region (Plains/Plateau):** Divided by the **Tungabhadra River** into:
         - **Northern Maidan:** Flat basalt plateau covered with **black soil**, sloping east towards the Krishna and Bhima rivers, with low rainfall.
         - **Southern Maidan (Mysore Plateau):** Undulating plateau with **red soil**, sloping east towards the Kaveri river, with hills like **Biligirirangana, Male Mahadeshwara, Nandi, and Chamundi hills**.
    - **Special Textbook Box Facts:** **Yana** rocky hillocks (Uttara Kannada); African bottle-shaped **Baobab (*Gorakhnath*) trees at Savanur** (Haveri district); **Mudhol Hounds** (Vijayapura/Bagalkot); **Banhatti** handloom sarees & **Ilkal** silk sarees (Bagalkot); **Athani** slippers; **Maski** hillock shaped like a reclining Buddha (Raichur); **Chuliki river** at Basavakalyana; **Davanagere** known as the **"Manchester of Karnataka"** (famous for cotton textiles).
- **Mapping & Disambiguation Rules:**
  - **Map here** for physical divisions of India and Karnataka, highest peaks ($\text{K}_2$, Guru Shikhar, Anaimudi, Mullayanagiri), islands (247 = 204 + 43), Sambhar lake / Playas, New Mangalore ("Gateway of Karnataka"), Agumbe, and Davanagere ("Manchester of Karnataka").

### `ss-7-13-north-america` — Chapter 13: North America
- **Book Metadata:** Class 7 Social Science Part 1 | Chapter 13 | Book pp. 171–193 (PDF pp. 185–207) | **Sub-Discipline:** Geography (World Regional Geography)
- **Canonical Topic Aliases:** Continent of North America, Physical Divisions of North America (Rockies, Great Plains, Canadian Shield, Appalachians), Rivers and Great Lakes (HOMES), Climate, Taiga and Prairies, Agriculture and Minerals of North America
- **Core Concepts & Subtopics:**
  - **13.1 Location, Extent, & Setting:**
    - Entirely in the Northern Hemisphere (**$7^\circ\text{N}$ to $84^\circ\text{N}$ latitudes**); **3rd largest continent** in area ($16.46\%$ of world land, $24.24\text{ million sq. km}$, ~7 times bigger than India); consists of **24 countries** (largest: **Canada, USA, Mexico**).
    - Separated from Asia by the **Bering Strait**; linked to South America by the **Isthmus of Panama**; **Greenland** (Denmark) is the largest island.
  - **13.2 Four Major Physical Divisions of North America:**
    1. **The Western Cordilleras (Rocky Mountains):** Young folded mountains from Alaska to Panama. Highest peak: **Mt. McKinley ($6,194\text{ m}$)** in Alaska; lowest point: **Death Valley ($86\text{ m}$ below sea level)**; intermontane plateaus: **Columbia, Colorado, Mexican, Yucatan**; **Old Faithful Geyser** in **Yellowstone National Park (USA)**.
    2. **The Great Central Plains (Prairies):** Covers $\frac{3}{5}$ of North America between the Rockies and Appalachians; formed by the **Mississippi and Missouri** rivers; subject to local tornadoes causing the **Dust Bowl**.
    3. **The Canadian Shield (Laurentian Plateau):** Surrounds **Hudson Bay**; composed of some of the oldest rocks in the world.
    4. **The Appalachian Highlands (Eastern Highlands):** Old folded mountains from Newfoundland to Alabama; rich in **coal, iron ore, copper, lead, zinc, and hydroelectricity**.
  - **13.3 Rivers & Great Lakes:**
    - **Rivers:** **Mississippi–Missouri** (largest river system, drains $\frac{2}{3}$ of North America into the Gulf of Mexico); **Colorado River** (carves the **Grand Canyon** in Arizona, USA — *"Natural Wonder of the World"*); **St. Lawrence River** (connects Great Lakes to Atlantic, frozen 3 months/year); **Yukon, Mackenzie, Columbia, Rio Grande**.
    - **Five Great Lakes — Acronym `HOMES`:** **H**uron, **O**ntario, **M**ichigan, **E**rie, and **S**uperior. **Lake Superior** is the **largest freshwater lake in the world**. (**Chicago** is on Lake Michigan; **Detroit** is on Lake Erie).
  - **13.4 Climate, Natural Vegetation, & Wildlife:**
    - **Tundra Belt (Arctic Circle — Alaska, Northern Canada, Greenland):** Mosses and lichens; **Reindeer, Caribou, Polar Bear**.
    - **Taiga / Coniferous Forest Belt:** Evergreen softwoods (**pine, fir, spruce, larch**); fur-bearing animals (**Lynx, Sable, Silver Fox, Beaver**). **Canada** is the world's largest exporter of timber, paper, pulp, and newsprint; Canadian woodcutters are called **Lumberjacks**.
    - **Temperate Grasslands (Prairies):** Vast treeless plain known as the **"Wheat Bowl / Wheat Basket of the World"**.
    - **Deciduous & Mixed Forests:** Cypress, oak, ash, chestnut, birch, beech, **maple**.
    - **Tropical Forests (Southern Mexico & West Indies):** Hardwoods like **mahogany and logwood**.
  - **13.5 Agriculture, Fishing, Minerals, & Industries:**

Covered by the world's largest tropical rainforest, locally called **Selvas** (hardwoods like **mahogany, ebony**, and **rubber trees** yielding *latex*).
    - **Grasslands of South America ("Land of Grasslands"):**
      - **Llanos:** Tropical grasslands in the **Orinoco basin (Venezuela)**.
      - **Campos:** Tropical grasslands in the **Brazilian Highlands (Brazil)**.
      - **Pampas:** Temperate grasslands south of **Gran Chaco** in **Argentina and Uruguay** (ideal for wheat and cattle/beef grazing).
    - **Deserts:**
      - **Atacama Desert (Chile):** **Driest area on Earth** (rain-shadow region; no rainfall for 200 years).
      - **Patagonia Plateau:** Temperate desert in the south (sheep rearing).
  - **14.4 Wildlife, Agriculture, & Population:**
    - **Unique Wildlife:** **Condor** (biggest flying bird in the world), **Rhea** (large flightless bird), **Anaconda** (largest reptile), **Piranha** (most dangerous flesh-eating fish) & electric fish, **Puma & Jaguar** (predators), **Llama & Alpaca** (camel-like pack/wool animals in the Andes), and **Giant Tortoise** (**Galapagos Islands**).
    - **Agriculture & Livestock:**
      - **Coffee:** **Brazil** is the world's largest producer of coffee — called the **"Coffee Pot of the World"**; **Rio de Janeiro** is called the **"Coffee Port of the World"** (Colombia and Ecuador also produce coffee).
      - **Sugarcane & Cocoa:** Brazil is the 2nd largest producer of sugarcane in the world.
      - **Gauchos:** Cowboys who herd cattle on the **Pampas** of Argentina/Uruguay.
    - **Population:** Mixed racial groups; **$80\%$ urbanized** (highest in **Uruguay at $93\%$**, lowest in Guyana at $36\%$); major cities: **São Paulo** (*"City of Skyscrapers"*), **Buenos Aires, Rio de Janeiro, Valparaiso**.

    - Founded by **Atmaram Panduranga** in **1867 C.E.**

    - Led by Anglo-Indian youth **Henry Vivian Derozio** in Bengal, calling on youth for independent and rational thinking.
  - **15.5 Arya Samaj (1875 C.E.) — Swami Dayananda Saraswati:**
    - Founded by **Swami Dayananda Saraswati (1824–1883 C.E.**; original name **Moolashankar**) in **1875 C.E.**
    - Slogan: ***"Return to the Vedas"***; wrote ***Satyartha Prakasha***; condemned idol worship, untouchability, child marriage, and caste by birth; declared everyone including women had the right to study the Vedas; inspired ***Swarajya* and *Swadeshi***.

    - Named *"Swami Vivekananda"* in **1892 C.E.** on the suggestion of **Ajit Singh, King of Khetri**. Prior to his US voyage, **Mysore King Chamarajendra Wodeyar X** invited him to Mysore Palace, funded his trip, and on his advice opened **three separate schools for Dalits**.

    - Established **Ramakrishna Mission in 1897 C.E.**; famous calls: ***"Arise! Awake! and Stop not till the goal is reached"*** and ***"Upliftment of women is upliftment of the nation"***. **Bal Gangadhar Tilak** called him **"The True Father of Indian Nationalism"**.

    - Founded in **New York (1875 C.E.)** by Russian lady **Madame H.P. Blavatsky** and American Colonel **H.S. Olcott**, who established its International Office at **Adyar (near Chennai)**.

### `ss-7-16-first-war-indian-independence` — Chapter 16: First War of Indian Independence (1857–58 C.E.)
- **Book Metadata:** Class 7 Social Science Part 2 | Chapter 16 | Book pp. 14–22 (PDF pp. 16–24) | **Sub-Discipline:** History (Modern Indian History)
- **Canonical Topic Aliases:** Revolt of 1857, Sepoy Mutiny vs. First War of Indian Independence, Mangal Pandey, Leaders of 1857 Revolt, Queen Victoria's Proclamation of 1858
- **Core Concepts & Subtopics:**
  - **16.1 Nature & Historiography of 1857:**
    - Called a mere *"Sepoy Mutiny"* by British historians, but **Vinayak Damodar Savarkar** was the **first to call it "The First War of Indian Independence"** (supported by **Pattabhi Sitaramayya** and **Jawaharlal Nehru**).
  - **16.2 Causes of the 1857 Revolt:**
    1. **Political Causes:** Wellesley's **Subsidiary Alliance** and Dalhousie's **Doctrine of Lapse** (annexation of **Satara, Jaipur, Sambalpur, Udaipur, Jhansi, Oudh**); humiliation of last Mughal king **Bahadur Shah II**; cancellation of royal titles and pensions.
    2. **Administrative Causes:** Reservation of high civil/military posts for Europeans; replacement of **Persian by English** as the language of courts.
    3. **Economic Causes:** Destruction of cottage industries, exploitative land revenue, confiscation of *zamindaris/talukdaris*, commercialization of agriculture, and Drain of Wealth.
    4. **Social & Religious Causes:** Racial arrogance and **"The White Man's Burden"** theory (propagated by **Rudyard Kipling**); *"Dogs and Indians are prohibited"* signs at European clubs; conservative suspicion towards abolition of Sati/child marriage, Christian missionary conversions, railways, and telegraph.
    5. **Military Causes:** Low pay and no promotions for Indian sepoys; ban on religious symbols/headgear; **Lord Canning's "Common Military Service Act"** (requiring every soldier to serve overseas/wherever ordered).
    6. **Immediate Cause:** Introduction of the **Enfield Rifle (1857)** whose cartridge paper was rumoured to be greased with **pig and cow fat** and had to be bitten off before loading.
  - **16.3 Course & Centres of the Revolt:**
    - **Barrackpore:** **Mangal Pandey** refused to use the greased cartridge, shot a British official in broad daylight at Barrackpore, and was executed.
    - **Outbreak at Meerut (10 May 1857 C.E.):** Sepoys revolted at **Meerut**, shouted ***"Maro Farangi Ko"*** (*Kill the foreigners!*), marched to **Delhi**, and proclaimed **Bahadur Shah II** as Emperor of India.
    - **Major Centres & Leaders:**
      - **Delhi:** **Bahadur Shah II** and **Commander Bakht Khan**.
      - **Kanpur:** **Nana Saheb** and **Tatya Tope**.
      - **Lucknow:** **Begum Hazrat Mahal**.
      - **Jhansi:** **Rani Laxmibai**.
      - **Arrah (Bihar):** **Kunwar Singh**.
      - **Karnataka:** **Mundargi Bhima Rao**, **Hunters (Bedanayakas) of Halagali**, **Raja Venkatappa Nayaka of Surapura**, and **Baba Saheb of Nargund**.
  - **16.4 Consequences of the Revolt:**
    - End of **East India Company rule**; administration of India was taken over directly by the **British Crown (Queen Victoria)**.
    - **Queen Victoria's Proclamation (1858 C.E.):** Promised to respect the rights, customs, and traditions of Indians and non-interference in religious matters.

Studied law in Britain (returned 1891); went to **South Africa** as legal consultant for **Dada Abdullah & Co.**, where he forged **Satyagraha** (*Satya* = truth + *Agraha* = firmness/persuasion) against apartheid.
    - **Influences, Ashram, & Writings:** Influenced by the *Bhagavadgita*, **John Ruskin's** *Unto This Last*, **Leo Tolstoy's** *The Kingdom of God Is Within You*, and the play *Satya Harishchandra*. Returned to India in **1915 C.E.**, established **Sabarmati Ashram (1916 C.E., Ahmedabad)**, and toured India on the advice of his **political guru Gopala Krishna Gokhale**. Books: ***My Experiments with Truth*, *Hind Swaraj*, *Satyagraha in South Africa***; weeklies: ***Harijan*** and ***Young India***.
    - **Khilafat Movement (1919 C.E.):** Led in India by the **Ali Brothers (Mohammed Ali and Shaukat Ali)** in support of the Caliph of Turkey (deposed by **Kamal Pasha**); backed by Gandhiji for Hindu–Muslim unity.

### `ss-7-18-unification-of-karnataka` — Chapter 18: Unification of Karnataka and Border Disputes

- **Core Concepts & Subtopics:**
  - **18.1 Historical Background & 20 Administrative Centres:**
    - Ancient work ***Kavirajamarga*** described Kannada land as stretching from the **Kaveri to the Godavari**. Under British rule, Kannada-speaking areas were fragmented across **20 administrative centres**:
      1. **Madras–Karnataka:** Ballari, South Canara, Nilgiri, Kollegal taluk.
      2. **Mumbai–Karnataka:** North Canara, Dharwad, Vijayapura, Belagavi.
      3. **Coorg (Kodagu):** Chief Commissioner's province.
      4. **Cantonments:** Belagavi and Bengaluru military stations.
      5. **Mysore Princely State (9 districts):** Mysuru, Mandya, Hassan, Shivamogga, Tumakuru, Chikkamagaluru, Chitradurga, Kolar, Bengaluru.
      6. **Hyderabad-Karnataka (3 districts):** Kalaburagi, Bidar, Raichur.
      7. **14 Small Princely States** (Total = 20 units).
  - **18.2 Organizations, Newspapers, & Literary Pioneers:**
    - **Karnataka Vidya Vardhaka Sangha (1890 C.E., Dharwad):** Founded under the presidentship of **R.H. Deshpande** (with early leader **Venkata Rango Katti**).
    - **Kannada Sahitya Parishat (1915 C.E., Bengaluru)** and **Karnataka Sabha (1916 C.E.)**.
    - **1924 Belagavi Congress Session:** Presided over by **Mahatma Gandhi**; **Huyilgol Narayan Rao** sang the welcome anthem ***"Udayavagali Namma Cheluva Kannada Nadu"***.
    - **Newspapers:**
      - *Bombay–Karnataka:* ***Vijaya*, *Rajahamsa*, *Kannada Kesari*, *Karnataka Vaibhava*, *Karnataka Vrutta***.

      - **Alur Venkata Rao — *"Karnataka Kulapurohita"* (conferred in 1941 by Hyderabad-Kannadigas):** Published ***Karnataka Gatha Vaibhava* (*Past Glory of Karnataka*)** with his own funds.
      - **Shantakavi:** Sang *"Bedalu Kannada Dasayya Bandiha..."* door-to-door to collect funds for Kannada Sahitya Sammelana.
      - **Rashtrakavi Kuvempu:** Composed *"Jaya Bharata Jananiya Tanujate / Jayahe Karnataka Maate"* and *"Nee Mettuva Nela Ade Karnataka"*.
      - **First Rashtrakavi Manjeshwar Govinda Pai** & **Kayyara Kinhanna Rai:** Fought tirelessly for the inclusion of **Kasaragod** in Karnataka.
  - **18.3 Three Stages of Post-Independence Unification:**
    1. **First Stage (1947–48 C.E.):** Constituent Assembly grouped Indian states into Parts A, B, C, and D, consolidating Karnataka's 20 units into **5 units: Mumbai, Madras, Mysore, Hyderabad, and Coorg**.
    2. **Second Stage (1953 C.E.):** **Akhanda Karnataka Rajya Nirmana Parishat (1952)** launched satyagraha (supported by CM **Kengal Hanumanthaiah**). Fazal Ali** with members **H.N. Kunzru** and **K.M. Panikkar** (report submitted in **1955 C.E.**).     - **Karnataka–Maharashtra Dispute:** Over **Belagavi** (claimed by *Maharashtra Ekikaran Samiti*) and Kannada-majority taluks in Maharashtra (**Akkalkot, South Solapur, Jat**). In **1966 C.E.**, the Centre appointed a one-man commission under **Justice Meherchand Mahajan**, whose **Mahajan Report** ruled that **Belagavi must remain an integral part of Karnataka** (affirmed by Karnataka building **Suvarna Vidhana Soudha** at Belagavi).
    - **Karnataka–Kerala Dispute:** Over **Kasaragod** (historically ruled by Keladi/Ikkeri Nayakas who built **Bekal Fort**, merged with Kerala by the 1955 SRC report); struggle led by **Manjeshwar Govinda Pai** and **Kayyara Kinhanna Rai**.

### `ss-7-22-judiciary` — Chapter 22: Judiciary
- **Book Metadata:** Class 7 Social Science Part 2 | Chapter 22 | Book pp. 108–114 (PDF pp. 110–116) | **Sub-Discipline:** Civics (Indian Judicial System)
- **Canonical Topic Aliases:** Supreme Court of India, High Courts, Subordinate Civil and Criminal Courts, Lok Adalat (People's Court), Public Interest Litigation (PIL)
- **Core Concepts & Subtopics:**
  - **22.1 The Supreme Court of India (New Delhi):**
    - Highest court of law in India; consists of the **Chief Justice of India and 33 other Judges** (total = 34), all **appointed by the President of India**.
    - **Qualifications of a Supreme Court Judge:** Citizen of India + either **(a) High Court Judge for at least 5 years**, or **(b) High Court Advocate for at least 10 years**, or **(c) a distinguished jurist** in the President's opinion.
    - **Retirement Age:** **65 years**.
    - **Functions:** Resolving disputes between the Union and States or between States; issuing **Writs** to safeguard Fundamental Rights; interpreting the Constitution; advising the President when asked.
  - **22.2 The High Courts:**
    - Highest court at the State level (**High Court of Karnataka is in Bengaluru**). There are **25 High Courts in India**.
    - **Qualifications of a High Court Judge:** Citizen of India + served in the **Indian Judicial Service for at least 10 years** OR as a **High Court Advocate for at least 10 years**.
    - **Appointment & Retirement Age:** Appointed by the **President of India** in consultation with the **Chief Justice of India and the State Governor**; retirement age is **62 years**.
  - **22.3 Subordinate Courts, Lok Adalat, & PIL:**
    - **Subordinate Courts:** District Courts, Magistrate's Courts, Junior Magistrate's Courts; divided into **Civil Courts** (property, money, contracts) and **Criminal Courts** (murder, theft, robbery).
    - **Lok Adalat (People's Court):** Settles cases rapidly and inexpensively through **mutual compromise**; its judgement is **final and binding on both parties, and no appeal can be made against it in any higher court**.
    - **Public Interest Litigation (P.I.L.):** Citizens can complain directly by letter to the court (treated as a writ petition without court fees) when a government or public body violates laws or exploits citizens.
    - **Presidential Immunity Box:** The **President of India** cannot be arrested during their term of office, no criminal case can be filed against them, and official acts cannot be questioned in any court.

### `ss-7-25-united-nations-organization` — Chapter 25: United Nations Organization (U.N.O.)
- **Book Metadata:** Class 7 Social Science Part 2 | Chapter 25 | Book pp. 135–144 (PDF pp. 137–146) | **Sub-Discipline:** Civics (International Organizations)
- **Canonical Topic Aliases:** Birth and Aims of UNO, Six Principal Organs of UN, Veto Power and Permanent Five, Specialized Agencies (UNESCO, FAO, WHO, UNICEF), India's Role in the UN
- **Core Concepts & Subtopics:**
  - **25.1 Origin, Emblem, & Languages of the UN:**
    - Following World War II (**1939–1945**, ~4 crore deaths), **US President Franklin D. Roosevelt, British PM Winston Churchill, and Soviet leader Joseph Stalin** led representatives of **51 nations** at **San Francisco** to establish the **United Nations Organization on 24 October 1945** (**24 October is observed as UN Day**).
    - **Emblem & Flag:** Light blue flag with the world map surrounded by **two olive branches** (symbolizing peace and friendship).
    - **Official Languages (6):** **English, Russian, Spanish, Chinese, French, and Arabic**.
    - **Membership:** **193 member states** (**South Sudan / Sudan** is the 193rd member).
  - **25.2 Six Principal Organs (Main Bodies) of the UN:**
    1. **The General Assembly:** Meets annually every **September**; each member nation can send 5 representatives but has **only 1 vote**; functions as the **"Parliament of the UN"**.
    2. **The Security Council:** Executive organ for international peace and security; consists of **15 members**:
       - **5 Permanent Members (with Veto Power):** **USA, Russia, Britain (UK), France, and China**.
       - **10 Non-Permanent (Temporary) Members:** Elected by the General Assembly for a **2-year term**. Passing a resolution requires **9 votes** including the concurrence of all 5 permanent members.
    3. **The Economic and Social Council (ECOSOC):** Addresses human rights, illiteracy, poverty, refugees, health, and women/child welfare.
    4. **The International Court of Justice (ICJ):** Headquartered at **The Hague (Netherlands)**; consists of **15 Judges** elected by the General Assembly and Security Council.
    5. **The Secretariat:** Permanent headquarters in **New York (Lake Success)**; headed by the **Secretary-General** (nominated by Security Council, appointed by General Assembly). Present Secretary-General: **Mr. António Guterres (Portugal)**.
    6. **The Trusteeship Council:** Formed to supervise **11 trust territories**, all of which attained independence by **1994**.
  - **25.3 Specialized Agencies of the UN (Headquarters):**
    - **UNESCO** (UN Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organization) $\to$ **Paris, France**.
    - **FAO** (Food and Agriculture Organization) $\to$ **Rome, Italy**.
    - **WHO** (World Health Organization) $\to$ **Geneva, Switzerland**.
    - **UNICEF** (UN International Children's Emergency Fund) $\to$ **New York, USA**.
  - **25.4 Achievements & India's Contribution to the UN:**
    - **Universal Declaration of Human Rights:** Adopted on **10 December 1948**.
    - **Prominent Indians in the UN:**
      - **Smt. Vijaya Lakshmi Pandit:** **First woman President (Chairperson) of the UN General Assembly**.
      - **Justice Benegal Narsing Rau (B.N. Rau):** Judge of the International Court of Justice.
      - **Dr. Nagendra Singh:** Served as **Chief Justice (President) of the International Court of Justice**.
      - **General K.S. Thimayya:** Commanded UN Peacekeeping operations.

### `ss-7-26-australia` — Chapter 26: Australia
- **Book Metadata:** Class 7 Social Science Part 2 | Chapter 26 | Book pp. 145–164 (PDF pp. 147–166) | **Sub-Discipline:** Geography (World Regional Geography)
- **Canonical Topic Aliases:** Continent of Australia, Oceania, Physical Divisions of Australia (Great Dividing Range, Central Lowlands/Artesian Basin, Western Plateau), Murray-Darling Basin & Lake Eyre, Willy-Willies, Downs & Savanna, Marsupials & Monotremes, Merino Sheep & Minerals (Kalgoorlie/Coolgardie)
- **Core Concepts & Subtopics:**
  - **26.1 Location, Extent, & Nicknames:**
    - Derived from Latin ***Austral*** (meaning *South*); lies entirely in the **Southern and Eastern Hemispheres** ($10^\circ 45'\text{S}$ to $43^\circ 39'\text{S}$ and $113^\circ 9'\text{E}$ to $150^\circ 39'\text{E}$); **Tropic of Capricorn** passes through its middle.
    - **Smallest (7th) continent** in both area ($76.82\text{ lakh sq. km}$, double the size of India) and population; part of **Oceania** (~10,000 Pacific islands).
    - Discovered by European explorer **Captain James Cook** (18th century; textbook prints 17th century) $\to$ called the **"New Continent"**.
    - **Other Nicknames:** **"Island Continent"**, **"Flattest Continent"** ($94\%$ of land is $<600\text{ m}$ elevation), and **"Desert Continent"**.
    - Bounded by **Timor & Arafura Seas** (NW), **Torres Strait & Coral Sea** (NE), **Tasman Sea** (SE), **Bass Strait** (separating Tasmania), and **Great Australian Bight** (South).
  - **26.2 Three Major Physical Divisions of Australia:**
    1. **The Eastern Highlands (Great Dividing Range):** Runs parallel to the east coast from **Cape York Peninsula** to **Bass Strait (Tasmania)**; called **Australian Alps and New England Ranges** in New South Wales; highest peak of Australia is **Mt. Kosciuszko ($2,230\text{ m}$)**. Off the NE coast lies the **Great Barrier Reef** (~$2,000\text{ km}$ long coral reef).
    2. **The Central Lowlands:** Divided into **(a) Carpentaria Lowlands**, **(b) Lake Eyre Basin** (contains the **world's largest Artesian Basin**), and **(c) Murray–Darling Basin** (richest agricultural zone). **Lake Eyre** is a saucer-shaped salt lake and the **lowest natural point of Australia**; west of it lies **Ayers Rock (Uluru)** — the **world's largest monolithic rock**.
    3. **The Western Plateau:** Ancient Gondwana shield covering $\frac{1}{3}$ of Australia; contains the **Great Sandy Desert, Gibson Desert, Tanami Desert, Simpson Desert, and Great Victoria Desert**, plus central **MacDonnell and Musgrave Ranges**.
  - **26.3 Rivers, Climate, Vegetation, & Unique Wildlife:**
    - **Rivers:** **Murray River ($2,590\text{ km}$)** is the longest/most important river (rises near Mt. Kosciuszko, flows into **Encounter Bay**; main tributaries: **Darling, Lachlan, and Murrumbidgee**). Inland rivers (**Cooper Creek, Diamantina, Macumba**) drain into **Lake Eyre** (endorheic basin).
    - **Climate & Cyclones:** Seasons are **opposite to the Northern Hemisphere** (Summer: **December–February**; Winter: **June–August**). Tropical cyclones in Australia are called **"Willy-Willies"**.
    - **Grasslands & Vegetation:** Dominated by **Eucalyptus** (oil & paper) and **Acacia (Wattle)**.
      - **Tropical Grasslands (North):** Called **Savannah**.
      - **Temperate Grasslands (Murray–Darling Basin):** Called **Downs** (famous for *Tussock, Mitchell, and Astrebla* grasses).
    - **Unique Wildlife ($60\%$ endemic):**
      - **Platypus & Echidna:** World's only **egg-laying mammals (Monotremes)**.
      - **Kangaroo & Wallaby:** Pouched marsupials (**Kangaroo** is Australia's national animal/symbol).
      - **Koala:** Arboreal marsupial feeding on eucalyptus leaves.
      - **Dingo:** Wild wolf-like dog.
      - **Birds:** **Emu** (Australia's largest flightless bird), **Lyrebird** (peacock-like), **Kookaburra** (laughing bird), and **Black Swan**.
  - **26.4 Agriculture, Merino Sheep, Minerals, & Population:**
    - **Agriculture:** Only **$4\%$ of land is cultivated** (chiefly wheat in the Murray–Darling basin, exporting $70\%$ of wheat output; sugarcane & tobacco in Queensland; apples in Tasmania).
    - **Sheep Rearing:** World's leading producer and exporter of wool; **$75\%$ of Australian sheep belong to the *Merino* breed** (fine wool), raised on vast sheep farms called **"Stations" or "Ranches"**.
    - **Minerals:** World's **largest deposits of Bauxite**; famous gold mines at **Coolgardie and Kalgoorlie** (Western Australia); major reserves of **Iron ore** (exported to Japan), **Uranium** ($60\%$ in Northern Territory), **Manganese** (Gulf of Carpentaria), and **Copper/Coal** (New South Wales).
    - **Population:** Very low average density (**$3\text{ persons/sq. km}$**), **$85\%$ urbanized**; **New South Wales** is the most populous state ($\frac{1}{3}$ of population), followed by **Victoria** ($\frac{1}{4}$); half the population lives in 6 cities (**Sydney, Melbourne, Brisbane, Adelaide, Perth, Newcastle**).

Kosciuszko, Great Barrier Reef, Artesian Basin, Lake Eyre, Ayers Rock (Uluru), Murray–Darling rivers, Willy-Willies, Savannah vs. Downs grasslands, Platypus/Echidna (egg-laying mammals), Kangaroo/Koala/Emu/Kookaburra/Lyrebird/Dingo, Merino sheep stations, and Coolgardie/Kalgoorlie gold mines.

### `ss-7-27-antarctica` — Chapter 27: Antarctica
- **Book Metadata:** Class 7 Social Science Part 2 | Chapter 27 | Book pp. 165–174 (PDF pp. 167–176) | **Sub-Discipline:** Geography (World Regional Geography)
- **Canonical Topic Aliases:** Continent of Antarctica, White Continent / Continent of Science, Trans-Antarctic Mountains, Vinson Massif and Mt. Erebus, Polar Wildlife (Penguins, Krill, Seals, Whales), Polar Explorers (Amundsen, Scott), Antarctic Treaty (1959), Indian Research Stations (Dakshina Gangotri, Maitri, Bharati)
- **Core Concepts & Subtopics:**
  - **27.1 Location, Extent, & Physical Setting:**
    - Surrounds the **South Pole** within the **Antarctic Circle ($66^\circ 30'\text{S}$)**; **5th largest continent** in area (**$14.2\text{ million sq. km}$**).
    - Surrounded by the **Southern / Antarctic Ocean** (meeting of Indian, Pacific, and Atlantic Oceans).
    - **Nearest Landmass:** **Cape Horn** (southernmost tip of South America), at a distance of **$990\text{ km}$**.
  - **27.2 Physical Features, Climate, & Wildlife:**
    - **$98\%$ covered by an ice sheet** averaging **$2,300\text{ m}$ thick** (makes Antarctica the **highest continent by average elevation** and stores **$90\%$ of the world's fresh water**).
    - **Nicknames:** **"White Continent"**, **"Snow Desert"**, **"Cold Desert"**, and **"Continent of Science"**.
    - **Trans-Antarctic Mountain Range:** Divides the continent into **East Antarctica (Greater Antarctica)** and **West Antarctica (Smaller Antarctica)**, indented by two opposite gulfs: the **Ross Sea** and the **Weddell Sea**.
    - **Major Peaks & Volcanoes:**
      - **Vinson Massif ($5,140\text{ m}$):** Located in the **Ellsworth Mountains** — **highest peak in Antarctica**.
      - **Mt. Erebus:** Located on **Ross Island** — **Antarctica's most active volcano**.
    - **World's Lowest Temperature:** Recorded at **Vostok Station** (Russia) near the South Pole at **$-89^\circ\text{C}$**.
    - **Flora & Fauna:**
      - **Vegetation:** Mosses, algae, and lichens on exposed rocks.
      - **Fauna:** **Krill** (tiny shrimp-like crustaceans feeding on plankton, forming the base of the food chain), **Penguins** (large flightless birds; chief species: **Adelie, Emperor, and Chinstrap**; their rock-hollow breeding nests are called a **Rookery**; **Adelie penguins** are first to welcome ships), **Whales**, and **6 species of Seals**.
  - **27.3 Exploration & The Antarctic Treaty (1959 C.E.):**
    - **Explorers:**
      - **Charles Wilkes** (head of US Navy): **Discovered the continent of Antarctica**.
      - **James Clark Ross (Great Britain, 1841):** Discovered the **Ross Sea**.
      - **Ernest Shackleton (England, 1909):** Led early South Pole expedition.
      - **Roald Amundsen (Norwegian explorer, 1911 C.E.):** **First person to reach the South Pole (1911)**.
      - **Captain Robert Falcon Scott (1912 C.E.):** Reached the South Pole a month after Amundsen, but **died on the return journey** (his diary entries aided science).
      - **Admiral Richard E. Byrd (US Navy, 1929 C.E.):** **First person to fly over the South Pole by airplane**.
    - **The Antarctic Treaty (1959 / 1961 C.E.):**
      - Signed in **Washington D.C. on 1 December 1959** by **12 nations** and came into force on **23 June 1961** (applies south of **$60^\circ\text{S}$ latitude**).
      - Reserves Antarctica **exclusively for peaceful scientific research** (*"Continent of Science"*); prohibits nuclear weapons testing, territorial/water ownership claims, environmental pollution, bringing exotic species, and whale hunting.
  - **27.4 Indian Research Stations in Antarctica:**
    - **First Indian Expedition (1981–82):** Led by **Dr. S.Z. Qasim**; sailed from **Goa on 6 December 1981** aboard the ship ***Polar Circle*** and landed on **9 January 1982**.
    - **Three Indian Research Stations:**
      1. **Dakshina Gangotri (built 1983 C.E.):** **India's first research station** in Antarctica (now unmanned and used as a supply/storage base).
      2. **Maitri (1988–89 C.E.):** **India's second research station**, built on the rocky **Schirmacher Oasis** (accommodates 26 scientists for Geology, Geography, and Medicine). India built a freshwater lake named ***Priyadarshini*** $255\text{ m}$ from Maitri.
      3. **Bharati:** **India's third research station**, established for **Oceanography** research.

Erebus (active volcano on Ross Island), Vostok ($-89^\circ\text{C}$), Krill, Penguins (Adelie, Emperor, Chinstrap; *Rookery* nests), Charles Wilkes, Roald Amundsen (1911), Robert Falcon Scott (1912), Richard E.
