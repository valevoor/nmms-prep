### `ss-8-01-sources` — Chapter 1: Sources
- **Book Metadata:** Class 8 Social Science Part 1 | Chapter 1 | Book pp. 1–7 (PDF pp. 15–21) | **Sub-Discipline:** History
- **Canonical Topic Aliases:** Historical Sources, Literary and Archaeological Sources, Numismatics and Epigraphy, Oral Sources and Legends, Carbon-14 Dating
- **Core Concepts & Subtopics:**
  - **1.1 Meaning & Importance of Sources:** Sources are the basic materials required to reconstruct objective history (agricultural tools, temples, forts, inscriptions, coins, weapons, written documents, oral literature). Without sources, history cannot be constructed scientifically.
  - **1.2 Classification of Historical Sources:**
    1. **Literary Sources (Written Literature):**
       - **Native Literature (Written by Indians in Sanskrit, Prakrit, Kannada, Tamil, Telugu, Marathi, etc.):**
         - **Vishakhadatta:** *Mudrarakshasa*
         - **Kalhana:** *Rajatarangini* (History of Kashmir)
         - **Ashwaghosha:** *Buddha Charita*
         - **Kautilya (Chanakya / Vishnugupta):** *Arthashastra*
         - **Hala:** *Gathasaptashati* (Prakrit)
         - **Banabhatta:** *Harsha Charitha*
         - **Chand Bardai:** *Prithviraja Raso*
         - **Pampa:** *Vikramarjuna Vijaya* (Kannada)
         - **Srivijaya:** *Kavirajamarga* (First available Kannada work)
         - **Sangam Literature** (Tamil) & **Buddhist *Tripitakas*** (*Vinaya, Dhamma, Abhidhamma*).
       - **Foreign Literature (Accounts of foreign writers, travellers, and scholars):**
         - **Megasthenes** (Greek ambassador): *Indica*
         - **Hiuen Tsang** (Chinese Buddhist pilgrim): *Si-Yu-Ki*
         - **Fa-Hien** (Chinese pilgrim): *Gho-Kho-Ki* (*Fo-Kwo-Ki*)
         - **Ceylon (Sri Lankan) Chronicles:** *Deepavamsha* and *Mahavamsha*
         - **Ptolemy:** *Geography*
         - **Ziauddin Barani:** *Tarikh-i-Firoz Shahi*
         - **Vijayanagara Foreign Travellers:** **Fernao Nuniz**, **Duarte Barbosa**, and **Nicolo Conti**.
    2. **Archaeological Sources (Inscriptions, Coins, Monuments, Excavations):**
       - **Inscriptions (Epigraphy):** Living evidences providing data on religion, culture, economy, and administration.
         - **Ashoka's Inscriptions:** Earliest inscriptions obtained in India; engraved on rocks and stone pillars; mostly in **Prakrit language** using **Brahmi and Kharoshti scripts** (some in **Greek and Aramaic**). Found in Karnataka at **Maski** (Raichur) and **Brahmagiri** (Chitradurga).
         - **Halmidi Inscription:** **First inscription available in Kannada language**, engraved during the reign of **King Kakutsavarma** of the **Kadamba dynasty**.
         - **Other Landmark Inscriptions:** **Samudragupta's Allahabad Pillar Inscription** (by Harisena), **Pulakeshi II's Aihole Inscription** (by Ravikirti), **Kharavela's Hathigumpha Inscription**, **Cholas' Uttaramerur Inscription** (village self-government).
       - **Coins (Numismatics):** **Numismatics** is the study of the evolution, nature, and development of coins. **Samudragupta** issued **7 different types of gold coins**. Availability of **Roman coins in Bengaluru** proves trade links with Rome 2,000 years ago.
       - **Monuments:** Temples, *basadis*, *chaityalayas*, *stupas*, palaces, forts, sculptures — e.g., Ashoka's Pillars, **Ajanta and Ellora Caves**, **Elephanta Caves**, **Badami, Aihole, and Pattadakallu** (Karnataka), **Mehrauli Iron Pillar** (Delhi), **Whispering Gallery of Gol Gumbaz** (Vijayapura), **Taj Mahal** (Agra).
       - **Excavations:** Scientific digging out of hidden ruins — **Bhimbetka** and **Sanganakallu** (Stone Age culture); **Harappa** and **Mohenjo-daro** (Indus Valley Civilization).
    3. **Oral Sources and Legends (*Sthala-Puranas*):**
       - **Oral Literature:** Folk songs, poems, ballads (*lavani*) passed down generations recording local history — e.g., **Nayakas of Chitradurga**, **Kempegowda**, **Tipu Sultan**, **Sangolli Rayanna**, **Kittur Rani Chennamma**, **Sindhura Lakshmana**, **Meadows Taylor**, **Kaivara Narayana Taatha**, **Sant Sevalal**, **Shishunala Sharif**.
       - **Legends (*Sthala-Puranas*):** Local traditional narratives associated with religious places, individuals, or heritage reflecting social beliefs.
  - **1.3 Carbon-14 ($\text{C-14}$) Dating Method ("You Should Know" Box):**
    - Applied **only to biological fossils** (trees, birds, plants, animals). Living organisms have equal proportions of $\text{C-14}$ (radioactive carbon) and $\text{C-12}$. After death, $\text{C-12}$ remains constant while **$\text{C-14}$ decays with a half-life of $5,700\text{ years}$**. Comparing $\text{C-14}$ to $\text{C-12}$ estimates the accurate age of fossils ($10,000+\text{ years}$ old).
- **Key Terms, Works & Inscriptions:**
  - *Native vs. Foreign literature*, *Numismatics*, *Excavation*, *Sthala-Puranas*, *C-14 carbon dating (5700 years half-life)*, *Halmidi inscription (Kakutsavarma)*, *Maski & Brahmagiri*, *Allahabad Pillar*, *Aihole*, *Hathigumpha (Kharavela)*, *Uttaramerur*, *Si-Yu-Ki (Hiuen Tsang)*, *Gho-Kho-Ki (Fa-Hien)*, *Indica (Megasthenes)*, *Rajatarangini (Kalhana)*, *Prithviraja Raso (Chand Bardai)*, *Gathasaptashati (Hala)*, *Buddha Charita (Ashwaghosha)*.
- **Mapping & Disambiguation Rules:**
  - **Map here** when a question tests **classification of historical sources** (literary vs. archaeological vs. oral, native vs. foreign literature), **Numismatics**, **$\text{C-14}$ radio-carbon dating**, *Rajatarangini*, *Gho-Kho-Ki*, *Tarikh-i-Firoz Shahi*, *Hathigumpha inscription*, or Roman coins found in Bengaluru.
  - **Disambiguation with Dynasty Chapters (Ch. 16–21):** If a question asks about a specific literary work or inscription *in the context of its dynasty's political achievements or administration* (e.g., details of Ashoka's Kalinga war, Samudragupta's conquests in Allahabad Prashasti, or Kadamba king Mayuravarma), map to the respective dynasty chapter (`ss-8-16` to `ss-8-21`), or accept `ss-8-01-sources` if it is a pure author/book or source-identification question.

### `ss-8-02-bharathavarsha-prehistoric` — Chapter 2: Bharathavarsha: Geographical Features and Prehistoric Period
- **Book Metadata:** Class 8 Social Science Part 1 | Chapter 2 | Book pp. 8–14 (PDF pp. 22–28) | **Sub-Discipline:** History
- **Canonical Topic Aliases:** Geographical Features of India and History, Prehistoric India, Stone Age (Palaeolithic, Mesolithic, Neolithic), Prehistoric Sites in India
- **Core Concepts & Subtopics:**
  - **2.1 Geographical Features of Bharathavarsha (India):**
    - **Names & Political Divisions:** Subcontinent and peninsula in southern Asia; historically called **Bharathavarsha, Bharatha Khanda, Sindhudweepa, Akhandabharatha, Greater India**. Consists of **28 States and 8 Union Territories** (including NCT Delhi).
    - **7 Neighbouring Countries Sharing Land Boundaries:** Pakistan, Afghanistan, China, Nepal, Bhutan, Bangladesh, and Myanmar (plus island neighbour Sri Lanka).
    - **Four Major Physical Divisions & Historical Impact:**
      1. **Himalayan Mountain Range (North):** Preserves safety/security; foreign invasions/intrusions entered mainly from the north-west through the **Bolan and Khyber passes**.
      2. **Indo-Gangetic Plains:** Flat and fertile; cradle of Indus Valley and Vedic civilizations and seat of major empires/battles.
      3. **Deccan Plateau & Central Highlands (Malwa Plateau):** Separated by the **Narmada River** into North India (Malwa/Central Highlands) and South India (Deccan Plateau).
      4. **Coastal Region:** Mainland coastline $= \mathbf{6,100\text{ km}}$; total coastline including islands $= \mathbf{7,516.60\text{ km}}$.
         - **Eastern Coastline:** **Coromandel Coast** and **Utkal Coast**.
         - **Western Coastline:** **Konkan Coast** and **Malabar Coast**.
         - Ancient sea trade with Romans through coastal ports led to the rise of the **Pandyas, Cheras, and Cholas**.
  - **2.2 Classification of History:**
    1. **Pre-historic Period:** Period before the discovery of the art of writing; only archaeological ruins (stone, bone, wood tools) are available — e.g., **Stone Age culture**.
    2. **Proto-historic Period:** Period having written documents/records along with archaeological ruins, **but the script cannot be read/deciphered** — e.g., **Indus Valley (Sindhu-Saraswati) Civilization**.
    3. **Historic Period:** Period having both archaeological ruins and readable written documents — e.g., **starting from the Vedic Civilization**.
  - **2.3 Prehistoric Lifestyle, Sites, Fire, & Environmental Change:**
    - **Prehistoric Sites in India:** **Bhimbetka** (MP), **Hunasagi** (Yadgir district, Karnataka), and **Kurnool caves** (AP) — located near river/lake banks and rocky areas (**residential and industrial sites**).
    - **Awareness of Fire:** **Signs of ashes found in the caves of Kurnool** prove Stone Age people used fire (for cooking food, light, and protection from wild animals).
    - **Cave Drawings:** Found on cave walls/rocks in **Madhya Pradesh, Uttar Pradesh, and Karnataka** depicting wild animals and hunting scenes.
    - **Environmental Shift (~12,000 Years Ago):** Global warming led to the spread of **grasslands** $\to$ multiplication of herbivorous animals (deer, elk, goat, sheep) $\to$ man observed and domesticated them (**beginning of animal husbandry and dairy farming**) and began cultivating **rice, wheat, and barley**.
  - **2.4 Stages of the Stone Age:**
    1. **Old Stone Age (Palaeolithic Age):** Prevailed from **$2\text{ million years ago}$ to $12,000\text{ years ago}$**; subdivided into **Early, Middle, and Late Old Stone Ages**. Used crude stone axes, spears, and scrapers.
    2. **Middle Stone Age (Mesolithic Age):** From **$12,000\text{ years ago}$ to $10,000\text{ years ago}$**. Characterised by **very small, delicate stone weapons (microliths)** fitted with bone/wood handles used as axes and saws.
    3. **New Stone Age (Neolithic Age):** Began **after $10,000\text{ years ago}$**. Tools were **bright, polished, and had sharp blades**; used **grinding stones** for grains/herbs, made **decorated clay pots/pans** for storing and cooking grains, and learnt to **weave cloth**.
- **Key Terms, Sites & Numbers:**
  - *Bharathavarsha*, *Sindhudweepa*, *28 States & 8 UTs*, *Bolan and Khyber passes*, *Narmada river divide*, *6,100 km (mainland coastline)*, *7,516.60 km (total coastline)*, *Coromandel & Utkal coasts*, *Konkan & Malabar coasts*, *Pre-historic vs. Proto-historic vs. Historic*, *Bhimbetka*, *Hunasagi*, *Kurnool caves (traces of ash/fire)*, *Old Stone Age (2 million–12,000 yrs)*, *Middle Stone Age (12,000–10,000 yrs, delicate stone weapons)*, *New Stone Age (after 10,000 yrs, sharp polished blades, pottery, weaving)*.
- **Mapping & Disambiguation Rules:**
  - **Map here** for India's geographical passes (Bolan, Khyber), coastline lengths/names, Pre-historic vs. Proto-historic vs. Historic definitions, Kurnool ash caves, Hunasagi/Bhimbetka Stone Age sites, and Old/Middle/New Stone Age tool characteristics.

### `ss-8-03-sindhu-saraswati-vedic` — Chapter 3: Ancient Indian Civilizations: Sindhu-Saraswati Civilization and Vedic Civilization
- **Book Metadata:** Class 8 Social Science Part 1 | Chapter 3 | Book pp. 15–22 (PDF pp. 29–36) | **Sub-Discipline:** History
- **Canonical Topic Aliases:** Indus Valley Civilization, Harappan Civilization, Sindhu-Saraswati Civilization, Bronze Age in India, Early and Later Vedic Period
- **Core Concepts & Subtopics:**
  - **3.1 Discovery & Naming of the Sindhu-Saraswati (Harappan) Civilization:**
    - Flourished **~5,000 years ago** across the valleys of the **Indus (Sindhu)** and **Saraswati (*Guptagamini*)** rivers (Punjab, Sindh, Rajasthan, Gujarat, Haryana); belongs to the **Bronze Age** (and Proto-historic period).
    - **Timeline of Discovery:**
      - **1826:** **Charles Masson** visited Harappa ruins (mistook it for the site where Alexander defeated Porus). **Alexander Burnes** also visited later.
      - **Alexander Cunningham:** First Director General of the Archaeological Survey of India; conducted small-scale excavations.
      - **1920:** **Dayaram Sahni** excavated **Harappa**.
      - **1921:** **R. D. Banerji** excavated **Mohenjo-daro**.
      - **September 1924:** **Sir John Marshall** (Head of the Indian Archaeology Department) formally announced the discovery of the **Harappan Civilization** to the world in the English newspaper ***Illustrated London News***.
  - **3.2 Pre-Urban Village Settlements:**
    - **Burzahom (Kashmir):** Famous for **underground pit-houses (bunkers)** dug into the ground with steps to protect against harsh cold and wild animals; clay cooking pots found inside and outside pits.
    - **Mehrgarh (near fertile plains of Bolan Pass, on the route to Iran):** **First village settlement** found from this era; people cultivated **barley and wheat**, reared sheep and goats, lived in multi-room rectangular houses ($4+$ rooms), and buried **animals along with humans** in graves.
  - **3.3 Harappan Town Planning & Architecture:**
    - **Two-Part Town Layout:**
      1. **Western Part:** Smaller and elevated — called the **Citadel / Fort**.
      2. **Eastern Part:** Wide and low-lying residential habitat — called the **Lower Town / Village**.
      - Surrounded by walls of **burnt clay bricks** laid in an **interlocking fashion**.
    - **Great Bath at Mohenjo-daro:** Ground-level leak-proof brick tank with stairs on two sides, surrounded by rooms; filled from a nearby well with a drain outlet.
    - **Fire Altars (*Agni Kundas*):** Found at **Kalibangan**, **Lothal**, and **Banawali**.
    - **Granaries:** Well-arranged granaries found at **Harappa, Mohenjo-daro, and Lothal**.
    - **Houses & Covered Underground Drainage:** 1- or 2-storey brick houses with courtyard rooms, private baths, and wells; doors faced the street but **no windows faced the street**. House drains connected to covered brick street drains with inspection holes in cover slabs.
    - **Lothal (Gujarat):** Huge **ship dockyard**; most important centre of **sea trade**.
    - **Dholavira (Gujarat):** Best practices of **rainwater management**.
  - **3.4 Cultural, Artistic, & Economic Life:**
    - **Artefacts & Sculptures:** **Mother Goddess** terracotta idol, **Bronze Dancing Girl** (Mohenjo-daro), **Bearded Man / Torso of a Man**, **Shivalinga** found at **Kalibangan**, **Swastik and Sun seals**, **Pashupati, Bull (*Vrishabha*), Unicorn, and Sacred Fig (*Peepal / Ashvattha*) leaf seals**, idols in **yoga and *namaskara* postures**.
    - **Script:** Undeciphered script on seals called **Picture Script (Pictographic script)**.
    - **Crafts & Metallurgy:** Expert bead-makers (drilled diametrical holes through precious stone beads exported to Mesopotamia), shell and metal bangles, cotton and silk weaving, **bronze alloy** (made by mixing tin with **copper from Baluchistan and Rajasthan**).
    - **Agriculture & Trade:** Primary crops were **wheat, barley, pulses, and cotton**; domesticated humped bull, cow, buffalo, sheep, goat, poultry; traded with Baluchistan, Saurashtra, Deccan, and **Mesopotamia** (Harappan seals and beads found in Mesopotamia).
    - **Decline (~4,000 Years Ago):** Drying up or changing course of rivers, deforestation, or prolonged floods forcing migration eastwards and southwards (persisted longest in **Lothal, Gujarat**).
  - **3.5 Vedic Civilization ($1500\text{ BCE}$ – $700\text{ BCE}$):**
    - Marks the start of the **Historic Period** in India. Word ***Veda*** comes from Sanskrit root ***Vid*** meaning **"knowledge"**.
    - **Classification:**
      1. **Early Vedic / Rigvedic Period:** **Rigveda** is the oldest Veda, containing **$1,028\text{ Suktas}$ (hymns)** organized into **$10\text{ Mandalas}$**. Priests who recited Rigvedic hymns were called ***Hotris***.
      2. **Later Vedic Period:** Period of *Yajurveda, Samaveda,* and *Atharvaveda*.
    - **Comparison of Pre-Vedic (Early Vedic) vs. Post-Vedic (Later Vedic) Society:**
      - **Varna System:** Mentioned in the ***Purushasukta*** in the **10th Mandala of the Rigveda** (4 Varnas: *Brahmana, Kshatriya, Vaishya, Shudra*). In the **Pre-Vedic period**, varna was based on **profession (not birth)**; in the **Post-Vedic period**, caste became hereditary (**determined by birth**) and rigid.
      - **Status of Women:** In the **Pre-Vedic period**, women had **equal status** and **widow remarriage** was practiced. In the **Post-Vedic period**, women lost equal status, widow remarriage was banned, and **dowry, *purdah*, and child marriage** emerged.
      - **Polity:** King was called ***Rajan***, assisted by assemblies ***Sabha, Samiti,* and *Vidatha*** in the Pre-Vedic period. In the Post-Vedic period, ***Vidatha* disappeared** and complex *Yajnas* and *Yagas* grew.
      - **Economy:** Shifted from **animal husbandry and farming** (Pre-Vedic) to **agriculture as the primary occupation** (Post-Vedic).
- **Key Terms, Sites & Discoverers:**
  - *Dayaram Sahni (1920, Harappa)*, *R.D. Banerji (1921, Mohenjo-daro)*, *Sir John Marshall (1924, Illustrated London News)*, *Charles Masson (1826)*, *Alexander Cunningham*, *Guptagamini (Saraswati)*, *Burzahom (pit houses, Kashmir)*, *Mehrgarh (Bolan pass, first village)*, *Great Bath (Mohenjo-daro)*, *Kalibangan (fire altars, Shivalinga)*, *Banawali (fire altar)*, *Lothal (dockyard, sea trade, fire altars, granary)*, *Dholavira (rainwater management)*, *Picture Script*, *Rigveda (1028 Suktas, 10 Mandalas, Hotris, Purushasukta in 10th Mandala)*, *Rajan*, *Sabha, Samiti, Vidatha*.
- **Mapping & Disambiguation Rules:**
  - **Map here** for all Sindhu-Saraswati / Harappan civilization questions (excavators, town planning, Great Bath, Lothal dockyard, Dholavira rainwater management, Kalibangan, Burzahom, Mehrgarh) and Vedic social/political/economic life (*Rigveda* 1028 suktas, *Hotris*, *Purushasukta*, *Sabha/Samiti/Vidatha*).
  - **Disambiguation with `ss-8-05-sanatana-dharma`:** If a question asks about the **philosophical structure of the Vedas** (the 4 *Skandhas*: *Samhita, Brahmana, Aranyaka, Upanishad*), the **6 *Vedangas***, **18 *Smritis***, **6 *Darshanas***, or ***Agama* traditions**, map to `ss-8-05-sanatana-dharma`.

### `ss-8-04-prominent-world-civilizations` — Chapter 4: Prominent Civilizations of the World
- **Book Metadata:** Class 8 Social Science Part 1 | Chapter 4 | Book pp. 23–39 (PDF pp. 37–53) | **Sub-Discipline:** History (World History)
- **Canonical Topic Aliases:** Ancient World Civilizations, Egyptian Civilization, Mesopotamian Civilization, Chinese Civilization, Greek and Roman Civilizations, Maya Aztec and Inca Civilizations
- **Core Concepts & Subtopics:**
  - **4.1 The Egyptian Civilization (Nile River Valley):**
    - **Gift of the Nile:** Annual flooding left fertile clay soil; developed complex irrigation via canals and wells.
    - **Contributions:** Invented the **solar calendar**, mastered **Hieroglyphics** ("sacred writings" — symbolic picture script), built grand **Pyramids** (towers over desert royal tombs) and temples.
    - **Religion & Afterlife:** Worshipped **Osiris** (God of Life after Death); preserved dead bodies smeared with chemicals and wrapped in thin cloth as **Mummies** inside coffins with daily-use items and dolls of cooks/barbers. Kings were called **Pharaohs** ("a person who lives in a palatial house").
    - **Political History & Foreign Rule:** Invaded by the **Hyksos** (Arabian shepherd tribe who ruled for 500 years, aided by wandering Jews in tax collection) $\to$ driven out by the revolt of **Thebes ($1700\text{ BCE}$)** $\to$ conquered by **Sardanapalus of Assyria** $\to$ **Persian Emperor Cambyses** ($6\text{th century BCE}$) $\to$ **Alexander the Great** ($4\text{th century BCE}$), whose general founded the **Ptolemy dynasty** and the city of **Alexandria** $\to$ last queen **Cleopatra** defeated by Caesar's nephew **Augustus (Octavius)** in **$30\text{ BCE}$** (Cleopatra consumed poison; Egypt became a Roman province).
  - **4.2 The Mesopotamian Civilization (Euphrates and Tigris Valley):**
    - **Geography:** Greek word *Mesopotamia* means **"Land between the rivers"** (**Euphrates** and **Tigris**). Southern part was **Babylonia** (divided into northern **Akkad** and southern **Sumer**); northern Mesopotamia was **Assyria**.
    - **Succession of Rulers & Tribes:**
      1. **Sumerians ($4000\text{ BCE}$):** First to evolve **Cuneiform** writing and build artificial hill-temples called **Ziggurats** (called **Towers of Babel** by the Jews).
      2. **Akkadians:** Semitic tribe of the Arabian desert who overpowered Sumerians.
      3. **Amorites:** Semitic tribe whose greatest king **Hammurabi** built a palace in Babylon and promulgated the famous **Law Codes of Hammurabi** (inscribed on a black stone/rock edict found in 1901, now kept in **The Louvre Museum, Paris**; based on *"An eye for an eye and a tooth for a tooth"*, protecting women and slaves).
      4. **Hittites** $\to$ defeated by **Assyrians** (followers of desert god **Ashura**).
      5. **Chaldeans ($7\text{th century BCE}$):** Re-established Babylonia; greatest king **Nebuchadnezzar II** built the **Hanging Gardens of Babylonia** (one of the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World, terraced trees/bushes on Ziggurats watered from the Euphrates to console his Median wife **Amitis**; some scholars attribute it to Assyrian queen **Sammu-Ramat**).
      6. Conquered by Persians ($6\text{th century BCE}$) $\to$ Alexander $\to$ Romans and Turks.
  - **4.3 The Chinese Civilization (Hwang Ho River Valley):**
    - **Hwang Ho River (Yellow River):** Called **"China's Sorrow" / "Symbol of Sorrow"** because its unpredictable floods changed course, inundated farmlands/houses, and ruined canals.
    - **Dynasties:**
      - **Shang Dynasty ($18\text{th}$–$12\text{th century BCE}$):** First ruling dynasty according to excavations; bronze helmets, metal armour, bronze daggers/vessels, rice and silk cultivation.
      - **Chau (Zhou) Dynasty (ruled till $250\text{ BCE}$):** Feudal hierarchy — King $\to$ land-holding officials (who supplied troops in war) $\to$ merchants and craftsmen $\to$ peasants $\to$ slaves (prisoners of war).
      - **Qin Dynasty — Great Wall of China:** Begun in the $7\text{th century BCE}$ by smaller states and connected by **Emperor Qin Shihuangti** (continued till the $16\text{th century CE}$, stretching **$> 5,000\text{ km}$**) to ward off northern invaders.
      - **Han Dynasty ($200\text{ BCE}$):** Developed **glazed porcelain** (semi-porcelain used since $1600\text{ BCE}$).
    - **Culture:** Ancestor worship, silk manufacturing (sericulture), **ideographic script** written on silk and bamboo slips, porcelain, and the Great Wall.
  - **4.4 Greek Civilization:**

    - **Alexander the Great:** King of **Macedonia**, student of the great philosopher **Aristotle**; defeated Persia, marched up to Indian borders, and died at **Babylonia in $323\text{ BCE}$**.

    - **Origins & Political Institutions:** Settled by migrants from North Africa, Spain, France, Greeks, and **Etruscans**. Under the monarchy and later the **Roman Republic** (established in the **$6\text{th century BCE}$**), Rome had two advisory bodies: the **Assembly** (all middle-aged men) and the elite **Senate** (controlled by Patricians, with power to reject proposals).
    - **Social Classes:**
      1. **Patricians:** Elite upper-class landowners who controlled the Senate.
      2. **Plebeians:** Common workers, small farmers, artisans, traders, and soldiers; after rebelling in the **$5\text{th century BCE}$**, they won the right to elect **Tribunes** (who could veto Senate/Council decisions) and established rule-based **Roman Law**.
      3. **Slaves:** Property of owners without citizenship; used as **Gladiators** to fight humans/animals in the **Amphitheatre** and **Colosseum**. In the $1\text{st century BCE}$, **$60,000\text{ slaves}$ rebelled under Spartacus** (crushed after 1 year; $60,000$ crucified).
    - **Punic Wars ($118\text{ years}$):** Rome fought and defeated **Carthage** (coastal town in North Africa) for control of fertile **Sicily**.
    - **Fall of the Republic:** Rivalry between generals **Pompey** and **Julius Caesar** $\to$ Caesar defeated Pompey and became dictator $\to$ assassinated in the Senate by **Brutus** and associates $\to$ Caesar's nephew **Octavius** took power under the title **Augustus** and **Princep** ("The First Citizen of the State"), ruling for $44\text{ years}$ and ending the Republic.
    - **Contributions:** **Latin language**, **Pantheon** (temple), **Colosseum** (auditorium), realistic sculpture.
  - **4.6 Ancient Columbian Civilizations of America (New World):**
    1. **Maya Civilization:** American-Indian aborigines of **Yucatan (Mexico), Guatemala, and Honduras**; spoke **Yucatec**; earliest stone cities built around **$320\text{ CE}$**; destroyed by Spanish attacks ($1527\text{–}1546\text{ CE}$) under **Hernan Cortes**.
    2. **Aztec Civilization (Mexico):** Built their capital city **Tenochtitlan** ("the stone rising in the water", also called **Mexica**) on an island in **Lake Texcoco**. Called **Tenochca** (after ancestor **Tenoch**); spoke **Nahua**; oral literature translated by Spanish scholars as **Indian Codices**; conquered by the Spanish army in **$1519\text{ CE}$**.
    3. **Inca Civilization ($15\text{th}$–$16\text{th centuries CE}$):** Spread across **Peru, Ecuador, Chile, and Argentina**. Famous buildings: **Palace on the island in Lake Titicaca** and the **Moon Temple**. Mastered gold jewellery/metalwork. Worshipped **Veerakocha (Viracocha)** as Creator God and the **Sun God** as the link between people and Veerakocha (built Sun Temples in all centres).

      2. ***Chandas*** (Prosody / Metre)
      3. ***Vyakarana*** (Grammar)
      4. ***Nirukta*** (Etymology)
      5. ***Jyotishya*** (Astrology / Astronomy)
      6. ***Kalpa*** (Religious practices / Rituals).
    - ***Itihasa* and *Puranas*:** Two great epics (**Ramayana** and **Mahabharata**), **$18\text{ Mahapuranas}$**, and **$18\text{ Upapuranas}$**.

    - *Brahman* is viewed dualistically as ***Saguna*** (with name/*Nama* and form/*Rupa*) and ***Nirguna*** (formless).

    - **24 Tirthankaras:** **Rishabhanatha** ($1\text{st}$ Tirthankara); **Parshwanatha** ($23\text{rd}$ Tirthankara, son of King Ashwasena in the $8\text{th century BCE}$, who preached **4 vows**: *Non-violence, Truth, Asteya [not to steal], Aparigraha [not to own property]*); **Vardhamana Mahaveera** ($24\text{th}$ Tirthankara, who added the $5\text{th}$ vow: ***Brahmacharya*** [chastity/celibacy]).

Renounced home at age $30$, meditated for $12\text{ years}$, attained enlightenment (***Kaivalya***) at age $42$, becoming ***Jina*** ("conqueror of senses") and ***Mahaveera***. Attained *Nirvana* at age $72$ at **Pavapuri (Bihar)**.
    - **Teachings:**
      - **Five Vows (*Pancha Mahavratas*):** 1. *Ahimsa* (Non-violence), 2. *Satya* (Truth), 3. *Asteya* (Non-stealing), 4. *Aparigraha* (Non-possession), 5. *Brahmacharya*.

    - **Sects & Literature:**
      - **Swethambaras** (wear white clothes) and **Digambaras** (wear no clothes).
      - Preachings compiled after the First Jain Council into **12 volumes called *12 Angas* in Prakrit language**.
      - Migration to **Shravanabelagola** (Karnataka) during a famine in Bihar; inspired great Kannada Jain poets (**Pampa, Ranna, Janna, Ponna**).
  - **6.3 Buddhism & Gautama Buddha ($6\text{th Century BCE}$):**
    - **Life of Buddha:** Original name **Siddhartha**, born in the $6\text{th century BCE}$ as son of **Shuddhodana** (king of the **Shakya** republic). Renounced royal luxury, attained enlightenment to become **Buddha** ("The Enlightened One").
    - **First Sermon (*Dharma Chakra Pravartana*):** Delivered at the **Deer Park in Sarnath** near Banaras.
    - **Core Teachings:**
      - **Four Noble Truths:** (1) The world is full of sorrow and suffering; (2) **Desire** is the root cause of sorrow; (3) Conquering excessive desire leads to peace (*Nirvana*); (4) Desire is eliminated by following the **Eightfold Path**.
      - **Eightfold Path (*Ashtangika Marga* / The Middle Path):** 1. Right Action, 2. Right Speech, 3. Right Vision, 4. Right Livelihood, 5. Right Effort, 6. Right Recollection (Mindfulness), 7. Right Resolve, 8. Right Thought.
    - **Death, Literature, Sects, & Spread:**
      - Passed away at age $80$ at **Kushinagar** (called ***Mahaparinirvana***); relics preserved in ***Chaityalayas* and *Stupas*** (e.g., Sanchi Stupa) and monks lived in ***Viharas***.
      - **Tripitakas:** Teachings compiled into three baskets — ***Vinaya Pitaka*, *Dhamma (Sutta) Pitaka*, and *Abhidhamma Pitaka***.
      - **Branches:** **Hinayana, Mahayana, and Vajrayana**.
      - Spread to Sri Lanka, Burma, Malaya, Thailand, Vietnam, Cambodia (**Angkor Wat**), and Afghanistan (**Bamiyan**). Visited by Chinese travellers **Fa-Hien, I-Tsing (It Sing), and Hiuen Tsang**. In **1956**, **Dr. B. R. Ambedkar** embraced Buddhism.
  - **6.4 Foreign Invasions (Persian & Macedonian):**
    - **Persian Invasion:** **King Darius** of the **Achaemenian** dynasty ($6\text{th century BCE}$) extended the Persian empire up to the Indian border.
    - **Alexander's Invasion ($4\text{th century BCE}$):** Macedonian king **Alexander** defeated Persia, entered India via Kandahar and Hindu Kush passes, accepted the invitation of **Ambhi (King of Taxila)**, and fought **King Paurava (Porus)** on the banks of the flooded **River Jhelum** (Hydaspes). When asked how he wished to be treated, Paurava famously replied: ***"As one king deserves to be treated by another."*** Alexander advanced up to the Ravi and Beas rivers, turned back, and died at **Babylonia**. His invasion paved the way for Mauryan unification and **Gandhara art**.

  - *Shramanic tradition*, *Rishabhanatha (1st)*, *Parshwanatha (23rd, Ashwasena)*, *Vardhamana Mahaveera (24th, 599–527 BCE, Kundagrama in Vaishali, Siddhartha, Trishaladevi, Jnatrika, Kaivalya at 42, Jina, Pavapuri at 72)*, *5 Vows (Ahimsa, Satya, Asteya, Aparigraha, Brahmacharya)*, *Triratnas (Samyak Gyan, Samyak Darshan, Samyak Charitra)*, *Sallekhana*, *Swethambaras & Digambaras*, *12 Angas (Prakrit)*, *Siddhartha / Gautama Buddha (Shuddhodana, Shakya)*, *Sarnath (Dharma Chakra Pravartana)*, *Four Noble Truths*, *Eightfold Path (Middle Path)*, *Kushinagar (Mahaparinirvana)*, *Tripitakas (Vinaya, Dhamma, Abhidhamma)*, *Hinayana, Mahayana, Vajrayana*, *Darius (Achaemenian)*, *Alexander, Ambhi (Taxila), Paurava/Porus (Battle of Jhelum)*.

### `ss-8-08-citizen-citizenship` — Chapter 8: Citizen and Citizenship
- **Book Metadata:** Class 8 Social Science Part 1 | Chapter 8 | Book pp. 56–63 (PDF pp. 70–77) | **Sub-Discipline:** Political Science (Civics & Constitution)
- **Canonical Topic Aliases:** Citizenship in India, Citizen vs. Alien, Citizenship Act 1955, Acquisition and Loss of Citizenship, Single vs. Dual Citizenship, Fundamental Duties (Article 51A)
- **Core Concepts & Subtopics:**
  - **8.1 Meaning of Citizen & Citizenship:**
    - **Ancient Greece:** Narrow privilege restricted to a few who directly participated in administration/justice; women, manual workers, and slaves were denied citizenship.
    - **Modern Meaning & Vattel's Definition:** A permanent, full-fledged member of a state who enjoys **civil, political, and economic rights**, owes allegiance to the state, and performs duties towards it.
    - **Subjects vs. Citizens:** Before $1947$ Indians were **subjects** of the British Crown; after Independence we became **citizens** of India.
  - **8.2 Citizen vs. Alien (Foreigner):**
    - **Citizens:** Permanent residents; enjoy **both civil and political rights** (right to vote, contest elections, hold high constitutional offices like President, Vice-President, Supreme/High Court Judge); owe allegiance to the state; cannot be driven out of their country.
    - **Aliens:** Temporary residents or tourists who are citizens of another state; enjoy only civil rights and **no political rights**; can be deported/asked to leave upon visa expiry or illegal activities.
  - **8.3 General Methods of Acquiring Citizenship:**
    1. **Natural Citizenship:** By birth.
    2. **Naturalised Citizenship:** An alien acquires citizenship after fulfilling legal conditions — (a) **Domicile/Residence** (e.g., $5\text{ years}$ in England and USA; $10\text{ years}$ in France), (b) **Marriage** (in Japan even a foreign husband gets citizenship), (c) **Foreign Government Service**, (d) **Application**, (e) **Purchasing Property** (e.g., Mexico).
  - **8.4 Acquisition & Loss of Citizenship in India (*Citizenship Act, 1955*):**
    - **Exclusive Authority:** **Parliament of India** is the sole authority to legislate on citizenship (State Legislatures have no power). Parliament enacted the **Citizenship Act in 1955** (amended in **1986** and **2019**).
    - **5 Modes of Acquiring Indian Citizenship (Citizenship Act, 1955):**
      1. **By Birth:** Born in India on or after **26 January 1950**.
      2. **By Descent:** Born outside India on or after 26 January 1950 if either parent is an Indian citizen at the time of birth.
      3. **By Registration:** Applying and registering as per constitutional/statutory rules.
      4. **By Naturalisation:** Foreigners applying to the President of India after residing in India for **not less than 7 years**.
      5. **By Incorporation of a New Territory:** When a new territory joins India, its people become Indian citizens by Government of India order.
    - **3 Modes of Losing Indian Citizenship:**
      1. **Renunciation:** Voluntarily giving up Indian citizenship by submitting an application.
      2. **Termination:** Automatic termination by law when an Indian citizen voluntarily acquires the citizenship of another country.
      3. **Deprivation:** Forced termination by the Government of India if citizenship was obtained by fraud, disloyalty to the Constitution, or anti-national activities.
  - **8.5 Types of Citizenship (Single vs. Dual):**
    - **Single Citizenship:** Only one national citizenship across the entire country regardless of state/province — practiced in **India**.
    - **Dual Citizenship:** Separate national citizenship and state citizenship in a federal system — practiced in the **USA** (e.g., citizen of New Jersey + citizen of the USA).
  - **8.6 Duties of a Citizen & Fundamental Duties:**
    - **42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976:** Incorporated **Fundamental Duties** into the Indian Constitution under **Article 51A**.
- **Key Terms, Acts & Articles:**
  - *Citizen vs. Subject vs. Alien*, *Vattel*, *Natural vs. Naturalised citizenship*, *Parliament of India (sole authority)*, *Citizenship Act 1955 (amended 1986, 2019)*, *5 modes of acquisition (Birth [26 Jan 1950], Descent, Registration, Naturalisation [7 years residence], Incorporation of territory)*, *3 modes of loss (Renunciation, Termination, Deprivation)*, *Single citizenship (India) vs. Dual citizenship (USA)*, *42nd Amendment Act 1976*, *Article 51A (Fundamental Duties)*.

### `ss-8-11-earth-our-living-planet` — Chapter 11: The Earth – Our Living Planet

    - **Relative Diameter:** Earth's diameter is $\approx \mathbf{4\text{ times}}$ greater than the Moon's and $\approx \mathbf{107\text{ times}}$ smaller than the Sun's.
    - **Shape (*Geoid* / Oblate Spheroid):** Flattened at the poles and bulged at the equator:

    - **Total Geographical Area of Earth:** $\mathbf{510\text{ million sq. km}}$:
      - **Water (Hydrosphere):** $\mathbf{361\text{ million sq. km}}$ ($\mathbf{70.78\%}$)
      - **Land (Lithosphere):** $\mathbf{149\text{ million sq. km}}$ ($\mathbf{29.22\%}$)
      - **Land : Water Ratio:** $\mathbf{1 : 2.43}$.
    - **Hemispheric Distribution:**
      - **Northern Hemisphere ("Land Hemisphere"):** $\mathbf{60\%\text{ land}}$ and $\mathbf{40\%\text{ water}}$.

  - **11.3 Latitudes and Longitudes (*Grid / Graticule*):**
    - Network of horizontal **parallels of latitude** and vertical **meridians of longitude** intersecting at right angles is called a **Grid or Graticule**.
    - **Latitudes (Parallels of Latitude):**
      - Imaginary circles parallel to the Equator measuring angular distance north or south of the Equator ($0^\circ$ to $90^\circ\text{ N}$ and $0^\circ$ to $90^\circ\text{ S}$).
      - **Total Latitudes:** $90\text{ N} + 90\text{ S} + 1\text{ Equator} = \mathbf{181\text{ latitudes}}$ ($90^\circ\text{ N}$ and $90^\circ\text{ S}$ are points).
      - **Ground Distance Between $1^\circ$ of Latitude:** $\mathbf{110.4\text{ km}}$.
      - **7 Important Latitudes:**
        1. $0^\circ$ — **Equator (Great Circle)** (longest latitude, equal to Earth's circumference)
        2. $23^\circ 30'\text{ N}$ — **Tropic of Cancer**
        3. $23^\circ 30'\text{ S}$ — **Tropic of Capricorn**
        4. $66^\circ 30'\text{ N}$ — **Arctic Circle**
        5. $66^\circ 30'\text{ S}$ — **Antarctic Circle**
        6. $90^\circ\text{ N}$ — **North Pole**
        7. $90^\circ\text{ S}$ — **South Pole**.
    - **Longitudes (Meridians of Longitude):**
      - Imaginary semi-circles running from Pole to Pole intersecting the Equator at right angles. All longitudes are of **equal length**. Called **Meridians** (*meri* $=$ mid, *dian* $=$ day) because all places on the same meridian experience noon at the same time.
      - **Prime Meridian ($0^\circ$ Longitude):** Passes through **Greenwich (England)**. Divides Earth into $180^\circ\text{ E}$ (Eastern Hemisphere) and $180^\circ\text{ W}$ (Western Hemisphere) $\to$ total **$\mathbf{360\text{ longitudes}}$**.
      - **Ground Distance Between Two Consecutive Longitudes at the Equator:** $\mathbf{111\text{ km}}$ (decreases towards the poles and becomes zero at the poles).
  - **11.4 Longitude & Time, Standard Time (IST), Time Zones, & International Date Line (IDL):**
    - **Time Calculation:** Earth rotates $360^\circ$ in $24\text{ hours}$ ($1,440\text{ minutes}$):
      $$1^\circ\text{ longitude} = \mathbf{4\text{ minutes}} \quad \mid \quad 15^\circ\text{ longitude} = \mathbf{60\text{ minutes (1 hour)}}$$
      - **E.G.A. Rule (East – Gain – Add):** Add time when moving **East** of GMT.
      - **W.L.S. Rule (West – Lose – Subtract):** Subtract time when moving **West** of GMT.
    - **Local Time vs. Standard Time:**
      - **Local Time:** Time according to the overhead Sun ($12\text{ noon}$) on the local meridian of a place.
      - **Indian Standard Time (IST):** Based on **$82^\circ 30'\text{ E}$ longitude**, passing through **Prayagraj (Allahabad) in Uttar Pradesh**. IST is **$5\text{ hours }30\text{ minutes}$ ahead of GMT** ($+5:30\text{ GMT}$).
    - **Time Zones:** The globe is divided into **$24\text{ Time Zones}$** ($15^\circ$ / $1\text{ hour}$ each). Countries with vast longitudinal extent have multiple time zones: **Russia ($11\text{ time zones}$)**, **USA ($5$)**, **Canada ($5$)**, **Australia ($3$)**.
    - **International Date Line (IDL):** Passes through the **Pacific Ocean along the $180^\circ$ meridian** (diametrically opposite $0^\circ$ GMT, with zig-zag detours to avoid land masses).
      - Crossing IDL from **West to East** (Asia to North America) $\to$ **gains a day / repeats a day twice**.
      - Crossing IDL from **East to West** (North America to Asia) $\to$ **loses one day**.

### `ss-8-12-lithosphere` — Chapter 12: Lithosphere
- **Book Metadata:** Class 8 Social Science Part 1 | Chapter 12 | Book pp. 79–95 (PDF pp. 93–109) | **Sub-Discipline:** Geography (Geomorphology)
- **Canonical Topic Aliases:** Interior of the Earth, Rocks and Rock Cycle, Endogenic Forces (Volcanoes and Earthquakes), Exogenic Forces (Weathering and Denudation), Landforms by Rivers Glaciers Underground Water Wind and Sea Waves
- **Core Concepts & Subtopics:**
  - **12.1 Earth's Interior & Composition ($4.6\text{ Billion Years Old}$):**
    - Temperature increases with depth at **$1^\circ\text{C}$ for every $32\text{ metres}$**.
    - **Deepest Mines/Boreholes in the World:** (1) **Kola Superdeep Borehole** (near Murmansk, Russia — **$12.262\text{ km}$**, deepest artificial hole), (2) **TauTona Gold Mine** (South Africa — **$3.9\text{ km}$**), (3) **Champion Reef Gold Mine at KGF** (Karnataka, India — **$3.2\text{ km}$**).
    - **Three Concentric Layers of the Earth:**
      1. **The Crust (Lithosphere, $0$ to $\sim 60\text{ km}$ depth):**
         - **Upper / Continental Crust (*SIAL*):** Rich in **Silica and Aluminium** (lighter materials).
         - **Lower / Oceanic Crust (*SIMA*):** Rich in **Silica and Magnesium** (denser).
      2. **The Mantle ($\sim 60\text{ km}$ to $2,900\text{ km}$ depth):** Composed of magnesium and iron; contains magma.
         - **Upper Mantle (*Asthenosphere*):** Partially molten / semi-liquid condition.
         - **Lower Mantle (*Mesosphere*):** Solid condition.
      3. **The Core (*NIFE*, $2,900\text{ km}$ to $6,371\text{ km}$ depth):** Composed of **Nickel ($\text{Ni}$) and Ferrous/Iron ($\text{Fe}$)**.
         - **Outer Core:** Liquid / molten form.
         - **Inner Core:** Solid form.
    - **Seismic Discontinuities:**
      - **Mohorovicic Discontinuity (Moho):** Boundary separating the **Crust and the Mantle**.
      - **Gutenberg Discontinuity:** Boundary separating the **Mantle and the Core**.
  - **12.2 Rocks and Classification of Rocks:**
    1. **Igneous Rocks (Primary Rocks):** Derived from Latin *Ignis* or Sanskrit *Agni* ("fire"); formed by cooling and solidification of molten **magma** (inside earth) or **lava** (on surface).
       - **Intrusive Igneous Rocks:** Slow cooling of magma deep below the surface forming large crystals — e.g., **Granite, Diorite, Gabbro**.
       - **Extrusive Igneous Rocks:** Rapid cooling of lava on the surface forming fine-grained/glassy rocks — e.g., **Basalt, Andesite**.
    2. **Sedimentary Rocks (Secondary / Stratified / Aqueous Rocks):** Derived from Latin *Sedimentum* ("settling down"); formed in layers/strata in water bodies.
       - **Mechanically Formed:** **Sandstone** (*Arenaceous* rock) and **Shale** (*Argillaceous* rock).
       - **Chemically Formed (by evaporation):** **Rock Salt** and **Gypsum**.
       - **Organically Formed:** **Limestone** (*Calcareous* rock — from marine shells) and **Coal** (*Carbonaceous* rock — from plant remains).
    3. **Metamorphic Rocks:** Hardest rocks, formed by alteration of pre-existing Igneous/Sedimentary rocks under **heat and pressure**; source of precious stones (Sapphire, Ruby, Emerald, Diamond).
       - **Key Transformations:**
         $$\text{Granite} \to \mathbf{Gneiss} \quad \mid \quad \text{Basalt} \to \mathbf{Schist} \quad \mid \quad \text{Limestone} \to \mathbf{Marble}$$
         $$\text{Sandstone} \to \mathbf{Quartzite} \quad \mid \quad \text{Coal} \to \mathbf{Graphite} \to \mathbf{Diamond}$$
  - **12.3 Endogenic (Internal) Forces — Volcanoes & Earthquakes:**
    - **Volcanoes:**
      - **Parts:** **Vent** (narrow pipe/passage), **Crater** (funnel-shaped hollow at the top), **Caldera** (large steep-sided basin-shaped crater).
      - **3 Types by Frequency of Eruption:**
        1. **Active Volcanoes ($\sim 1,350$ worldwide):** **Mt. Stromboli** and **Mt. Etna** (Italy), **Mt. St. Helens** (USA), **Mauna Loa** (Hawaii), **Mt. Pinatubo** (Philippines).
        2. **Dormant Volcanoes:** **Mt. Vesuvius** (Italy), **Mt. Fujiyama** (Japan), **Mt. Kilimanjaro** (Tanzania), **Mt. Krakatoa** (Indonesia). *(Note: Mt. Pelee in West Indies is also listed under super eruptions).*
        3. **Extinct Volcanoes:** **Mt. Aconcagua** (Argentina), **Ngorongoro / Gorongoro** (Tanzania), **Arthur's Seat** (Scotland).
      - **Volcanic Materials:** *Solid:* Volcanic bombs, cinders, scoria, pumice, dust, ash; *Liquid:* Lava; *Gases:* Sulphur, Hydrogen, Carbon dioxide.
      - **Volcanic Belts:** (1) **Circum-Pacific Belt ("Pacific Ring of Fire")**, (2) **Mid-Atlantic Belt** (Iceland, West Indies), (3) **Mid-Continental Belt** (Italy, Spain, Greece, Turkey).
    - **Earthquakes:**
      - **Terminology:** **Seismic Focus / Hypocentre** (point of origin inside crust/mantle), **Epicentre** (point on surface vertically above the focus), **Seismology** (scientific study), **Seismograph** (instrument recording origin, time, velocity, and direction of waves), **Richter Scale** (measures magnitude and intensity).
      - **Causes:** Plate tectonics, volcanic eruptions (Krakatoa), faulting (**San Andreas Fault** in California, USA), and man-made factors like deep mining, nuclear tests, and huge reservoirs (**Koyna Dam** in Maharashtra, **Hoover Dam** in USA).
      - **3 Types of Earthquake Waves:**
        1. **Primary Waves (PW / Longitudinal / Compressional Waves):** **Fastest** waves, first to reach the surface; pass through **solids, liquids, and gases**.
        2. **Secondary Waves (SW / Transverse / Distortional Waves):** Reach epicentre after P-waves; **cannot pass through liquids**.
        3. **Surface Waves (LW / Long Waves):** **Slowest** waves, but cause the **highest destruction** on the Earth's surface.
      - **Tsunami:** Japanese word meaning **"harbour waves"**; huge sea waves caused by underwater earthquakes (e.g., **26 Dec 2004 Indian Ocean Tsunami**; **11 March 2011 Japan Tsunami** at Fukushima/Sendai/Miyagi).
      - **Seismic Recording Centres in India:** **Gauribidanur** (Karnataka), **Kodaikanal**, **Pune**, **Hyderabad**, and **Dehradun**.
  - **12.4 Exogenic (External) Forces — Weathering & Denudation:**
    - **Weathering (In-situ disintegration of rocks):**
      1. **Mechanical (Physical) Weathering:** Granular disintegration, Block disintegration, and **Exfoliation** (by temperature, wind, frost).
      2. **Chemical Weathering:** **Oxidation** (oxygen + iron $\to$ rust/oxides), **Carbonation** ($\text{CO}_2$ in rainwater dissolves limestone $\to$ calcium bicarbonate), **Hydration** (minerals like **feldspar** absorb water and turn into powdery mass), and **Solution**.
      3. **Biological Weathering:** Plant roots, burrowing animals (earthworms, rabbits, rats), and human mining/quarrying.
    - **Agents of Denudation (Erosion, Transportation, & Deposition) & Landforms:**
      1. **River (*Fluvial Cycle*):** Source, Mouth, Tributaries, **Confluence** (meeting point), **Estuary** (tidal mouth broadening into sea) vs. **Delta** (fan-shaped deposit at mouth).
         - **Upper Course (Vertical Erosion):** **Gorges, Canyons, 'V'-shaped valleys, Waterfalls, Rapids** (Angel Falls, Niagara Falls, Jog Falls).
         - **Middle Course (Transportation):** **Alluvial fans, Meanders**.
         - **Lower Course (Deposition):** **Flood plains, Natural levees, Ox-bow lakes, Deltas** (Sundarbans, Nile).
      2. **Glacier (*Glacial Cycle*):** **Continental glaciers** (Greenland, Antarctica) and **Mountain/Alpine/Valley glaciers**.

      3. **Underground Water (*Karst Topography* in Limestone Regions):**
         - Pervious/porous rocks vs. impervious rocks; water-bearing beds are **Aquifers**.
         - **Springs & Wells:** Perennial springs, Intermittent/Periodic springs, Hot/Thermal springs, **Geysers** (fountain of hot water and steam — e.g., **Old Faithful in Yellowstone National Park, USA**), and **Artesian Wells** (water trapped in basin between two non-porous layers gushes out when bored — common in **Australia**).
         - **Karst Landforms:** **Lapies, Sinkholes, Limestone caves** (**Akalagavi/Ulavi caves** in Uttara Kannada, Karnataka; **Belum and Borra caves** in AP), **Stalactites, Stalagmites, Calcite pillars**.
      4. **Wind (*Aeolian Cycle* in Arid/Desert Regions):**
         - **Erosional Landforms:** **Rock pedestals, Mushroom rock, Inselberg**.
         - **Depositional Landforms:** **Sand dunes** (Longitudinal dunes and crescent-shaped **Barchans**) and **Loess** (yellow silt/soil deposited beyond desert borders, e.g., **Loess Plain in China**).
      5. **Sea Waves (Coastal Landforms):** **Cliff, Sea cave, Sea stack, Sea arch, Headland, Sand bars, Beaches, Lagoons**.

  - *SIAL, SIMA, NIFE*, *Asthenosphere, Mesosphere*, *Mohorovicic (Moho) & Gutenberg discontinuities*, *Kola borehole (12.262 km), TauTona (3.9 km), Champion Reef KGF (3.2 km)*, *Igneous (Granite, Gabbro, Diorite vs. Basalt, Andesite)*, *Sedimentary (Arenaceous/Sandstone, Argillaceous/Shale, Calcareous/Limestone, Carbonaceous/Coal, Rock salt, Gypsum)*, *Metamorphic (Gneiss, Schist, Marble, Quartzite, Graphite, Diamond)*, *Vent, Crater, Caldera*, *Active/Dormant/Extinct volcanoes*, *Hypocentre/Focus vs. Epicentre*, *Seismograph vs. Richter scale*, *P, S, and L waves*, *Gauribidanur*, *Oxidation, Carbonation, Hydration, Solution*, *Fluvial cycle (V-valley, Meander, Ox-bow lake, Levee, Delta)*, *Glacial cycle (Cirque, Horn, Arete, U-valley, Hanging valley, Moraines, Drumlin, Esker, Kame)*, *Karst topography (Stalactite, Stalagmite, Lapies, Akalagavi, Borra/Belum, Old Faithful Geyser, Artesian well)*, *Aeolian cycle (Mushroom rock, Inselberg, Barchans, Loess)*.

### `ss-8-13-atmosphere` — Chapter 13: Atmosphere
- **Book Metadata:** Class 8 Social Science Part 1 | Chapter 13 | Book pp. 96–108 (PDF pp. 110–122) | **Sub-Discipline:** Geography (Climatology & Meteorology)
- **Canonical Topic Aliases:** Composition and Layers of the Atmosphere, Temperature Zones and Inversion, Atmospheric Pressure Belts and Planetary/Local Winds, Humidity Clouds and Types of Rainfall, Weather vs. Climate
- **Core Concepts & Subtopics:**
  - **13.1 Meaning, Composition, & Five Layers of the Atmosphere ($\sim 1,000\text{ km}$ thick):**
    - **Gas Composition:** **Nitrogen ($\mathbf{78.08\%}$)**, **Oxygen ($\mathbf{20.94\%}$)**, **Argon ($\mathbf{0.93\%}$)**, **Carbon dioxide ($\mathbf{0.03\%}$)**, **Ozone ($\mathbf{0.000005\%}$)**, plus water vapour and dust particles (condensation nuclei).
    - **Five Concentric Layers of the Atmosphere:**
      1. **Troposphere (Lowest layer):** Extends up to **$18\text{ km}$ at the Equator** and **$8\text{ km}$ near the Poles**. Contains all weather elements (clouds, winds, rain); **all weather changes occur here**; temperature and pressure decrease with altitude.
      2. **Stratosphere ($18\text{–}50\text{ km}$):** Contains the **Ozone layer ($\text{O}_3$)** which absorbs harmful **ultraviolet (UV) rays** from the Sun; free from clouds and weather disturbances, providing **ideal flying conditions for jet aircraft**.
      3. **Mesosphere ($50\text{–}80\text{ km}$):** Temperature decreases with altitude; **coldest layer** in the atmosphere.
      4. **Thermosphere / Ionosphere (above Mesosphere):** Temperature rises drastically; gas atoms are **ionized** and **reflect radio waves** back to Earth.
      5. **Exosphere (Topmost layer):** Air is extremely rarefied and pressure is minimal.
  - **13.2 Temperature, Lapse Rate, Temperature Zones, & Extremes:**
    - **Insolation:** Incoming solar radiation; temperature is measured by a **Thermometer** (Centigrade/Celsius and Fahrenheit).
    - **Normal Lapse Rate:** Temperature decreases with altitude at **$1^\circ\text{C}$ for every $165\text{ metres}$** (or **$6.4^\circ\text{C}$ per $1,000\text{ metres} / 1\text{ km}$**).
    - **Inversion of Temperature:** Temperature *increases* with increasing altitude in mountain valleys during long winter nights with clear skies, dry air, calm wind, and snow-covered ground.
    - **Three Temperature Zones:**
      1. **Torrid Zone:** $0^\circ$ to $23^\circ 30'\text{ N}$ (Tropic of Cancer) and $0^\circ$ to $23^\circ 30'\text{ S}$ (Tropic of Capricorn).
      2. **Temperate Zone:** $23^\circ 30'\text{ N}$ to $66^\circ 30'\text{ N}$ and $23^\circ 30'\text{ S}$ to $66^\circ 30'\text{ S}$.
      3. **Frigid Zone:** $66^\circ 30'\text{ N}$ to $90^\circ\text{ N}$ and $66^\circ 30'\text{ S}$ to $90^\circ\text{ S}$ (slanting rays, coldest).
    - **Isotherms:** Imaginary lines on a map connecting places with **equal temperature**.
    - **Temperature Extremes ("Facts File"):**
      - **Highest in World:** **Al Aziziya (Libya, Africa)** ($+58^\circ\text{C}$) | **Lowest in Northern Hemisphere:** **Verkhoyansk (Siberia)** ($-24^\circ\text{C}$ average) | **Coldest on Earth:** **Vostok (Antarctica)** ($-89^\circ\text{C}$).
      - **Highest in India:** **Ganganagar (Rajasthan)** ($+54^\circ\text{C}$) | **Lowest in India:** **Leh (Ladakh)** ($-10^\circ\text{C}$).
  - **13.3 Atmospheric Pressure & Seven Pressure Belts:**
    - **Measurement:** Measured in **millibars ($\text{mb}$)** using a **Barometer**. Average sea-level pressure $= \mathbf{1013.25\text{ mb}}$. Pressure decreases by **$\sim 34\text{ mb}$ per $300\text{ metres}$** of altitude. Temperature and pressure are **inversely related** (high temp $\to$ low pressure; low temp $\to$ high pressure).
    - **Isobars:** Imaginary lines connecting places with **equal atmospheric pressure**.
    - **Seven Global Pressure Belts:**
      1. **Equatorial Low Pressure Belt ($0^\circ$ to $5^\circ\text{ N & S}$):** Zone of calm called **Doldrums** ("belt of calm") or **Inter-Tropical Convergence Zone (ITCZ)** where Trade Winds converge.
      2. **North & South Sub-Tropical High Pressure Belts ($30^\circ$ to $35^\circ\text{ N & S}$):** North Sub-Tropical High ($30^\circ\text{–}35^\circ\text{ N}$) is popularly called **Horse Latitudes**.
      3. **North & South Sub-Polar Low Pressure Belts ($60^\circ$ to $65^\circ\text{ N & S}$).**
      4. **North & South Polar High Pressure Belts ($80^\circ$ to $90^\circ\text{ N & S}$).**
  - **13.4 Winds — Instruments & Four Major Types:**
    - **Instruments:** **Wind Vane / Weather Cock** (shows wind direction); **Anemometer** (measures wind speed).
    - **1. Planetary (Permanent / Prevailing / Regular) Winds:**
      - **Trade Winds:** Blow from Sub-Tropical Highs ($30^\circ\text{–}35^\circ$) to Equatorial Low ($0^\circ\text{–}5^\circ$) — **NE to SW** in Northern Hemisphere, **SE to NW** in Southern Hemisphere.
      - **Westerlies (Anti-Trade Winds):** Blow from Sub-Tropical Highs ($30^\circ\text{–}35^\circ$) to Sub-Polar Lows ($60^\circ\text{–}65^\circ$) — **SW to NE** in Northern Hemisphere, **NW to SE** in Southern Hemisphere. In the Southern Hemisphere over oceans, they are called **Roaring Forties ($40^\circ\text{ S}$)**, **Furious Fifties ($50^\circ\text{ S}$)**, and **Shrieking Sixties ($60^\circ\text{ S}$)**.
      - **Polar Winds (Easterlies):** Cold dry winds blowing from Polar Highs to Sub-Polar Lows (NE to SW in N. Hemisphere, SE to NW in S. Hemisphere).
    - **2. Seasonal (Periodic) Winds:** Indian **Monsoon winds** — **South-West Monsoon** (June to September, SW to NE) and **North-East Monsoon** (late September to mid-December, NE to SW).
    - **3. Local Winds:** Land breeze, Sea breeze, **Mountain breeze (*Katabatic* winds)**, **Valley breeze (*Anabatic* winds)**, and regional winds:
      - ***Loo*** (India), ***Chinook* / "Snow Eater"** (USA), ***Foehn*** (Alps region), ***Mistral*** (France), ***Sirocco*** (Sahara region), ***Brickfielder*** (Australia), ***Blizzard*** (Antarctica).
    - **4. Cyclones and Anti-Cyclones:**
      - **Cyclones:** Low-pressure centre surrounded by high pressure; winds blow spirally **inwards** — **anti-clockwise (counter-clockwise) in the Northern Hemisphere** and **clockwise in the Southern Hemisphere**. Regional names: **Typhoon** (Japan & China), **Hurricane** (USA & Mexico), **Willy-Willies** (Australia), **Cyclone** (India — e.g., *Bhola, Nargis, Nisha, Aila, Laila, Bijli, Jal, Tej, Biparjoy, Mocha, Michaung*), **Whirlpool** (Russia).
      - **Anti-Cyclones:** High-pressure centre with winds blowing spirally **outwards** — **clockwise in the Northern Hemisphere** and **anti-clockwise in the Southern Hemisphere**.
  - **13.5 Humidity, Clouds, Rainfall, & Weather vs. Climate:**
    - **Humidity:** Measured by **Hygrometer / Psychrometer**.
      - **Absolute Humidity:** Total water vapour per unit volume of air.
      - **Specific Humidity:** Actual water vapour per unit mass of air.
      - **Relative Humidity ($\%$):** $\frac{\text{Actual water vapour in air}}{\text{Maximum water vapour air can hold at that temperature}} \times 100$.
    - **Four Types of Clouds:**
      1. **Stratus ($< 2\text{ km}$):** Low thin sheet clouds; fair weather.
      2. **Cumulus:** Cauliflower-shaped vertical clouds with flat base and dome top; called **"wool packs"**; rain-bearing.
      3. **Cirrus ($\sim 6\text{ km}$, highest clouds):** Feathery/fibrous clouds resembling **"curls of hair"**; popularly called **"mare's tail"** or **"witch's broom"**; fair weather.
      4. **Nimbus (and Nimbo-stratus):** Low, dark-grey/black rain clouds causing heavy rain or snow.
    - **Rainfall (Measured by Rain Gauge in $\text{mm}$ or inches) — 3 Types:**
      1. **Convectional Rainfall:** Rising heated air cools and condenses; called **"afternoon rain"** in the Equatorial region (accompanied by thunder and lightning).
      2. **Orographic (Mountain / Relief) Rainfall:** Moist air forced up a mountain barrier; **windward side** (e.g., **Mangaluru**) gets heavy rain, while **leeward side** (e.g., **Hassan**) lies in the **Rain Shadow Region**.
      3. **Cyclonic (Frontal) Rainfall:** Warm air mass rises over heavier cold air mass.
    - **Rainfall Extremes ("Do You Know?"):**
      - **Wettest on Earth:** **Mawsynram (Meghalaya, India)** ($1,140\text{ cm/year}$).
      - **Wettest in Karnataka ("Cherrapunji of South India"):** **Agumbe**.
      - **Driest in India:** **Royli (Rajasthan)** ($8.3\text{ cm/year}$).
      - **Driest in Karnataka:** **Nayakanahatti (Chitradurga)**.
      - **Driest on Earth:** **Atacama Desert (Chile)** (no rain for 200 years).
    - **Weather vs. Climate:** **Weather** is short-term atmospheric condition of a small area (study $=$ **Meteorology**); **Climate** is long-term average atmospheric condition of a large area (study $=$ **Climatology**).
- **Key Terms & Instruments:**
  - *Troposphere, Stratosphere (Ozone), Mesosphere (coldest), Thermosphere/Ionosphere (radio waves), Exosphere*, *Normal lapse rate ($1^\circ\text{C}/165\text{ m}$ or $6.4^\circ\text{C}/1000\text{ m}$)*, *Isotherms & Isobars*, *Barometer ($1013.25\text{ mb}$)*, *Doldrums (ITCZ, $0^\circ\text{–}5^\circ$)*, *Horse Latitudes ($30^\circ\text{–}35^\circ\text{ N}$)*, *Wind Vane & Anemometer*, *Roaring Forties, Furious Fifties, Shrieking Sixties*, *Katabatic & Anabatic winds*, *Loo, Chinook, Foehn, Mistral, Sirocco, Brickfielder, Blizzard*, *Cyclone vs. Anticyclone rotation*, *Typhoon, Hurricane, Willy-Willies*, *Hygrometer/Psychrometer*, *Stratus, Cumulus (wool packs), Cirrus (mare's tail), Nimbus*, *Convectional, Orographic (windward Mangaluru vs. leeward rain-shadow Hassan), Cyclonic/Frontal rain*, *Mawsynram, Agumbe, Royli, Nayakanahatti, Atacama*, *Meteorology vs. Climatology*.

    - **Utility:** The capacity/power of goods and services to satisfy human wants.

      2. **Consumption Activities:** Buying and using goods and services to satisfy wants.
      3. **Exchange Activities:** Marketing system connecting producers and consumers (collection of goods, transportation, buying, and selling).
      4. **Distribution Activities:** Distributing the income earned from production justly among the **four factors of production** (determining factor prices).

### `ss-8-15-meaning-importance-business-studies` — Chapter 15: Meaning and Importance of Business Studies
- **Book Metadata:** Class 8 Social Science Part 1 | Chapter 15 | Book pp. 114–122 (PDF pp. 128–136) | **Sub-Discipline:** Business Studies (Commerce)
- **Canonical Topic Aliases:** Introduction to Business Studies, Home Trade (Retail and Wholesale) and Foreign Trade (Import Export Entrepot), Primary and Secondary Industries, Cottage and Small Scale Industries, Aids to Trade (Auxiliaries), Business Ethics and Quality Marks (ISI, AGMARK)
- **Core Concepts & Subtopics:**
  - **15.1 Objectives of Business:**
    1. **Economic Objectives:** Earning **reasonable profit** (to cover costs and provide a comfortable living), supplying goods/services, creating consumers, innovating production techniques, and advertising.
    2. **Social Objectives:** Providing employment with fair wages, paying taxes/duties for national development, and supporting social welfare (schools, hospitals, stadiums, public gardens).
  - **15.2 Trade — Home Trade vs. Foreign Trade:**
    1. **Home Trade (Internal Trade — within the country):**
       - **Retail Trade:** Last connecting link in distribution; buys in small quantities from wholesalers and sells directly to consumers.
         - **a) Permanent Shops:** Fixed shop location.
         - **b) Itinerant / Mobile Traders (no fixed shop):**
           - **Hawkers:** Carry goods on their **heads**, move door-to-door, and sell **only 1 or 2 types of goods** (vegetables, fruits, flowers).
           - **Peddlers:** Carry goods on **wheeled carts** to consumers' doors and sell **more than one type of goods**.
           - **Street Traders / Footpath Sellers:** Display goods on busy street corners or pavements.
           - **Market Sellers (Shandy Sellers):** Sell at **weekly village/town markets (*shandies*)** or annual fairs.
       - **Wholesale Trade:** Buy in **large quantities** from producers and sell in small quantities to retailers; deal in 1 or 2 types of goods, provide credit/storage to retailers, operate on low profit margins, and inform producers about market trends.
    2. **Foreign Trade (International Trade — between two countries):**
       - **Import:** Buying goods from other countries for domestic use.
       - **Export:** Selling domestic goods to other countries.
       - **Entrepot Trade (Re-export):** Buying goods from one country with a view to **selling them to another country** — best examples are **Singapore** and **Middle East countries**.
  - **15.3 Classification of Industries:**
    1. **Primary Industries (use natural resources):**
       - **a) Genetic Industries:** Reproduction/breeding of crops, plants, and animals — e.g., **horticulture, dairy farming, poultry farming**.
       - **b) Extractive Industries (Non-renewable industries):** Extracting raw materials/ores from the Earth — e.g., **mining of ores, drilling of oil wells**.
    2. **Secondary Industries (labour-oriented):**
       - **a) Manufacturing Industries:** Converting raw materials or semi-finished goods into finished products — e.g., **iron ore into steel, sugarcane into sugar**.
       - **b) Construction Industries:** Building **roads, canals, bridges**, dams.
    3. **Cottage vs. Small-Scale Industries:**
       - **Cottage (Household) Industries:** Carried out by craftsmen in their **own houses** with family members/apprentices, concentrated in **villages** for local markets — e.g., **carpentry, smithy, carpet weaving, pottery, blanket making, stone carving**.
       - **Small-Scale Industries:** Organized in **sheds** using **electric power, machines, hired labourers**, and modern techniques for local and external markets — e.g., **chemicals, engineering goods, shoes, bicycles, fans, radios, sewing machines, soaps, garments**.
  - **15.4 Auxiliaries / Aids to Trade (Overcoming the 5 Hindrances of Trade):**
    1. **Hindrance of Place $\to$ Transportation** (Road, Rail, Water, Air).
    2. **Hindrance of Finance $\to$ Banks** (Financial services and credit).
    3. **Hindrance of Risk of Loss (fire, water, calamities) $\to$ Insurance Companies**.
    4. **Hindrance of Time $\to$ Storage / Warehouses** (including cold storage).
    5. **Hindrance of Knowledge $\to$ Advertisement**.
  - **15.5 Business Ethics & Consumer Protection Measures:**
    - **Unethical / Anti-Social Practices:** Adulteration, charging abnormal prices, false weights and measures, artificial scarcity, hoarding, black marketing.
    - **Government Safeguards:**
      - **Public Distribution System (PDS):** Fair Price Shops.
      - **Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS):** Prints **ISI** (industrial products) and **AGMARK** (agricultural products) quality certification marks.
      - **Mandatory Packaging Seal:** Quantity, Date of Manufacture, Date of Expiry, and **Maximum Retail Price (MRP)**.
      - **Janatha Bazaars** and **Consumer Co-operative Societies**.
- **Key Terms & Classifications:**
  - *Economic vs. Social objectives*, *Retailers vs. Wholesalers*, *Itinerant traders (Hawkers [head], Peddlers [wheeled cart], Footpath traders, Shandy/Market sellers)*, *Import, Export, Entrepot trade (Singapore, Middle East)*, *Primary Industries (Genetic vs. Extractive)*, *Secondary Industries (Manufacturing vs. Construction)*, *Cottage vs. Small-scale industries*, *5 Hindrances (Place–Transport, Finance–Banks, Risk/Loss–Insurance, Time–Storage/Warehouses, Knowledge–Advertisement)*, *PDS*, *Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS)*, *ISI & AGMARK*, *MRP*.

      - **Kalidasa:** Great poet and dramatist. Lyrical poems: ***Meghadoota*** (exiled Yaksha sends a love message via a cloud to his wife near Mount Kailash), ***Raghuvamsha***, ***Kumarasambhava***, and ***Ritu Samhara***. Greatest play: ***Abhijnana Shakuntala*** (story of King Dushyanta, Shakuntala at Sage Kanva's ashram, Sage Durvasa's curse, the lost ring found in a fish's stomach, and reunion with their son **Sarvadamana [Bharata]** at Sage Maricha's ashram).

      1. **Dhanwantari:** Specialist in Ayurveda; known as the **"Father of Indian Medicine"**; compiled the **Ayurvedic Dictionary (*Nighantu*)**.

      3. **Sushruta:** Great surgeon; first Indian to explain surgical procedures and military medical wings in the ***Sushruta Samhita***.
      4. **Aryabhata:** Great astronomer and mathematician (India's first artificial satellite and the Aryabhata Award are named after him). Invented **"Zero"**, mastered **Algebra**, and scientifically explained that **solar eclipses** occur when the Moon comes between the Sun and Earth, and **lunar eclipses** occur when the Earth comes between the Sun and Moon (not caused by *Rahu*).

      *(Also mentions mathematician **Bhaskara-I**).*

      - Patronised by Gupta kings (**Kumaragupta** established a fine arts school there) and **Harshavardhana** (who gifted a **$25\text{-metre}$ tall bronze image of Buddha**).

  - **18.1 The Shatavahanas ($230\text{ BCE}$ – $220\text{ CE}$):**
    - **First dynasty established in the Deccan** (between the Godavari and Krishna rivers; South India lies between the Vindhya mountains and Kanyakumari).
    - **Simukha:** Formerly vassals of Chandragupta Maurya; in **$220\text{ BCE}$**, **Simukha** declared independence and made **Srikakulam** his capital (Pratishthana/Paithan was also a major capital in broader history).
    - **Gautamiputra Shatakarni:** Greatest ruler; drove away the **Sakas**, started the ***Shalivahana Saka*** era, expanded the empire over Konkan, Berar, Saurashtra, and Malwa, and earned the titles ***Trisamudratoyapitavahana*** ("one whose horses drank the waters of the three seas") and ***Shatavahana Kulayasha Pratishthapanakara***.

    - **Administration, Economy, & Culture:**
      - Kingdom divided into provinces called ***Janapadas***; trade centres/ports at **Nasik, Kalyan, Broach, and Bhatkal**.
      - Patronised **Prakrit** language: King **Hala** authored ***Gathasaptashati*** in Prakrit.
      - Art & Architecture: **Ajanta and Amaravati paintings**; **Chaityagraha at Karle** built by the Banavasi merchant **Bhootapala**.
  - **18.2 The Kadambas ($325\text{ CE}$ – $540\text{ CE}$):**
    - **First indigenous dynasty established in Karnataka**; capital was **Banavasi** (in present-day **Uttara Kannada / North Canara** district).
    - **Mayuravarma (Mayurasharma):** Founder and greatest king. Insulted by the Pallava king **Shivaskandavarma** (at Kanchi), the Brahmin scholar Mayurasharma adopted the Kshatriya way of life as **Mayuravarma**, defeated the Pallavas, and established the Kadamba empire.

      1. **Chandravalli Inscription (Chitradurga):** Records that **Mayuravarma** raised the height of the bund/wall of the **Chandravathi lake**.
      2. **Halmidi Inscription ($5\text{th Century CE}$):** Engraved during **King Kakutsavarma's** reign — **first inscription available in the Kannada language**.
      3. **Malavalli Inscription:** In **Prakrit** language.
      4. **Talagunda Inscription:** **First Sanskrit inscription found in Karnataka**.
    - **Education & Architecture:** Built the **Madhukeshwara Temple at Banavasi**; established residential educational centres (*Mathas, Ghatikas, Brahmapuris,* and *Agraharas*) notably at **Talagunda** and **Balligave**.

    - **Rulers:**
      - **Dadiga (Kongunivarma Madhava):** Founder of the Ganga dynasty.
      - **Durvinita:** Most famous Ganga king; brave warrior and scholar who annexed **Punnata**; **Nallala copper plate inscription** states he built many irrigation lakes. Wrote the Sanskrit work ***Shabdavatara*** and translated **Gunadhya's** Prakrit work ***Vaddakatha* (*Brihatkatha*)** into Sanskrit.
      - **Rachamalla IV:** His minister and general **Chavundaraya** erected the **$58\text{-foot}$ monolithic statue of Gommateshwara (Bahubali) at Shravanabelagola** (where *Mahamastakabhisheka* is held once every $12\text{ years}$; textbook narrates the story of brothers **Bharata and Bahubali** engaging in *drishti-yuddha, jala-yuddha,* and *malla-yuddha* before Bahubali's renunciation).
    - **Architecture & Tall Pillars:** Built **Kapileshwara temple** (Manne), **Pataleshwara and Maruleshwara temples** (Talakadu), **Kolaramma temple** (Kolar), **Nageshwara temple** (Begur), and tall pillars called ***Manastambhas* and *Brahma Manastambhas***.
    - **Literature of the Ganga Period:**
      - **Madhava II:** Commentary on *Dattaka Sutra*.
      - **Durvinita:** *Shabdavatara* (Sanskrit) and Sanskrit translation of *Vaddakatha*.
      - **Sripurusha:** *Gajashastra* (Sanskrit treatise on elephants).
      - **Shivamara II:** *Gajashtaka* (Kannada work).
      - **Hemasena:** *Raghava Pandaviya*.
      - **Vadibhasimha:** *Gadya Chintamani* and *Kshatra Chudamani*.
      - **Nemichandra:** *Dravyasara Samgraha*.
      - **Chavundaraya:** *Chavundaraya Purana* (*Trishashti Lakshana Mahapurana* in Kannada prose).
- **Key Terms, Rulers, Inscriptions & Works:**
  - *Shatavahanas (Simukha, Srikakulam, Gautamiputra Shatakarni [Trisamudratoyapitavahana, Shalivahana Saka], Yajnashri Shatakarni, Hala's Gathasaptashati, Karle Chaityalaya by Bhootapala)*, *Kadambas (Banavasi in Uttara Kannada, Mayurasharma/Mayuravarma vs. Pallava Shivaskandavarma, Kakutsavarma, Chandravalli, Halmidi, Malavalli, Talagunda inscriptions, Madhukeshwara temple, Talagunda & Balligave agraharas)*, *Gangas (Kuvalala, Talakadu, Manyapura/Manne, Dadiga, Durvinita [Nallala copper plate, Shabdavatara, Vaddakatha], Sripurusha [Gajashastra], Shivamara II [Gajashtaka], Rachamalla IV, Chavundaraya [58-ft Gommateshwara at Shravanabelagola, Chavunda Purana], Manastambhas)*.

      - **Shivaskandavarma:** First ruler of the dynasty.

Founded the coastal city of **Mahabalipuram (Mamallapuram)** and built monolithic rock-cut temples. **Hiuen Tsang** visited Kanchi during his reign.

      - **Sanskrit Poets at Kanchi:** **Bharavi** (author of ***Kiratarjuniya***) and **Dandin / Dandi** (author of ***Dashakumaracharita***). Nalanda scholar **Dharmapala** was born in Kanchi.

  - **21.1 The Cholas ($850\text{ CE}$ – $1279\text{ CE}$, Capital: Thanjavur):**
    - **Rulers:**
      - **Karikala Chola:** Early Sangam-age founder.
      - **Vijayalaya Chola:** Revived the Chola kingdom in the $9\text{th century}$ and made **Thanjavur** his capital.
      - **Rajaraja Chola I:** Greatest builder and conqueror; defeated Cheras, Gangas, and Pandyas, built a powerful **naval force**, conquered **Sri Lanka**, extended influence to Malaysia and Singapore, and built the famous **Brihadeeshwara Temple at Thanjavur** ($500\text{ ft}$ long courtyard, $200\text{ ft}$ tall *Shikhara*).

      - Administrative hierarchy: *Mandalam* $\to$ *Kotwangi (Kottam/Valanadu)* $\to$ *Nadu* $\to$ *Kurram* (community of villages) $\to$ *Tara-kurram* (single village).
      - **Village Assemblies:** Every village had a committee of villagers called ***Ur***. Every *Kurram* had a village assembly called ***Mahasabha***, also known as ***Perumguri***, and its elected members were called ***Perumakkal*** (chosen by election/Kudavolai; only Sanskrit scholars and wealthy property owners could contest, as detailed in the **Uttaramerur Inscription**).

      - **Temples in Karnataka:** **Aprameya Temple** (near Channapatna), **Choleshwara Temple** (at Begur near Bengaluru), and **Mukteshwara Temple** (near Binnamangala). Famous for Chola bronze idols (Shiva/Nataraja, Ganapati, Vishnu, Durga, Kartikeya).
      - **Tamil Literature:** **Kamba** wrote the Tamil ***Ramayana* (*Kamba Ramayanam*)**, **Sekkilar** composed ***Periya Purana***, and **Thirத்தக்கdevar (Thirukkadeva)** wrote ***Jeevaka Chintamani***.
  - **21.2 The Hoysalas of Dwarasamudra ($984\text{ CE}$ – $1346\text{ CE}$, Capital: Dwarasamudra / Halebidu):**
    - **Origin Myth & Founder:** **Sala**, on the order of the Jain monk **Sudatta**, fought and killed a tiger near **Sosevuru village** (modern **Angadi** in Mudigere taluk, Chikkamagaluru district). People exclaimed ***"Poy Sala!" / "Hoysala!"*** ("Strike, Sala!"), and the tiger-killing scene became the **royal emblem of the Hoysalas**.
    - **Rulers:**
      - Early kings: **Nripakama, Ereyanga, Ballala I**.
      - **Vishnuvardhana (originally *Bittideva*):** Greatest Hoysala king. Captured **Gangavadi** from the Cholas and earned the title ***Talakadugonda***. To commemorate this victory, he built the **Kirtinarayana Temple at Talakadu** and the **Chennakeshava (Vijayanarayana) Temple at Belur**. Patronised **Ramanujacharya** (who fled Chola persecution and spread **Srivaishnavism / Vishishtadvaita** across Karnataka, settling at Melukote).
      - **Ballala III:** Last great ruler, during whose reign the kingdom declined and the Vijayanagara Empire rose.
    - **Administration, *Garuda* Bodyguards, & Irrigation:**
      - Village officials: ***Gowda, Senabova* (accountant), and *Talawara* (watchman)**.
      - ***Garuda* (Royal Suicide Squads):** Special bodyguards of the Hoysala king who voluntarily sacrificed their lives when the king died.
      - Built major reservoirs: **Shanti Sagara (Sulekere), Ballalaraya Samudra, Vishnu Samudra**.
    - **Literature of the Hoysala Period:**
      - **Kannada Literature:**
        - **Rudrabhatta:** ***Jagannatha Vijaya***
        - **Janna:** ***Yashodhara Charite***
        - **Harihara:** ***Girija Kalyana*** (*Champu* work)
        - **Raghavanka:** ***Harishchandra Kavya*** (pioneered *Shatpadi* metre)
        - **Keshiraja:** ***Shabdamanidarpana*** (authoritative Kannada grammar work).
      - **Sanskrit Literature:** **Ramanujacharya** wrote ***Sreebhasya***; **Parasharabhatta** composed ***Sri Guna Ratnakosha***.
    - **Hoysala Soapstone Architecture (World-Famous):**
      - Carved in soft **soapstone (chloritic schist)**.
      - **5 Signature Features of Hoysala Temples:** (1) **Star-shaped Sanctum Sanctorum (*Garbhagriha*)**, (2) ***Upapeetha* (*Jagati* — raised star-shaped platform)**, (3) **Intricate decorative wall panels**, (4) ***Shikhara***, and (5) **Lathe-turned polished pillars** with bracket figures called ***Madanikas / Shilabalikas*** (at Belur).
      - **Three Masterpiece Temples:**
        1. **Chennakeshava Temple at Belur:** Built by **Vishnuvardhana**.
        2. **Hoysaleshwara Temple at Halebidu:** Built by military general **Ketamalla**.
        3. **Keshava Temple at Somanathapura:** Built by general **Somadandanayaka**.
      - **Famous Hoysala Sculptors:** **Dasoja, Chavana, Jakanachari (Jakana), and Dankanachari (Dankana)**.
- **Key Terms, Rulers, Works & Monuments:**
  - *Cholas (Karikala, Vijayalaya [Thanjavur], Rajaraja Chola I [Brihadeeshwara temple, Sri Lanka, navy], Rajendra Chola I [Gangaikonda Cholapuram, Choleshwara], Ur, Mahasabha / Perumguri, Perumakkal, Uttaramerur agrahara, Bellandur lake, Aprameya [Channapatna] / Begur Choleshwara / Binnamangala Mukteshwara temples, Kamba's Ramayana, Sekkilar's Periya Purana, Thirukkadeva's Jeevaka Chintamani)*, *Hoysalas (Sala & Jain monk Sudatta at Sosevuru/Angadi, Bittideva / Vishnuvardhana [Talakadugonda, Kirtinarayana temple at Talakadu, Chennakeshava temple at Belur], Ramanujacharya [Vishishtadvaita, Sreebhasya], Ballala III, Gowda/Senabova/Talawara, Garuda bodyguards, Rudrabhatta [Jagannatha Vijaya], Janna [Yashodhara Charite], Harihara [Girija Kalyana], Raghavanka [Harishchandra Kavya], Keshiraja [Shabdamanidarpana], Soapstone, Star-shaped Jagati, Shilabalikas/Madanikas, Ketamalla [Halebidu Hoysaleshwara], Somadandanayaka [Somanathapura Keshava], Dasoja, Chavana, Jakana, Dankana)*.

### `ss-8-22-democracy` — Chapter 22: Democracy
- **Book Metadata:** Class 8 Social Science Part 2 | Chapter 22 | Book pp. 43–49 (PDF pp. 45–51) | **Sub-Discipline:** Political Science
- **Canonical Topic Aliases:** Meaning and Types of Democracy (Direct vs. Indirect), Universal Adult Franchise (61st Amendment), Election Commission of India, General Elections vs. By-Elections
- **Core Concepts & Subtopics:**
  - **22.1 Meaning, Etymology, & Classic Definitions of Democracy:**
    - Ancient Indian roots: ***Sabha* and *Samiti*** of the Vedic period.
    - **Greek Etymology:** Derived from ***Demokratia*** — ***Demos*** ("people") + ***Kratia*** ("rule/power") $\to$ **"Rule of the people"**.
    - **Abraham Lincoln's Definition:** *"Democracy is a government of the people, by the people, and for the people."*
    - **Lord Bryce (Braze):** Governing power is vested not in any particular class, but in the members of the community as a whole.
    - **Herman Finer:** *"Democracy is not only a philosophical form of government but also a way of life."*
  - **22.2 Types of Democracy — Direct vs. Indirect:**
    1. **Direct Democracy:** Citizens directly make laws/policies through devices like **Referendum** and **Initiative**. Practiced in **ancient Greece** and currently in **Switzerland** (feasible only in small countries with small populations).
    2. **Indirect (Representative) Democracy:** Citizens periodically elect representatives to legislatures who make laws and control the executive — practiced in large nations like **India and the USA**.
  - **22.3 Characteristics, Success Factors, Merits, & Demerits of Democracy:**
    - **Core Principles:** Consent of the people, Rule of Law, Liberty and Equality, independent judiciary, welfare state, strong opposition party, free and unbiased press (Fourth Estate), educated and vigilant electorate.
  - **22.4 Universal Adult Franchise & Elections in India:**
    - **Universal Adult Franchise ("One Man, One Vote"):** Every Indian citizen aged **$18\text{ years}$ and above** has the right to vote without discrimination of caste, religion, gender, language, or qualification.
    - **61st Constitutional Amendment Act, 1989:** Reduced the minimum voting age in India from **$21\text{ years}$ to $18\text{ years}$**.
    - **General Elections vs. By-Elections:**
      - **General Elections:** Routine elections held once every **$5\text{ years}$**.
      - **By-Elections:** Elections held within the 5-year term to fill a seat that falls vacant (due to death or resignation).
    - **Election Commission of India (ECI):**
      - Constitutional body headquartered in **New Delhi**.
      - Consists of **1 Chief Election Commissioner (CEC)** and **2 Election Commissioners**, appointed by the **President of India** (Parliament determines the number of commissioners).
      - **State Election Commission:** Separate constitutional body at the state level that conducts elections to **rural and urban local bodies** (Panchayat Raj and Municipalities).
- **Key Terms, Thinkers & Amendments:**
  - *Demokratia (Demos + Kratia)*, *Abraham Lincoln*, *Lord Bryce*, *Herman Finer*, *Direct Democracy (Ancient Greece, Switzerland; Referendum & Initiative) vs. Indirect/Representative Democracy (India, USA)*, *Universal Adult Franchise (18 years)*, *61st Amendment Act 1989 (reduced voting age from 21 to 18)*, *General elections (5 years) vs. By-elections*, *Election Commission of India (New Delhi, 1 CEC + 2 ECs appointed by President)*, *State Election Commission (local body elections)*.

### `ss-8-23-local-self-government` — Chapter 23: Local Self Government
- **Book Metadata:** Class 8 Social Science Part 2 | Chapter 23 | Book pp. 50–62 (PDF pp. 52–64) | **Sub-Discipline:** Political Science (Public Administration & Civics)
- **Canonical Topic Aliases:** Panchayat Raj System in Karnataka (73rd Amendment), Grama Sabha Grama Panchayat Taluk Panchayat and Zilla Panchayat, Urban Local Bodies (74th Amendment: Town/City Municipalities and Mahanagara Palike / BBMP)
- **Core Concepts & Subtopics:**
  - **23.1 Constitutional Background & Evolution in Karnataka:**
    - Realises Mahatma Gandhi's dream of ***Grama Swarajya*** (Directive Principles of State Policy — Article 40).
    - **1983 Karnataka Act** (implemented in **1985**): Introduced Zilla Parishat, Taluk Panchayat Samithi, Mandal Panchayat, and Nyaya Panchayat.
    - **73rd and 74th Constitutional Amendment Acts (came into effect in 1993):**
      - **73rd Amendment (1993):** Gave constitutional status to **3-tier Rural Panchayat Raj Institutions (PRIs)**; **Karnataka Panchayat Raj Act, 1993** enacted. **Article 243K** provides for the **State Election Commission** for PRIs.
      - **74th Amendment (1993):** Gave constitutional status to **Urban Local Bodies (ULBs)**; **Article 243ZA** provides for State Election Commission for ULBs.
    - **Current Karnataka PRI Counts (Karnataka Economic Survey 2023–24 chart):** **31 Zilla Panchayats**, **239 Taluk Panchayats**, and **5,954 Grama Panchayats**.
  - **23.2 Rural Local Self-Government (Three-Tier Panchayat Raj System):**
    - **Base Council — *Grama Sabha* (Village Assembly):**
      - **No election:** Comprises **all registered voters aged $18\text{ years}$ and above** in the village.
      - Presided over by the *Grama Panchayat Adhyaksha*; must meet **at least once in 6 months**; selects beneficiaries for government welfare schemes and discusses village development.
    - **Tier 1 (Village Level) — *Grama Panchayat* (GP):**
      - **Population Norm:** Formed for a village/group of villages with **$5,000$ to $7,000\text{ population}$** (**$2,000\text{ population}$** in Uttara Kannada, Dakshina Kannada, and Malnad regions).
      - **Representation:** **1 member elected for every $400\text{ voters}$** for a **5-year term** (with reservations for SC, ST, OBC, and Women). *(2000 Amendment requires every GP member to have a household toilet).*
      - **Meetings & Quorum:** Meets **at least once in 2 months**; quorum is **$1/3\text{rd}$ of members**.
      - **Leadership & Officials:** Members elect ***Adhyaksha* (President)** and ***Upadhyaksha* (Vice-President)** for a term of **$30\text{ months}$**. State Government appoints a **Panchayat Development Officer (PDO)** and a **Grama Panchayat Secretary (Grade I / II)**.
    - **Tier 2 (Taluk / Block Level) — *Taluk Panchayat* (TP):**
      - **Representation:** **1 directly elected member for every $10,000\text{ voters}$** (5-year term), plus **$1/5\text{th}$ of Grama Panchayat Presidents** chosen by **lottery system (lots)** on a **1-year rotation**, plus local MLAs, MLCs, and MPs.
      - **Meetings & Leadership:** Meets **at least once in 2 months**; **President and Vice-President** elected for a **$30\text{ months}$** term.
      - **Official:** State Government appoints an **Executive Officer (EO)**. Consolidates GP annual plans and submits them to the Zilla Panchayat.
    - **Tier 3 (District Level) — *Zilla Panchayat* (ZP):**
      - **Representation:** Directly elected members for a 5-year term based on district population:
        - **1 member per $40,000\text{ population}$** (general districts)
        - **1 member per $30,000\text{ population}$** (Dakshina Kannada, Uttara Kannada, Chikkamagaluru)
        - **1 member per $18,000\text{ population}$** (Kodagu / Coorg)
        - Plus all **Taluk Panchayat Presidents, MLAs, MLCs, and MPs** of the district (who can attend and vote).
      - **Meetings, Leadership, & Relief Power:** Meets **at least once in 2 months**; ***Adhyaksha* (President)** and ***Upadhyaksha*** elected for **$30\text{ months}$**. The ZP President can sanction up to **Rs. $1\text{ lakh}$** for immediate natural calamity relief; has **5 Standing Committees**.
      - **Official:** State Government appoints a **Chief Executive Officer (CEO)** (of **District Commissioner / IAS rank**), who participates in meetings/discussions **but has no right to vote**.
  - **23.3 Urban Local Bodies (74th Amendment & Karnataka Municipal Corporations Act, 1976 / 2011):**
    - **Three Categories:** (1) **City Corporations (*Mahanagara Palike*)** in major cities, (2) **Town Municipalities (*Purasabha*), City Municipalities (*Nagarasabha*), and *Pattana Panchayats***, and (3) **Cantonment Boards** (under the Ministry of Defence).
    - **1. Town Municipality vs. City Municipality:**
      - **Town Municipality (*Purasabha*):** Population between **$20,000$ and $50,000$** $\to$ **$23\text{ to }27\text{ Councillors}$**.
      - **City Municipality (*Nagarasabha*):** Population between **$50,000$ and $3\text{ lakhs}$** $\to$ **$31\text{ to }37\text{ Councillors}$**.
      - **Structure:** Elected **Councillors** (5-year term) + **$5\text{ nominated members}$** appointed by the State Government (experts in municipal administration — **no right to vote**) + local MLAs, MLCs, MPs (can vote).
      - **Leadership & Official:** **President and Vice-President** elected by councillors for a **$1\text{-year}$ term**; **4 Standing Committees**; administrative head is the **Chief Officer** (appointed by State Government).
    - **2. City Corporation (*Mahanagara Palike*):**
      - Governed by the **Karnataka Municipal Corporations Act, 1976** (amended in **2011**).
      - **Criteria:** Population **$> 2\text{ lakhs}$** and annual income **$> \text{Rs. }1\text{ crore}$**.
      - **Total in Karnataka:** **$11\text{ Municipal Corporations}$** ($10\text{ City Corporations} + 1\text{ Bruhat Bengaluru Mahanagara Palike [BBMP]}$ with **$225\text{ wards}$**).
      - **Representation:** Divided into **Wards**; 1 **Corporator** elected per ward ($30\text{ to }100\text{ corporators}$ generally; 5-year term) + **$5\text{ nominated expert members}$** (no voting right) + local MLAs, MLCs, MPs (can vote).
      - **Leadership & Official:** **Mayor** (the **"First Citizen of the City"**) and **Deputy Mayor** are elected by Corporators for a **$1\text{-year}$ term**. The real executive head is the **Commissioner** (generally an **IAS officer** appointed by the State Government).

### `ss-8-25-kinds-of-societies` — Chapter 25: Kinds of Societies
- **Book Metadata:** Class 8 Social Science Part 2 | Chapter 25 | Book pp. 65–68 (PDF pp. 67–70) | **Sub-Discipline:** Sociology
- **Canonical Topic Aliases:** Types of Human Societies, Hunting and Food-Gathering Society, Cattle-Rearing (Pastoral) Society, Farming (Agricultural) Society, Industrial Society
- **Core Concepts & Subtopics:**
  - **25.1 Classification of Societies (Stages of Human Social Evolution Based on Occupation):**
    1. **Hunting and Food-Gathering Society:**
       - **Oldest and simplest** form of human society; **nomadic** lifestyle using stone axes, spears, and daggers to hunt wild animals and gather forest fruits, seeds, roots, and tubers.
       - **Key Features:** Very small scattered groups (**never more than $40\text{–}50\text{ people}$**); **no concept of wealth** or desire to earn money (share everything); bound only by **family and kinship relationships**; **no political institutions** (no ruler or ruled).
    2. **Cattle-Rearing (Pastoral) Society:**
       - Rears herds of cattle for livelihood alongside hunting/gathering; found in **grasslands, hilly regions, deserts**, and areas unsuitable for agriculture in parts of **Africa and Central Asia**.
       - **Key Features:** Larger groups of several hundreds to thousands — e.g., the **Bakhtiari (*Bhaktiyari*) community** has **$5,000\text{ to }1,50,000\text{ people}$**. The person owning the **largest number of cattle** is considered rich, powerful, and becomes the **leader of the group**.
    3. **Farming (Agricultural) Society:**
       - **Key Features:** Triggered by the **"Farming Revolution"** with the **invention of the plough around $3000\text{ BCE}$** (and animal-drawn ploughs). Led to **permanent settlements and the birth of villages**. Surplus food freed many people from full-time fieldwork to develop diverse **arts, crafts, and skills**.
    4. **Industrial Society:**
       - Depends on industries manufacturing goods using **science and technology** (steam engine, internal combustion engine, electric and nuclear energy).
       - **Key Features:** Massive rural-to-urban migration causing **growth of cities (urbanization)**, and pervasive **Division of Labour** (allocating tasks according to workers' specialized skills).
- **Key Terms & Clues:**
  - *40–50 people (Hunting & food-gathering society)*, *Bakhtiari community (5,000–1,50,000 people, Cattle-rearing society)*, *Invention of the plough (3000 BCE, Farming Revolution, birth of villages)*, *Steam/internal combustion engine & Division of labour (Industrial society)*.

### `ss-8-26-hydrosphere` — Chapter 26: Hydrosphere
- **Book Metadata:** Class 8 Social Science Part 2 | Chapter 26 | Book pp. 69–76 (PDF pp. 71–78) | **Sub-Discipline:** Geography (Oceanography)
- **Canonical Topic Aliases:** Water Bodies (Ocean, Sea, Gulf, Bay, Strait, Isthmus), Relief of the Ocean Floor, Temperature and Salinity of Ocean Water, Warm and Cold Ocean Currents, Tides (Spring and Neap Tides) and Conservation of Oceans
- **Core Concepts & Subtopics:**
  - **26.1 Types of Water Bodies & Land Connectors:**
    - Water covers **$70.78\%$ ($361\text{ million sq. km}$)** of the Earth.
    - **Oceans:** Deep, vast expanses of water between continents (Pacific, Atlantic, Indian, Arctic).
    - **Sea:** Part of an ocean extending far inland, smaller than an ocean — e.g., **Arabian Sea, Caspian Sea, Red Sea**.
    - **Gulf:** Part of an ocean/sea penetrating deep into land, smaller than a sea — e.g., **Gulf of Mannar, Persian Gulf, Gulf of Mexico**.
    - **Bay:** Semi-circular water body partially surrounded by land — e.g., **Bay of Bengal, Bay of Biscay, Bay of Fundy, Hudson Bay**.
    - **Strait:** Narrow strip of water **joining two large water bodies** — e.g., **Palk Strait, Strait of Gibraltar, Bering Strait**.
    - **Isthmus:** Narrow stretch of land **joining two large landmasses** (ideal for cutting ship canals) — e.g., **Suez Canal** (between Asia and Africa) and **Panama Canal** (between North America and South America).
  - **26.2 Relief of the Ocean Floor (Four Major Parts):**
    1. **Continental Shelf:** Shallow coastal margin bordered by the sea coast and continental slope. Average depth $= \mathbf{100\text{ fathoms}}$ (**$1\text{ fathom} = \mathbf{6\text{ feet}}$**). Vital for **fishing, aquaculture, navigation, and mineral/oil extraction**.
    2. **Continental Slope:** Steep slope linking the continental shelf to the deep sea plain; **submarine canyons** are a special feature here.
    3. **Deep Sea Plain (*Abyssal Plain*):** Vast flat plain covering the **largest area** of the ocean floor; features **Seamounts** (volcanic peaks) and **Guyots** (flat-topped seamounts).
    4. **Ocean Deeps (Ocean Trenches):** Deepest parts of the ocean floor:
       - **Challenger Deep in the Mariana Trench** (Pacific Ocean, near the Philippines) $= \mathbf{10,929\text{ metres}}$ deep (**deepest point on Earth**).
       - Other Pacific trenches: **Tonga Trench** and **Kurile Trench**.
  - **26.3 Temperature and Salinity of Ocean Water:**
    - **Temperature:** Decreases from Equator towards poles and decreases with depth; **sunlight penetrates only up to $200\text{ metres}$ depth** (photic zone where marine life is concentrated).
    - **Salinity:** Percentage of dissolved salts in ocean water. **Average ocean salinity $= \mathbf{35\text{ PPT}}$** (parts per thousand, i.e., $35\text{ g}$ per $1,000\text{ g}$ of water). Salinity is **highest near the Tropics** (high evaporation), **lowest near the Poles**, and **average at the Equator** (high evaporation offset by heavy rainfall).
    - **Most Saline Water Bodies in the World ("Facts File"):**
      1. **Lake Van (Turkey):** $\mathbf{330\text{ ppt}}$
      2. **Dead Sea (Israel & Jordan):** $\mathbf{300\text{ ppt}}$
      3. **Sambhar Lake (Rajasthan, India):** $\mathbf{265\text{ ppt}}$
      4. **Red Sea (Asia & Africa):** $\mathbf{240\text{ ppt}}$.
  - **26.4 Ocean Currents (Warm vs. Cold Currents by Ocean):**
    - Three movements of ocean water: **Waves, Currents, and Tides**.
    - **Warm Currents** flow from Equatorial to Sub-polar regions; **Cold Currents** flow from Polar to Equatorial regions.

| Ocean Basin | Warm Ocean Currents | Cold Ocean Currents |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **North Pacific Ocean** | **North Equatorial Current**, **Kuroshio Current** | **Oyashio (Kurile) Current**, **California Current** |
| **South Pacific Ocean** | **South Equatorial Current**, **East Australian Current** | **Peru (Humboldt) Current**, **West Wind Drift** |
| **North Atlantic Ocean** | **North Equatorial Current**, **Gulf Stream**, **North Atlantic Drift** | **Labrador Current**, **Norwegian Current**, **Canary Current** |
| **South Atlantic Ocean** | **South Equatorial Current**, **Brazilian Current** | **Falkland Current**, **Benguela Current** |
| **North Indian Ocean** | Reverses seasonally with **SW Monsoon** (SW $\to$ NE) and **NE Monsoon** (NE $\to$ SW) | — |
| **South Indian Ocean** | **Mozambique Current**, **Madagascar Current**, **Agulhas Current** | **West Australian Current** |

    - **World's Major Fishing Grounds (Meeting Places of Warm & Cold Currents):**
      1. **Honshu–Hokkaido Fishing Grounds (Japan):** Meeting of **Kuroshio (warm)** and **Oyashio (cold)** currents.
      2. **Grand Banks (Newfoundland, USA/Canada):** Meeting of **Gulf Stream (warm)** and **Labrador (cold)** currents.
      3. **Dogger Bank:** North Sea region of Europe.
  - **26.5 Tides — Causes, Timing, & Types:**
    - **Causes:** Gravitational pull of the **Moon** and **Sun**, Earth's rotation, and centrifugal force.
    - **High Tide (*Flood Tide*) vs. Low Tide (*Ebb Tide*) Timing:**
      - Time between 1 High Tide and the next Low Tide $= \mathbf{6\text{ hours }13\text{ minutes}}$.
      - Time between two successive High Tides $= \mathbf{12\text{ hours }26\text{ minutes}}$.
      - In $\mathbf{24\text{ hours }52\text{ minutes}}$, there are **2 High Tides and 2 Low Tides**.
    - **Spring Tide vs. Neap Tide:**
      - **Spring Tide:** Occurs on **New Moon (*Amavasya*) and Full Moon (*Purnima*)** when the **Sun, Moon, and Earth are in a straight line** (syzygy) $\to$ high tides are highest and low tides are lowest.
      - **Neap Tide:** Occurs on **First Quarter and Last Quarter** days of the Moon when the **Sun-Earth line and Moon-Earth line are at right angles ($90^\circ$)** $\to$ high tides are not very high and low tides are not very low.
    - **Uses of Tides:** Aids navigation, harbour construction, keeps harbours clean of silt, salt preparation, fishing, and **tidal energy** generation.
- **Key Terms & Numbers:**
  - *Sea, Gulf, Bay, Strait (Palk, Gibraltar, Bering), Isthmus (Suez, Panama)*, *Continental Shelf (100 fathoms; 1 fathom = 6 ft)*, *Continental Slope (submarine canyons)*, *Abyssal / Deep Sea Plain (Seamounts, Guyots)*, *Ocean Trenches (Challenger Deep in Mariana Trench = 10,929 m; Tonga, Kurile)*, *200 m sunlight limit*, *Average salinity 35 ppt*, *Lake Van (330 ppt), Dead Sea (300 ppt), Sambhar Lake (265 ppt), Red Sea (240 ppt)*, *Kuroshio, Oyashio, California, Peru/Humboldt, Gulf Stream, Labrador, Canary, Brazilian, Falkland, Benguela, Mozambique, Agulhas, West Australian currents*, *Grand Banks, Honshu-Hokkaido, Dogger Bank*, *Flood tide vs. Ebb tide (6 hr 13 min / 12 hr 26 min / 24 hr 52 min)*, *Spring tide (straight line, New/Full Moon) vs. Neap tide (right angle, Quarters)*.

### `ss-8-27-biosphere` — Chapter 27: Biosphere
- **Book Metadata:** Class 8 Social Science Part 2 | Chapter 27 | Book pp. 77–82 (PDF pp. 79–84) | **Sub-Discipline:** Geography (Environmental Geography)
- **Canonical Topic Aliases:** Biosphere and Ecology, Environmental Pollution (Air, Water, Soil, Noise), Global Warming and Greenhouse Effect, Ozone Depletion (CFCs), Acid Rain (Lake Killer / Black Triangle), Biodiversity Conservation
- **Core Concepts & Subtopics:**
  - **27.1 Meaning of Biosphere, Ecology, Ecosystem, & Environment:**
    - **Biosphere:** The 4th component of the Earth where life exists (totality of all living organisms).
    - **Ecology:** Study of the interaction of living organisms with their physical, chemical, and biological environment; natural harmony is called **Ecological Balance**.
    - **Ecosystem:** Community of plants and animals interacting with their immediate animate and inanimate environment.
    - **Environment:** Natural (geographical) environment and Cultural (man-made) environment.
  - **27.2 Environmental Pollution & Four Major Types:**
    - **Pollutants:** Visible (smoke, dust, sewage, garbage) and invisible (bacteria, toxic chemicals).
    1. **Air Pollution:** Defined by **WHO** as substances put into air by human activity in concentrations harmful to health, vegetation, and property.
       - *Sources:* Natural (volcanoes, forest fires, cosmic dust) and Man-made (industries, automobiles, mining, nuclear plants).
       - *Major Pollutants:* $\text{CO}_2$, Carbon monoxide ($\text{CO}$), Sulphur dioxide ($\text{SO}_2$), Nitrogen oxides ($\text{NO}_x$), **Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs)**, hydrocarbons.
    2. **Water Pollution:** Alteration in physical, chemical, and biological characteristics of water harmful to human and aquatic life.
       - *Effects:* Spreads water-borne epidemics (**cholera, typhoid, diarrhoea, jaundice, TB**), kills aquatic life, contaminates groundwater/oceans (oil spills).
    3. **Soil Pollution:** Decrease in soil quality due to industrial/mining/urban/nuclear waste and excessive chemical fertilizers/pesticides $\to$ loss of humus, soil microbes, and fertility (turns land into wasteland).
    4. **Noise Pollution:** Undesirable sound dumped into the atmosphere causing **hearing loss, headaches, cardiovascular diseases, psychological disorders, and lack of concentration**.
  - **27.3 Global Environmental Hazards & Biodiversity:**
    - **Global Warming & Greenhouse Effect:** Burning fossil fuels increases greenhouse gases ($\text{CO}_2$, etc.) that trap heat radiated from the Earth's surface, warming the globe, shifting climatic zones, melting glaciers (Himalayas, Arctic, Antarctic), and raising sea levels.
    - **Ozone Depletion:** Thin **Ozone ($\text{O}_3$) layer in the Stratosphere** absorbs harmful **UV rays**. Synthetic chemicals like **Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs)** from air conditioners, refrigerators, and aerosol sprays destroy ozone. **Highest ozone depletion ("Ozone Hole") is recorded over Antarctica**.
    - **Acid Rain ("Lake Killer"):** Raindrops passing through polluted air containing high amounts of **sulphuric acid** and **carbon monoxide / nitric oxides**. Kills marine/aquatic life (hence called ***"Lake Killer"***), destroys forests, crops, and monuments.
      - **"Black Triangle":** Region stretching over **Poland, Czech Republic, and South-East Germany** — one of the worst acid-rain-affected regions in the world.
    - **Biodiversity:** Variety of **flora (plants) and fauna (animals)** in a region.
- **Key Terms & Clues:**
  - *Biosphere*, *Ecology & Ecological balance*, *Ecosystem*, *Air, Water, Soil, Noise pollution*, *Global warming & Greenhouse effect*, *Ozone depletion in Stratosphere (CFCs, Antarctica Ozone Hole)*, *Acid rain (Sulphuric acid, "Lake Killer", "Black Triangle" — Poland, Czech Republic, SE Germany)*, *Biodiversity (flora and fauna)*.

### `ss-8-28-basic-concepts-economics` — Chapter 28: Basic Concepts of Economics
- **Book Metadata:** Class 8 Social Science Part 2 | Chapter 28 | Book pp. 83–89 (PDF pp. 85–91) | **Sub-Discipline:** Economics
- **Canonical Topic Aliases:** Basic Concepts of Economics (Demand, Supply, Production, Distribution, Consumption), Four Types of Utility (Principle, Form, Time, Place), Factors of Production and Rewards, Co-operation, National Income and Per Capita Income (including Legacy GDP, GNP, NNP, CSO), Physical vs. Mental Labour
- **Core Concepts & Subtopics:**
  - **28.1 Demand and Supply (The Two Pillars of Economic Activity):**
    - **Demand:** Desire/want backed by **willingness to buy and ability to pay money** at a given price and time.
      - *Law of Demand:* When price increases, demand decreases; when price decreases, demand increases.
      - **Demand is considered the "Index of the Nation's Progress"** (rising demand boosts production, employment, income, and standard of living).
    - **Supply:** Quantity of goods and services readily available for sale in the market at a given price. Generally, as price rises, supply increases.
    - **Price:** Value of a good or service expressed in terms of **money**; equates demand and supply.
  - **28.2 Production, Four Factors of Production, & Distribution:**
    - **Production:** Man cannot create new matter; in Economics, **production means creating or increasing special utility and value** (completed only when goods reach the consumer via transport, trade, and distribution).
    - **Distribution & the Four Factors of Production with Their Rewards:**

| Factor of Production | Reward / Share of National Income |
| :--- | :--- |
| 1. **Land (Natural Wealth)** | **Rent** |
| 2. **Labour** | **Wages** |
| 3. **Capital** | **Interest** |
| 4. **Organisation (Entrepreneurship)** | **Profit** |

    - **Profit:** The reward that the producer/organiser gets for services rendered in production (what remains from income after paying rent, wages, and interest). Profit is the main engine of economic activity.
  - **28.3 Consumption & Utility (Four Types of Utility):**
    - **Consumption:** Using goods and services to satisfy wants. Two types: (1) Goods/services used for further production, and (2) Finished goods used directly to satisfy wants.
    - **Utility:** Power/capacity of a good or service to satisfy human wants (distinct from usefulness or moral goodness — e.g., alcohol has no usefulness/benefit, yet it possesses *utility* because it satisfies a want; *utility* is the power to give satisfaction, while *satisfaction* is the effect).
    - **Four Types of Utility:**
      1. **Principle (Natural/Basic) Utility:** Things used in their original natural form — e.g., **water, fruit, egg**.
      2. **Form Utility:** Utility created by giving a new shape or form to raw material — e.g., a **sculptor carving an idol from stone**, or a **carpenter making a chair/table from wood**.
      3. **Time Utility:** Utility created by storing/preserving abundant goods and using them when scarce — e.g., **making mango pickles in summer to use when fresh mangoes are out of season**.
      4. **Place Utility:** Utility created by transporting goods from where they are abundant to where they are scarce — e.g., **transporting Shimla apples or Kodagu oranges to other markets**.
  - **28.4 Co-operation:**
    - Economic system where people voluntarily unite on the basis of **equality and self-help** to protect their economic interests without middlemen or exploitation.
    - **Spirit / Motto:** ***"Each for all and all for each"***; operates on **"One member, one vote"** irrespective of the number of shares held.
  - **28.5 National Income & Per Capita Income (Plus Legacy Syllabus National Income Metrics):**
    - **National Income (NI):** The sum total of the **money value of all final goods and services produced in a country during a year** from all sources of production. Rising National Income is the symbol of a nation's economic development.
    - **Per Capita Income (PCI):** Average income per person in a country for a year:
      $$\text{Per Capita Income (PCI)} = \frac{\text{National Income}}{\text{Total Population of the Nation}}$$
    - **Legacy NMMS Syllabus Concepts (from Pre-2024 Class 8 Economics Chapters on *National Income & Sectoral Aspects*):**
      - **GDP (Gross Domestic Product):** Total money value of all final goods and services produced within the domestic territory of a country in a year.
      - **GNP (Gross National Product):** $\text{GDP} + \text{Net Factor Income from Abroad (NFIA)}$.
      - **NNP (Net National Product):** $\text{GNP} - \text{Depreciation}$ (wear and tear of capital goods). $\text{NNP at Factor Cost} = \text{National Income}$.
      - **CSO (Central Statistical Organisation / NSO):** The official agency in India responsible for estimating and publishing **National Income** data (founded in 1951).
      - **Three Sectors of the Economy:** **Primary Sector** (Agriculture, Forestry, Fishing, Mining), **Secondary Sector** (Manufacturing, Construction, Electricity/Water), and **Tertiary / Service Sector** (Trade, Transport, Banking, Insurance, Education, Health, Administration — largest contributor to India's GDP).
  - **28.6 Labour — Physical vs. Mental Labour & Dignity of Labour:**
    - **Physical Labour:** Requires physical strength — e.g., **digging, ploughing, lifting/carrying loads, loading/unloading cargo**.
    - **Mental Labour:** Requires intelligence and education — e.g., **teacher, scientist, lawyer, technician, IT worker, clerk**.
    - **Dignity of Labour:** Championed and practiced by **Mahatma Gandhi** — all forms of honest work (physical or mental) deserve equal respect without discrimination of high or low.
- **Key Terms & Formulas:**
  - *Demand ("Index of Nation's progress")*, *Supply*, *Price*, *Production (creating utility)*, *4 Factors of Production & Rewards (Land–Rent, Labour–Wages, Capital–Interest, Organisation–Profit)*, *Consumption*, *Utility vs. Usefulness*, *4 Types of Utility (Principle, Form, Time, Place)*, *Co-operation ("Each for all and all for each")*, *National Income*, $\text{Per Capita Income} = \frac{\text{National Income}}{\text{Total Population}}$, *Physical vs. Mental labour*, *Dignity of labour (Mahatma Gandhi)*, *Legacy terms: GDP, GNP, NNP, CSO, Primary/Secondary/Tertiary sectors*.
- **Mapping & Disambiguation Rules:**
  - **Map here** for all questions on Demand, Supply, Price, Factors of Production & their rewards (Rent, Wages, Interest, Profit), the 4 types of Utility (Principle, Form, Time, Place), National Income, Per Capita Income, Physical vs. Mental Labour, and any legacy NMMS PYQs on **GDP, GNP, NNP, CSO, or Economic Sectors**.

### `ss-8-29-emergence-business-organisations` — Chapter 29: Emergence of Different Business Organisations
- **Book Metadata:** Class 8 Social Science Part 2 | Chapter 29 | Book pp. 90–95 (PDF pp. 92–97) | **Sub-Discipline:** Business Studies (Commerce)
- **Canonical Topic Aliases:** Forms of Business Organisations, Small Scale Private Business Organisations, Sole Trading Concerns (Sole Proprietorship), Partnership Firms (Indian Partnership Act 1932), Types of Partners, Hindu Undivided Family (HUF) Business and Karta
- **Core Concepts & Subtopics:**
  - **29.1 Four Broad Sectors of Business Organisations:**
    1. **Private Sector** (Sole Trading, Partnership, HUF, Co-operatives, Joint Stock Companies, MNCs)
    2. **Public Sector (Government Undertakings)**
    3. **Joint Sector**
    4. **Public Utilities**.
  - **29.2 Small-Scale Private Sector Business Organisations (Three Types):**
    1. **Sole Trading Concerns (Sole Proprietorship):**
       - **Oldest and simplest** form of business organisation; owned, invested, and managed by a **single person** who enjoys all profits and bears **unlimited liability** for all losses.
       - *Merits:* No legal formalities to start/close, own capital, quick decisions, direct contact with consumers.
       - *Demerits:* Limited capital, limited managerial skill, unlimited liability on a single person, closes upon death/insolvency of owner.
    2. **Partnership Firms (*Indian Partnership Act, 1932*):**
       - Regulated by **Section 4 of the Indian Partnership Act, 1932**: two or more persons agree to share the profits of a business carried on by all or any of them acting for all.
       - **Maximum Number of Partners (as per textbook):**
         - Minimum: **$2\text{ partners}$**.
         - Maximum in **Banking / Financial business:** **$\mathbf{10\text{ partners}}$**.
         - Maximum in **General partnership business:** **$\mathbf{20\text{ partners}}$**.
       - **Types of Partners:**
         1. **Active (Working) Partners:** Contribute capital, share profits/losses, and **actively manage day-to-day business**.
         2. **Sleeping (Dormant) Partners:** Contribute capital and share profits/losses, but **do NOT take active part** in day-to-day management.
         3. **Nominal Partners:** Neither contribute capital nor participate in day-to-day business, and get no share of profits, **but lend their name and are liable for business losses**.
         4. **Minor Partners (below $18\text{ years}$):** Cannot form a new partnership, but can be admitted to the **benefits/profits of an existing firm** by mutual consent of all partners; **eligible only for profits and NOT liable for losses**.
         *(Also mentions Secret partners, Limited partners, and Partial partners).*
       - **Partnership Deed:** Stamped, written agreement signed by all partners specifying firm name, capital shares, profit/loss ratio, duties, admission/retirement rules, and goodwill calculation.
       - **Dissolution:** Easily dissolved by mutual consent or by any partner giving **$14\text{ days' notice}$**.
       - **Registration of Partnership Firms:** Optional (not compulsory), done with the **Registrar of Firms**. However, only a **Registered Firm** can file a lawsuit against a third party for recovering loans exceeding **Rs. $100$** or sue co-partners.
    3. **Hindu Undivided Family (HUF) Business Concerns:**
       - Found **only in India**, governed by **Hindu Law**.
       - Consists of **3 successive generations of male descendants** from a common male ancestor (father, sons, grandsons, great-grandsons) who acquire a right in ancestral property by birth.
       - **Karta:** The **eldest / senior-most male member** who heads and manages the HUF business. The **liability of the *Karta* is unlimited**, whereas the liability of all other members (coparceners) is **limited** to their share in the business.
- **Key Terms & Numbers:**
  - *Sole trading concern (single owner, oldest form)*, *Indian Partnership Act 1932 (Section 4)*, *Max partners: 10 (financial/banking) & 20 (general business)*, *Active, Sleeping, Nominal, and Minor partners*, *Partnership deed*, *14 days' notice for dissolution*, *Registrar of Firms (Rs. 100 suit threshold)*, *Hindu Undivided Family (HUF — found only in India, Hindu Law, 3 generations)*, *Karta (eldest male head, unlimited liability)*.
- **Mapping & Disambiguation Rules:**
  - **Map here** for Sole Trading Concerns, Partnership Firms (1932 Act, 10/20 partner limits, Active/Sleeping/Nominal/Minor partners, Partnership Deed, Registration), and Hindu Undivided Family (HUF / *Karta*).

### `ss-8-30-large-scale-business-organisations` — Chapter 30: Large Scale Business Organisations
- **Book Metadata:** Class 8 Social Science Part 2 | Chapter 30 | Book pp. 96–102 (PDF pp. 98–104) | **Sub-Discipline:** Business Studies (Commerce)
- **Canonical Topic Aliases:** Co-operative Societies (1904/1912 Acts, Robert Owen, Kanaginahala), Joint Stock Companies (1956 Act), Multinational Corporations (MNCs), Public Sector and Joint Sector Undertakings, Public Utilities, Stock Exchanges (BSE 1875) and SEBI
- **Core Concepts & Subtopics:**
  - **30.1 Co-operative Societies:**
    - Voluntary associations started by economically weaker sections on the basis of equality and mutual service to free members from exploitation by moneylenders and middlemen.
    - **Historical Milestones:**
      - **World's First Co-operative Society:** Started by **Robert Owen in $1844$ at Rochdale, England** (a Consumer Co-operative Society).
      - **India:** Co-operative Movement began with the **Co-operative Credit Societies Act, 1904**, followed by the **Indian Co-operative Societies Act, 1912** (which made registration compulsory).
      - **Karnataka's First Co-operative Society:** Started in **$1905$ at Kanaginahala** in **Gadag** taluk/district (and Bengaluru City Central Co-operative Society in $1905$). **Dharwad district** is called ***"The Cradle of the Co-operative Movement"*** in Karnataka.
    - **Features & Principles:** Open to all adults ($\ge 18\text{ years}$), no upper limit on membership, democratic management (**"One member, one vote"** regardless of shares held), limited liability, profit dividend distributed equally in proportion to share capital (**not less than $6.25\%$**), and motto ***"One for all and all for one"***. Acts as a *via media* between capitalism and socialism.
    - **7 Types:** Credit, Marketing, Producers, Consumers, Farmers, House-building, and Service co-operative societies.
  - **30.2 Joint Stock Companies (*Indian Companies Act, 1956*):**
    - Regulated by the **Joint Stock Companies Act, 1956**.
    - **Legal Definition:** An artificial business organisation created by law, having a **separate legal entity, perpetual succession, and a common seal**, with its capital divided into **transferable shares** (shareholders are the owners; companies also raise capital through **debentures**).
  - **30.3 Multinational Corporations (MNCs):**
    - **Origin & Examples:** Originated as early as **$1860$** (homeland was the **USA**; expanded rapidly after World War II, and in India after the **1991 Economic Liberalisation Policy**).
    - **Definition:** A large corporation that owns and manages business in **two or more countries** (operates in at least 6 countries) with centralized control from its head office in the **Mother (Home) Country** and operations in **Patron (Host) Nations**.
    - **Examples:**
      - *Foreign MNCs:* **Sony** (Japan), **Coca-Cola** (USA), **Samsung & Hyundai** (South Korea), **Hindustan Unilever & GlaxoSmithKline** (UK), **Philips** (Holland/Netherlands).
      - *Indian MNCs:* **Tata, Birla, Infosys, Wipro**.
  - **30.4 Government Undertakings, Joint Sector, & Public Utilities:**
    - **Government (Public Sector) Undertakings:** Owned, controlled, and managed by a Government Department under a Minister (Central, State, or Local Govt.) for public purpose — e.g., **Mining, Metallurgy, Shipbuilding, Aeronautics**.
    - **Joint Sector Undertakings:** Controlled jointly by the public (private investors) and the government — e.g., **Gujarat Fertilizers, Cochin Refineries**.
    - **Public Utilities:** Government undertakings established to supply essential goods and services to the public **without profit motive** — e.g., **cooking gas, water supply, electricity, railways, posts, telegraphs**.
  - **30.5 Stock Exchanges & SEBI:**
    - **Stock Exchanges (Share Markets):** Markets where shares and debentures of listed Joint Stock Companies are bought and sold.
    - **India's First Share Market:** Started in **$1875$ at Mumbai** (Bombay Stock Exchange / BSE).
    - **SEBI (Securities and Exchange Board of India):** The statutory regulatory board that controls and regulates all stock exchanges in India.
  - **30.6 Legacy NMMS Syllabus Note — Accounting in Business (Pre-2024 Ch. 33 Mapping):**
    - If an older NMMS PYQ asks about **Book-Keeping and Accounting** (formerly a standalone Class 8 Business Studies chapter):
      - **Luca Pacioli** (Italian friar, 1494) invented the **Double-Entry System** of book-keeping.
      - **Accounting Cycle:** $\text{Business Transactions} \to \text{Journal (Book of Original Entry)} \to \text{Ledger (Book of Final/Principal Entry)} \to \text{Trial Balance} \to \text{Final Accounts}$.
      - **Three Types of Accounts & Golden Rules:**
        1. **Personal Account:** *Debit the Receiver, Credit the Giver*.
        2. **Real (Asset) Account:** *Debit what comes in, Credit what goes out*.
        3. **Nominal (Income/Expense) Account:** *Debit all expenses and losses, Credit all incomes and gains*.
      - Map any such legacy PYQs to **`ss-8-30-large-scale-business-organisations`** (or `ss-8-15-meaning-importance-business-studies`).
- **Key Terms, Acts & Places:**
  - *Co-operative Societies (Robert Owen, 1844, Rochdale, England; Indian Acts of 1904 & 1912; Kanaginahala in Gadag [1905, first in Karnataka]; Dharwad ["Cradle of Co-operative Movement"]; "One for all and all for one"; $\ge 6.25\%$ dividend)*, *Joint Stock Companies Act 1956 (perpetual succession, common seal, transferable shares, debentures)*, *Multinational Corporations (MNCs — 1860, USA, Mother country vs. Patron nations, 1991 Liberalisation, Tata/Birla/Infosys/Wipro)*, *Government Undertakings*, *Joint Sector (Gujarat Fertilizers, Cochin Refineries)*, *Public Utilities (water, electricity, railways, cooking gas)*, *First Stock Exchange in India (Mumbai, 1875)*, *SEBI (Securities and Exchange Board of India)*.
- **Mapping & Disambiguation Rules:**
  - **Map here** for Co-operative Societies (Robert Owen 1844, Kanaginahala 1905, 1904/1912 Acts), Joint Stock Companies (1956 Act), MNCs, Public/Joint Sector & Public Utilities, Stock Exchanges (Mumbai 1875), SEBI, and legacy Accounting PYQs.
